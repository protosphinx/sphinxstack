## Brief

Every hobby has a wall of jargon that nobody explains: fishing,
skating, knitting, climbing, keyboards. You climbed that wall the
hard way. Write the glossary you needed at the start, every term
defined the way you wish someone had defined it for you, with the
context that dictionary definitions leave out: when you hear it, why
it matters, what beginners confuse it with.

## Personalize

- **The hobby.** One you are past the beginner stage in. You need to
  have been confused by these terms yourself, so you know which
  definitions are useless.
- **The term list.** Brain-dump every term you had to learn, then
  ask one newer person in the hobby which words confused them. Aim
  for 25 or more.
- **Your examples.** Each definition uses a moment from your own
  time in the hobby. That is what a generic glossary cannot have.

## Milestones

1. Ten terms with one-line definitions live at a URL, day one.
2. Full term list assembled and grouped into whatever categories
   the hobby splits into.
3. Every definition written properly: what it means, when you hear
   it, a personal example.
4. Alphabetical index and category navigation, cross-links between
   related terms.
5. A newcomer to the hobby reads it and flags what still confuses
   them. Rewrite those entries.
6. Phone styling and final README.

## Done means

- Live URL responding; navigable on a phone
- At least 25 terms, each with a personal example
- Newcomer review and the rewrites it caused recorded in the README
- Cross-links between related terms work
- At least 8 commits across at least 5 distinct days, authored by you
- SESSIONS.md has an entry per session, written by you

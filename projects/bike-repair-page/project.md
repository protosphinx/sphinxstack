## Brief

You already fix flats and adjust brakes for friends. The page writes
it down: what you can fix, what you charge, what you will not touch,
and where to find you on Saturdays. The will-not-touch list matters
as much as the fix list, because it is what keeps a neighbor's brakes
safe.

## Personalize

- **The fix list.** Repairs you have completed successfully more than
  once: flats, brake adjustment, chain replacement, whatever your
  track record covers.
- **The no list.** What goes to the bike shop instead. Hydraulic
  brakes, cracked frames, and anything you would be guessing at
  belong here. Check the list with the shop or an adult who wrenches.
- **Prices and parts.** A labor price per repair. Parts like tubes
  and cables are paid back at receipt price; you show the receipt.
- **When and where.** The driveway or park corner, and the Saturday
  hours you will keep.

## Milestones

1. Page live on day one: fix list, prices, hours, spot.
2. No list written and checked with the bike shop or a wrenching
   adult.
3. Page shared by mid-week with the friends whose bikes you already
   fix, asking them to spread it.
4. First paid repair done, with a before-and-after photo taken with
   the owner's okay.
5. Final README: repairs done, parts bought, what showed up that you
   had to refuse.

## Done means

- Live URL responding; readable on a phone
- Honest-claims check passed: everything on the fix list is a repair
  you have completed before
- No list on the page and respected; refused jobs pointed to the shop
- Brakes and anything structural test-ridden by you before the bike
  goes back
- First repair or inquiry logged in SESSIONS.md, which has an entry
  per session
- Owners pay you in person; parts receipts shown every time

## Brief

Your family's garage sale, online: every item photographed and
priced, reachable by a QR code on the yard sign, with items marked
sold as the morning goes. The catalog gives buyers a reason to show
up for a specific thing instead of driving past. Cash is handled at
the table by your family; the page is the menu.

## Personalize

- **The sale.** A sale your family is holding for real, on a date
  your parents set. If no sale is planned, propose one; the decluttering
  argument usually wins.
- **The items.** Each family member prices their own things. You
  photograph everything as it is, flaws in frame.
- **The sold flow.** How you mark an item sold from your phone while
  standing in the driveway. Practice it before sale day.
- **Bigger items.** If your parents also list furniture or
  electronics on a marketplace, the listing links to the catalog page
  for photos.

## Milestones

1. Catalog structure live on day one with the first ten items.
2. Every item photographed, priced by its owner, and listed by
   mid-week.
3. QR code on the yard sign tested from a stranger's distance; link
   added wherever the sale is advertised.
4. Sale day: items marked sold in real time from a phone.
5. Afterward: page switched to a sale-over state; totals counted with
   the family.
6. Final README: what sold fast, what nobody wanted, what you would
   price differently.

## Done means

- Live catalog with photos and prices for every item
- Honest-claims check passed: photos show items as they are, damage
  visible
- Sold status updated during the sale, from a phone
- All money handled by your family in cash at the sale; the page
  never takes payment
- If anything went on a public marketplace, the family knew the
  common scams before replying to buyers: overpayment refund
  requests, fake payment-app screenshots, courier-collection stories
- SESSIONS.md has an entry per session, written by you

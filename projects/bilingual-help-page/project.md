## Brief

You speak two languages; some of your neighbors' forms, letters, and
phone calls only come in one. A page, written in both languages, that
says what you will help with, what you will not, and when. The limits
section is the most important part, because a translation mistake in
the wrong document has consequences you cannot fix.

## Personalize

- **The languages and the tasks.** Reading a letter aloud, filling a
  school form together, sitting in on a customer-service call. Offer
  what you have already done for your own family.
- **The limits.** You are not a certified translator. Legal, medical,
  and immigration documents go to proper translation services, and
  the page says so with a pointer to where those services are found
  locally.
- **The neighbors.** The ones who already ask you or your family.
  This service spreads by word of mouth in one language.
- **Free or paid.** Many of these favors should stay favors. Decide
  with your parents which tasks, if any, carry a small price, and say
  it plainly.

## Milestones

1. Page live on day one, in both languages.
2. Limits paragraph checked with a parent and, if you can, with
   someone who knows what certified translation covers.
3. Page shared by mid-week with the neighbors who ask, and posted at
   the community spot they use (shop noticeboard, community center).
4. First help session done and logged: kind of document, how long it
   took.
5. Final README: the document types that came up and where the limits
   held.

## Done means

- Live URL, both languages, readable on a phone
- Honest-claims check passed: fluency claimed matches how you speak
  and read
- Limits on the page and respected: nothing high-stakes translated
  solo, referrals given instead
- First session or inquiry logged in SESSIONS.md, which has an entry
  per session
- Neighbors' documents stay private; nothing from a session appears
  on the page or anywhere else
- Any payment set in advance and handled in person

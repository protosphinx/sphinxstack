## Brief

Your study group has a chat where the useful things drown: the past
papers, the formula sheet link, what got covered last Tuesday. Build
the group's page: when and where you meet, what each session
covered, and the links everyone keeps re-asking for. The chat stays
for talking; the hub holds what the group needs to find twice.

## Personalize

- **The group and the subject.** Your actual study group, three or
  more people, working toward a specific exam or course. The exam
  date gives the hub its deadline and its purpose.
- **What keeps getting lost.** Scroll the group chat and list what
  people re-ask for. Those items seed the links page; do not stock
  it with resources nobody asked about.
- **The session log.** After each meeting, three lines: date, what
  got covered, what is next. Agree who writes it; it can be you
  every time, but the group should know that is the deal.

## Milestones

1. Pitch the group; agree what goes on the hub and whether the page
   is public or unlisted. Members' names go up only with their okay.
2. Hub live at a URL: meeting time, place, and the next session's
   topic.
3. The rescued links in, organized so the most re-asked-for item is
   one click from the top.
4. First session logged within a day of the meeting.
5. The group adopts it: link pinned in the chat, and members open
   the hub instead of asking. Watch for the first "it's on the hub"
   reply; that is the milestone.
6. Three sessions logged, hub kept current through them. Final
   README.

## Done means

- Live URL responding; readable on a phone
- The group agreed to the hub and its visibility; named members
  consented, recorded in the README
- Three or more sessions logged, each within a day of the meeting
- The link is pinned in the group chat and a member other than you
  has used the hub to find something, noted in the README
- At least 8 commits across at least 5 distinct days, authored by
  you
- SESSIONS.md has an entry per session, written by you

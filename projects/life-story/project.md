## Brief

Sit down with a grandparent, take notes, and build the page: where
they grew up, what they worked as, the moves, the stories they always
tell at dinner. You are the writer; they are the source and the
editor. They approve every word before it goes live, which is a
constraint most first projects never teach: publishing about a person
who gets a veto.

## Personalize

- **The grandparent.** Or a great-aunt, or the oldest relative who
  will talk to you. Ask them first, and explain a web page to them
  in whatever terms land.
- **The interview questions.** Write ten before you sit down, then
  abandon the list when a story gets going. Take notes or record
  with permission.
- **The artifacts.** Old photos, a wedding invitation, a work badge.
  Ask what they still have and scan what they lend you.

## Milestones

1. A page with their name and one paragraph live at a URL, day one.
2. First interview done; notes typed up the same day while you
   remember the tone.
3. The story drafted in sections: early life, work, family, the
   stories. Read it to them and mark every correction.
4. Scanned photos and artifacts placed where they belong in the
   story.
5. Second read-through; they approve the final text word by word.
6. Styling that suits them, shown to them on a phone or tablet they
   can hold. Final README.

## Done means

- Live URL responding; readable on the device they own
- Word-by-word approval from the subject, recorded in the README
- At least five scanned photos or artifacts in the story
- Two interview sessions logged with dates
- At least 8 commits across at least 5 distinct days, authored by you
- SESSIONS.md has an entry per session, written by you

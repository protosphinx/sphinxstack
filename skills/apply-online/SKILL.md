# apply-online

Get real applications submitted with someone, probably
their first. Online applications often remain in drafts; the bar here is
applications actually sent this sitting, each one honest, tailored,
and logged so they know what they are waiting on.

## Ground rules

- Nothing invented. No fabricated jobs, dates, availability,
  eligibility, references, or certifications — on the resume, in
  form fields, or in screening questions. If a question exposes a
  real gap, answer it truthfully; if they ask you to fudge it,
  refuse and point at the honest fix (build the missing thing —
  sphinxstack has skills for that).
- Their words. You draft, they edit until every sentence sounds like
  them; anything submitted under their name is theirs. Never answer
  "why do you want this job" with boilerplate they could not say out
  loud.
- They drive the browser on anything that submits. You read the
  form with them and prep every answer; they click send.

## Parse the posting

Read the full posting together. Pull out: the actual duties, the
hard requirements (age, permit, schedule, certifications), the
nice-to-haves, and the exact nouns the employer uses. Check the hard
requirements against their reality first — if one genuinely blocks
them, say so now and move to the next opening instead of wasting an
application.

## Tailor the materials

- Resume: start from their real one (build-resume if none exists).
  Reorder and reword so the most relevant true things come first,
  using the posting's own vocabulary where it is honest. Save as a
  new copy named for this employer.
- Short answers and cover letter, only if the form asks: three or
  four plain sentences — why this place, what true thing they bring,
  when they can work. Workshop until they own every line.
- Screening questions: rehearse the likely ones (availability,
  transport, why here) so the form holds no surprises.

## Submit and log

Go through the form field by field. You supply the prepared answers;
they type or paste, and they hit submit. Watch for the traps: resume
uploads that mangle formatting (check the parsed preview), required
fields hidden below the fold, accounts the ATS forces them to
create — have them make those with their own email and password.
After each submission, log it: employer, role, date, link, which
resume version, any login created. Use job-application-tracker if
they have it; a plain spreadsheet row otherwise.

## Done

- At least one application fully submitted, confirmation seen
- Every submitted word true and theirs
- Each application logged with a follow-up date

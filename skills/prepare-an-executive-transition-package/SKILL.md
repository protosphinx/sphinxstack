# prepare-an-executive-transition-package

Keep career positioning consistent with legal duties, public records, board authority, and operational handoff.

## When to use

- Use for chief executive, officer, partner, founder, public-company, regulated, or other high-visibility transitions.
- Use an ordinary resume workflow when no material disclosure, governance, reputation, stakeholder, confidentiality, or access-control risk exists.

## Preconditions

- Identify the executive, board or employer authority, counsel, communications, investor or regulator relations, HR, security, records, reference, and search advisers.
- Preserve governing agreements, public filings, approved biographies, role records, board minutes where authorized, performance evidence, litigation holds, investigations, and transition decisions.
- Confirm who may approve factual claims, external statements, references, portfolio evidence, timing, system access, and data retention.

## Procedure

1. Establish the **authority and disclosure boundary** for departure reason, dates, successor, performance, compensation, investigations, disputes, health, family, strategy, transactions, customer facts, and nonpublic information.
2. Build an **evidence and reputation inventory** of appointments, responsibilities, decisions, results, failures, public statements, filings, awards, sanctions, corrections, press, conflicts, board roles, and source records.
3. Reconcile title, entity, dates, transaction status, metrics, attribution, and outcomes across public filings, biographies, profiles, resumes, board records, and authorized references.
4. Classify facts as public, approved for release, confidential, privileged, disputed, under review, superseded, or prohibited.
5. Create **coordinated transition materials** including master career record, targeted executive resume, board bio, short biography, transition statement, reference brief, recruiter profile, interview boundary, and authorized Q&A.
6. State material complexity and setbacks accurately without speculating, concealing required facts, breaching agreements, or claiming sole ownership of organization results.
7. Align references and spokespeople on verified scope and process without scripting false endorsement.
8. Review required securities, regulatory, employment, litigation, privacy, records, conflict, and contractual disclosures with qualified owners.
9. Maintain a post-departure restriction register for material nonpublic information, trading limits, information barriers, insider status, conflicts, cooperation, confidentiality, and the authority and date that release each restriction.
10. Inventory company devices, accounts, data, board materials, contact exports, credentials, social profiles, domains, assistants, and third-party access.
11. Plan **release and revocation control** for announcement timing, document versions, recipient lists, profile changes, account transfer, access removal, data return, retention, and correction.
12. Rehearse leak, conflicting-reference, outdated-bio, disputed-claim, premature-announcement, and account-access scenarios.
13. Release only approved materials, monitor public and recipient accuracy, correct errors through authorized channels, and preserve the final package.

## Failure plan

- If board, executive, counsel, and public records disagree, freeze the disputed claim and record the authorized wording or unresolved status.
- If an investigation, dispute, or disclosure duty may apply, do not convert silence into a false positive account; obtain qualified review.
- If premature disclosure occurs, preserve it, limit further spread, assess legal and stakeholder duties, and issue only authorized correction.
- If the executive retains company data or access, prioritize security, records, litigation-hold, and privacy obligations over portfolio convenience.
- Never fabricate references, backdate titles, erase public corrections, trade on nonpublic information, or retaliate against truthful sources.

## Worked example

A chief executive is leaving after a merger while a nonpublic restructuring, active employment dispute, public-company disclosure timetable, confidential board material, stale biographies, and future board search overlap. The team inventories public and restricted facts, reconciles titles and transaction metrics, pauses disputed claims, and creates resume, board bio, statement, reference brief, and interview boundaries from one approved record. Security rehearses access removal and data return. Counsel and the board authorize the release sequence, while monitoring catches and corrects an old profile without disclosing the restructuring early.

## Done

- An executive transition register records authority and disclosure boundary, owners, dates, evidence and reputation inventory, public records, conflicts, materials, access, and approvals
- A claim and disclosure review verifies titles, metrics, attribution, disputes, confidentiality, legal duties, references, coordinated transition materials, and authorized wording
- A release and access closeout report proves release and revocation control, versions, recipients, announcements, account removal, data return, corrections, monitoring, and retention

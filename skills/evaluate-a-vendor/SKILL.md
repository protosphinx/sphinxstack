# evaluate-a-vendor

Make the decision reproducible. Keep vendor claims, verified facts, assumptions, and stakeholder preferences separate.

## When to use

- Use for software, services, equipment, logistics, professional services, and other operational suppliers.
- Add security, legal, finance, privacy, accessibility, or domain review when their risks are material.

## Procedure

1. Define the business outcome, users, constraints, decision owner, budget boundary, and implementation deadline.
2. Turn needs into must-have gates, weighted criteria, and explicit non-goals before reviewing vendors.
3. Build a common evidence request covering functionality, service, implementation, support, security, data, exit, references, and pricing.
4. Record each response as verified, vendor-asserted, assumed, not applicable, or unknown.
5. Test critical workflows with representative data and acceptance criteria.
6. Model total cost including implementation, migration, usage, support, training, integration, growth, renewal, and exit.
7. Assess concentration, continuity, lock-in, subcontractors, incident history, capacity, and dependency risk.
8. Check references with comparable scale and use cases.
9. Score consistently, run sensitivity analysis on weights and uncertain costs, and document exceptions.
10. Recommend select, pilot, negotiate, defer, or reject with conditions and an evidence trail.

## Failure plan

- Do not convert a polished demo or roadmap promise into verified capability.
- If a must-have fails, do not hide it inside a weighted total.
- Keep confidential bids access-controlled and follow procurement conflict rules.
- If no vendor passes, report the gap instead of selecting the least-bad option silently.

## Done

- Requirements, evidence status, tests, cost, risk, references, and scoring are auditable
- The outcome remains understandable under reasonable weight changes
- Unknowns, exceptions, owners, and exit conditions are explicit

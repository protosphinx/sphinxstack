# start-a-youtube-channel

Start a YouTube channel with someone. Explain the actual production time and
the uncertainty of audience growth without inventing failure rates. Your job is to aim the channel
at what they genuinely know and leave the session with the channel
live and video one planned shot by shot.

## Aim at what they know

The channel subject is something they could talk about for ten
minutes with no script today. Ask what that is: the game they are
actually good at, the sport they play, the thing they repair or
build or cook, the local knowledge nobody else films. Do not let
them pick a niche because it is popular — a commentary channel about
games they do not play is unlikely to remain useful or sustainable.

Test the pick the same way as any recurring format: can they list
five videos right now? Then ask the effort question directly — one
video is realistically several hours of recording and editing. How
many hours a month do they have? Set the cadence from that answer,
and monthly is a fine answer.

## Set up the channel

With them making each call:

- Read YouTube's current account, age, privacy, and publication requirements.
  A minor involves a parent or guardian and does not reveal school, home,
  routine, or precise location in the channel identity or footage.

- Channel name: plain and searchable beats clever. Their own name
  works if the channel is about their skill.
- One-sentence channel description saying who it is for.
- Banner and avatar: simple and legible. A phone photo of their
  actual setup or work beats generated art.
- Recording reality check: what do they own? A phone and free
  editing software (CapCut, DaVinci Resolve) are enough. Do not let
  gear shopping become the project.

## Plan video one, for real

Video one gets planned to the point where recording is the only
step left:

1. They pick the topic from their five-video list — the one they
   know best, not the one they think will get views.
2. Together, write the shot list: hook in the first ten seconds,
   the 3–5 beats of the middle, the ending. Their words throughout;
   you structure, they supply everything said on camera.
3. Set a recording date within the next seven days and put it in
   their calendar.
4. Commit the plan: a repo with the shot list, future video ideas,
   and the cadence promise. It versions their scripts from day one
   and gives the channel a paper trail a resume can point at.

If they want a channel site or a place to embed videos later,
https://sphinxstack.com/skills/build-website/ picks up from here.
More ideas worth filming live at https://sphinxstack.com/ideas/.

## Done

- Channel identity and privacy settings reviewed against current platform rules
- Five grounded video ideas and one shot-level plan saved
- Recording date, realistic time budget, and guardian involvement where needed
  are documented; nothing private is exposed

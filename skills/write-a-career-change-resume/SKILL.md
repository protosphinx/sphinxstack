# write-a-career-change-resume

Bridge the evidence gap instead of relabeling old work.

## When to use

- Use for industry, function, occupation, or specialization changes.
- Do not rename prior jobs, manufacture experience, or present training projects as paid production work.

## Procedure

1. Define target role outcomes, required capabilities, entry assumptions, and realistic level.
2. Inventory prior achievements by problem, stakeholder, decision, method, constraint, and transferable result.
3. Separate directly relevant evidence, transferable evidence, newly learned capability, and remaining gaps.
4. Lead with a truthful summary and selected evidence rather than a long objective.
5. Translate jargon into target-language outcomes while preserving original scope and title.
6. Add relevant projects, courses, credentials, volunteer work, or portfolio evidence with accurate status.
7. Address the transition briefly in the resume document and show why recent actions support readiness.
8. Remove unrelated detail only when the resulting chronology remains clear.
9. Test every target criterion and prepare examples for unresolved gaps.

## Done

- A career-change resume maps target criteria to direct, transferable, and newly developed evidence
- Titles, dates, project status, credentials, gaps, and translated claims are verified without disguising the transition

# audit-an-academic-cv-for-public-appointment

Repeated prestige claims are not independent verification.

## When to use

- Use before public academic appointments, leadership elections, endowed roles, awards, expert panels, board seats, or regulated biographies.
- Obtain qualified appointment, governance, research-integrity, legal, privacy, publication, grants, credential, and communications review.

## Preconditions

- Establish independent verification authority, conflicts and recusal, materiality, consent, privacy limits, deadlines, fair response, appeal, and stop authority.
- Preserve submitted CVs, biographies, applications, questionnaires, prior disclosures, profiles, edits, sources, and approvals.
- Define which claims will be public and the applicable appointment or disclosure rules.

## Procedure

Complete **identity and claim inventory**, **independent scholarly verification**, **conflict and disclosure review**, and **approved release and correction**.

1. Build an **academic claim verification register** for identity, names, employment, education, licenses, honors, publications, authorship, grants, patents, data, software, teaching, supervision, service, impact, boards, interests, sanctions, findings, and conflicts.
2. Compare CV, biography, identifiers, institutional profiles, indexes, repositories, funder records, publisher records, course records, questionnaires, and prior disclosures without treating repetition as corroboration.
3. Resolve names, identifiers, affiliations, disciplines, dates, and similarly named people using authorized privacy-preserving evidence.
4. Verify employment, education, credentials, honors, memberships, leadership, board, and service status with authoritative custodians where available.
5. Verify every material output’s existence, version, date, venue, peer-review status, correction, expression of concern, retraction, authorship, and contribution.
6. Distinguish submitted, under review, accepted, in press, published, corrected, retracted, working, invited, and self-published material.
7. Verify grants, amount basis, period, funder, recipient institution, principal or co-investigator role, personal versus consortium value, status, and overlap.
8. Verify courses, teaching responsibility, learners, supervision status, completion, examination, and outcomes without exposing student information.
9. Test quantitative claims about citations, rankings, acceptance, funding, learners, patents, adoption, policy influence, clinical effect, or economic impact against dated methods and scope.
10. Before using absence as adverse evidence, record search identities, sources, coverage, retention limits, query method, custodian completeness, alternatives, and independent negative-inference approval.
11. Identify research-integrity findings, active processes, sanctions, corrections, conflicts, related parties, funder or publisher relationships, and appointment-reviewer conflicts under applicable rules.
12. Give the candidate specific discrepancies, relevant source descriptions, privacy-safe evidence, reasonable response, and a recorded disposition.
13. Correct, omit, qualify, or escalate claims, then reconcile one approved claim set across CV, biography, announcement, website, board, media, and filing surfaces.
14. Define an evidence cutoff and immediately before release recheck every materially mutable authoritative source, recording source, available source version, checked time, status, volatility, and reviewer.
15. Give dynamic metrics an explicit as-of date and method; any material change invalidates approval, reopens fair response, and pauses affected derivatives.
16. Bind approvals to exact versions and prepare visible derivative correction, notification, withdrawal, and appointment reconsideration routes for new evidence.

## Failure plan

- If a material claim cannot be verified before deadline, omit or qualify it rather than imply confirmation.
- If identity, authorship, contribution, or record coverage is uncertain, do not draw an adverse or favorable conclusion.
- If sponsors or reviewers are conflicted, transfer the decision to independent governance.
- If a public claim proves wrong, preserve the original and correct every derivative under the authorized process.

## Worked example

A university is appointing a president when the candidate’s CV, biographies, indexes, and forms conflict about a completed degree, professorial dates, corresponding authorship, retracted work, grant totals, principal-investigator roles, doctoral completions, teaching, patents, board service, consulting, awards, and citation counts. Independent reviewers resolve identity, verify claim status and contribution, handle conflicts and fair response, approve one disclosure set, and prepare correction.

## Done

- An academic claim verification register records every public claim, identity, source, status, output version, contribution, discrepancy, conflict, search coverage, response, privacy treatment, and approval
- A scholarly source and discrepancy report proves employment, credentials, outputs, corrections, authorship, grants, teaching, supervision, service, impact, conflicts, integrity records, and unresolved limits
- An approved public-appointment and correction package includes a publication-time freshness certificate and demonstrates exact-version consistency across CV, biography, announcement, website, board, media, and filing surfaces with independent approval and correction readiness

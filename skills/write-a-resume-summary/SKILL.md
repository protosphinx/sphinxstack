# write-a-resume-summary

Summarize demonstrated value, not personality adjectives.

## When to use

- Use for experienced candidates, career changers, or roles where a short positioning statement helps.
- Do not invent titles, years, industries, tools, outcomes, or leadership scope.

## Procedure

1. Gather target role, job language, verified work history, strengths, scope, outcomes, and constraints.
2. Identify two or three recurring capabilities supported by specific resume evidence.
3. Choose the most relevant role identity without overstating current title or license.
4. Draft two or three sentences covering profession, scope, strengths, and value.
5. Use target terminology only where the candidate's evidence supports it.
6. Remove clichés, first-person filler, objectives, unsupported superlatives, and duplicated bullets.
7. Verify every phrase against the resume and read it with the first experience entry.

## Done

- A resume summary draft records target role, verified identity, scope, strengths, outcomes, and supporting resume references
- Claim, title, years, keyword, duplication, length, evidence, and candidate-approval checks verify truthful positioning

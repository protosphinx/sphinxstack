# write-a-skills-based-resume

Reorganize truthful history without hiding it.

## When to use

- Use for career changes, varied contract work, returners, students, or mixed paid and unpaid experience.
- Do not conceal dates, employers, role type, gaps, or the source of a claimed skill.

## Preconditions

- Gather the target role, complete chronology, verified projects, education, service, and evidence.

## Procedure

1. Extract the target capabilities, outcomes, contexts, and required qualifications.
2. Inventory examples with organization, role, date, action, tool, scale, outcome, and evidence.
3. Group examples under three to five role-relevant skill headings.
4. Write bullets that preserve context and distinguish paid, contract, volunteer, academic, care, and personal work.
5. Add a compact reverse-chronology history with truthful dates and role types.
6. Include only verified education, credentials, links, and proficiency.
7. Check keywords, readability, accessibility, chronology, duplication, and evidence with the candidate.

## Failure plan

- If evidence is thin, keep the claim modest or build current evidence rather than padding.
- If the format could look evasive, strengthen the chronology and context.

## Worked example

A career changer groups customer research, process improvement, and facilitation examples while retaining a clear ten-year employment and care timeline.

## Done

- A skills-based resume document records verified capability groups, evidence-backed bullets, complete chronology, education, and links
- Target-fit, source, role-type, date, gap, duplication, accessibility, and candidate-approval checks verify truthfulness

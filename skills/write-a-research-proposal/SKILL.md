# write-a-research-proposal

Make the proposed evidence capable of answering the stated question.

## When to use

- Use for theses, dissertations, grants, capstones, independent studies, or internal research.
- Do not claim approvals, access, partnerships, data, or results that have not been confirmed.

## Procedure

1. Confirm audience, format, evaluation criteria, word limit, deadline, and required attachments.
2. Define the problem, context, research gap, question, objectives, and practical or scholarly significance.
3. Synthesize the most relevant evidence and show where uncertainty remains without manufacturing novelty.
4. State conceptual framework, definitions, scope, and expected contribution.
5. Justify design, sampling, measures, procedure, analysis, quality controls, and alternatives.
6. Address ethics, consent, safety, privacy, data governance, inclusion, and required permissions.
7. Build a staged schedule with dependencies, review points, recruitment or access lead time, and contingency.
8. Estimate people, equipment, software, travel, participant, repository, and dissemination resources.
9. Record risks, feasibility evidence, limitations, fallback scope, and stopping conditions.
10. Cross-check question, method, budget, schedule, and claimed outputs for consistency.

## Worked example

A proposal promises national conclusions from interviews at one accessible site. Review narrows the question to that setting, explains purposeful sampling, adds a transferability boundary, and provides a fallback if recruitment is slow. The contribution becomes credible rather than inflated.

## Done

- A proposal document contains a sourced gap, bounded question, justified method, governance, schedule, resources, risks, and contribution
- A consistency check verifies that scope, claims, method, access, approvals, budget, and timeline agree

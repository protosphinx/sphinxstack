# linkedin-profile

Help someone set up or repair their LinkedIn profile.
The profile is the resume a recruiter finds without asking, so the
bar is the same: every entry true, consistent with the resume, and
short enough that the real material is visible. You draft field by
field with them; they paste each one in and confirm it saved.

## Ground rules

- The resume is the source of truth. If they have none, run
  build-resume first — writing a profile from scratch invents.
- Nothing padded. No inflated titles ("Founder" of a two-person
  study group), no skill endorsement farming, no buzzword about
  section. A short profile that checks out beats a long one that
  does not.
- You cannot log in for them and must not handle their password.
  They drive the browser; you draft text and check results by
  asking them to read back what the section shows.
- Work section by section at doing-speed — the whole profile is one
  sitting.

## Section pass

1. Headline: what they are and what they are looking for, in plain
   words. "CS student at Lincoln High building web apps — seeking
   summer internship" beats any pipe-separated slogan.
2. About: three or four sentences, first person, in their words.
   Ask what they would tell a stranger at the job itself; that
   answer, tightened, is the about section. They approve every
   sentence.
3. Experience: one entry per resume job or project, same dates,
   same facts, same links. LinkedIn allows projects under
   Experience or Projects — put anything with a live link where a
   recruiter will actually see it. Where entries disagree with the
   resume, the resume wins or gets corrected; the two must match
   because interviewers compare them.
4. Education, skills, photo: school and dates as on the resume;
   skills limited to what the profile itself evidences; a plain,
   recent photo of their face if they have one, no photo over a
   bad one.
5. Housekeeping: custom URL (their name, no digits if free),
   profile set to visible, resume PDF not uploaded unless they
   want it public.

## Done

- Headline, about, experience, and education filled in and saved —
  they confirmed each on screen
- Profile and resume tell the same story, date for date
- Custom URL claimed and added to the resume header
- Nothing on the profile they could not say to an interviewer

If the experience section came up thin, that is a resume problem
inventing a professional persona — point at build-resume's "thin resume"
path and the skills at https://sphinxstack.com/skills/.

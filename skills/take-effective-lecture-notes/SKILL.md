# take-effective-lecture-notes

Record meaning and uncertainty, not a transcript of every sentence.

## When to use

- Use before, during, and after a lecture or demonstration.
- Do not record people or restricted material without permission.

## Procedure

1. Record course, topic, date, lecturer, learning objectives, source slides, and any recording or sharing rules.
2. Preview headings and prior material, then write two or three questions the lecture should answer.
3. During the lecture, capture claims, definitions, mechanisms, examples, formulas, evidence, and lecturer emphasis in distinct blocks.
4. Mark direct quotations, your own interpretation, unresolved confusion, and material copied from slides differently.
5. Note references, page numbers, timestamps, diagrams, assumptions, units, and worked-example steps needed to reconstruct the reasoning.
6. Leave space rather than guessing when content is missed.
7. Within one day, resolve abbreviations, summarize the central argument from memory, and link each question to an answer or follow-up.
8. Create retrieval prompts and one application example from the notes.
9. Compare with approved sources or peers and log corrections without erasing the original record.

## Worked example

In an economics lecture, a student records the assumptions behind a supply model, the lecturer's example, and a question about an apparent exception. After class, the student checks the assigned reading, corrects a copied sign error, links the correction, and creates three prompts that require explaining the model rather than recognizing highlighted text.

## Done

- A lecture-note document records source context, claims, examples, questions, diagrams, and clearly marked corrections
- A verified summary and retrieval set can be answered without reopening the lecture materials

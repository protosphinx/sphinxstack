# apply-for-internships

Run an internship hunt with someone whose resume is
mostly potential. Internships differ from jobs in two ways that
shape everything: deadlines cluster months ahead and pass silently,
and no-experience applicants are the expected case — the application
just has to show real interest and real output of any kind. The bar:
a deadline-ordered target list and the first applications submitted.

## Ground rules

- Nothing invented — no fabricated coursework, projects, dates, or
  eligibility, and no inflated titles for informal work. Call the
  informal work what it was; that reads fine at this level. If a
  program requires something they lack, it goes on the list as
  blocked-until, never fudged.
- Check the fine print early: age minimums, enrollment requirements,
  paid or unpaid, location and housing, application windows. A
  perfect program they cannot attend is a wasted slot.
- Essays and answers are theirs. Workshopped hard, ghostwritten
  never — programs interview people about what they wrote.

## Find where they hide

Internships mostly do not sit on general job boards. Sweep, with
them, the places they do sit: company career pages under "early
careers" / "students" (check the ten organizations they would
actually want); their school's counselor or career office and its
email lists; programs run for their age group — municipal and
library youth-employment programs, museum and hospital volunteer-to-
intern tracks, national STEM or arts programs in their country;
local small businesses that would say yes to a well-made direct ask
(that path is cold-outreach-for-work). For each find, record the
window and deadline first, because a missed deadline ends the application
regardless of its quality.

## The no-experience application

Order the list by deadline, pick the top few, then build materials
that answer the only real question — "will this person show up and
learn fast?":

- Resume via build-resume: projects, coursework, clubs, volunteer
  work, informal jobs. One page, all true.
- Evidence beats claims. Anything they have made — code, writing,
  designs, a club they ran — gets linked. If there is nothing to
  link, say so honestly and offer to fix it for real: a one-week
  project from sphinxstack's ideas, then apply with it.
- The short essay or "why us": specific to the program (name a team,
  program detail, or product — proof they looked), honest about
  being early, concrete about what they want to learn.

## Submit and track

Applications go in well before deadline, them at the keyboard for
every submit, each one logged — program, deadline, date sent,
materials used, decision date — in their tracker
(job-application-tracker builds one). Recommenders, if required, get
asked now with polite lead time: who, by when, with the resume
attached so writing the letter is easy.

## Done

- A deadline-ordered list of real programs they are eligible for
- First applications submitted, true and in their own words
- Everything tracked; interview-prep on the calendar for replies

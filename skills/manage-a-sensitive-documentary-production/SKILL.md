# manage-a-sensitive-documentary-production

Editorial value does not erase the production's duty to people.

## When to use

- Use for investigations, whistleblowers, minors, trauma, health, conflict, abuse, crime, displacement, stigmatized identity, confidential sources, or hostile environments.
- Obtain qualified safeguarding, security, legal, clinical, and local guidance for the actual risk.

## Preconditions

- Establish independent editorial, participant-safety, source, safeguarding, security, privacy, legal, fact, field, archive, mental-health, and release authority.
- Define story purpose, public interest, populations, locations, adversaries, jurisdictions, funder and distributor influence, emergency contacts, and stop authority.
- Keep participant support and consent roles sufficiently independent from pressure to obtain footage.

## Procedure

1. Create a **participant and source protection register** for each person: role, capacity, vulnerability, age, guardian or advocate where appropriate, risks, promises, identity options, contact, consent state, withdrawal boundary, support, and review dates.
2. Conduct threat and harm modeling across physical, digital, legal, immigration, employment, family, community, reputational, financial, health, and retraumatization risks.
3. Use ongoing informed consent in understandable language, separating filming, identity, sensitive topics, archive, promotion, territory, platform, and future use where meaningful.
4. Never promise absolute anonymity, editorial control, withdrawal after irreversible distribution, or protection the production cannot provide.
5. Establish **source and evidence governance**: provenance, corroboration, custody, access, compartmentation, pseudonyms, authenticity, contradictions, publication threshold, and correction route.
6. Minimize location, identity, contact, device, travel, and communication exposure; use secure channels and separate identity keys from working media.
7. Build **production security and duty of care** for crew vetting, travel, check-ins, safe locations, equipment, transport, emergency extraction, insurance, legal support, trauma practice, working hours, and post-assignment care.
8. Record only the data and footage needed. Control cameras, metadata, cloud sync, backups, transcripts, translations, review links, exports, and deleted-media recovery.
9. Maintain editorial independence from sources, funders, fixers, authorities, distributors, and participants while honoring documented promises and factual correction rights.
10. Review each scene for source support, chronology, translation, representation, identity, location, foreseeable retaliation, stereotyping, minors, trauma, rights, and accessibility.
11. Conduct an **editorial and release harm review** comparing public value, evidence, necessity, alternatives, risk reduction, affected-person input, distribution, promotion, archive, and residual harm. Require trailers, thumbnails, press stills, subtitle excerpts, festival descriptions, behind-the-scenes material, and social targeting to pass the same identity, geolocation, translation, consent, and retaliation controls as the film.
12. Prepare release, legal threat, doxxing, participant crisis, source exposure, takedown, correction, platform, and crew-safety response plans.
13. Sequence publication and participant notice without giving dangerous actors advance access. Monitor harm and support commitments after release.
14. Retain or delete sensitive sources according to consent, promises, evidence, legal hold, security, archive, and documented authority.

## Failure plan

- If a participant faces imminent credible harm, pause filming or release and activate the qualified safety plan.
- If consent capacity, coercion, or identity protection is uncertain, do not publish identifiable material until resolved.
- If source provenance cannot support a material claim, remove, qualify, or continue investigation.
- If a security breach occurs, contain access, protect people first, preserve evidence, and reassess every promise and release decision.

## Worked example

A cross-border documentary follows whistleblowers, young participants, trauma survivors, and a confidential source while a powerful subject threatens legal and physical retaliation. Crews use fixers and remote editors, footage contains geolocation and identity clues, translations are contested, and a distributor wants aggressive promotion. The production records ongoing consent and promises, separates identity from working media, corroborates evidence, operates secure field and review workflows, conducts scene-level and release harm reviews, sequences notices safely, and maintains post-release support plus incident response.

## Done

- A participant and source protection register records consent, capacity, identity, promises, risks, contact, support, withdrawal boundaries, evidence status, and review authority
- A secure production and evidence log verifies threat controls, custody, access, field safety, data minimization, provenance, corroboration, translations, crew care, and incidents
- An editorial, release, and follow-through report proves public-interest reasoning, scene evidence, harm alternatives, rights, accessibility, participant notices, response plans, monitoring, support, retention, and residual risk

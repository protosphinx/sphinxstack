# create-a-support-escalation-policy

Create a policy that gets risk to the right authority quickly without making escalation a substitute for normal
ownership. Cover customer, technical, security, safety, financial, legal, and operational triggers as relevant.

## Inputs

- Gather support scope, severity model, service levels, team ownership, on-call paths, contracts, and incident procedures.
- Review historical missed, unnecessary, delayed, bounced, and successful escalations.
- Identify decision rights, hours, regions, vendors, communication channels, and restricted-data rules.

## Procedure

1. Define escalation and distinguish functional help, management attention, incident response, and complaint review.
2. Write observable triggers for impact, urgency, safety, security, privacy, money, legal duty, repeated failure, and customer commitment.
3. Map each trigger to receiving role, authority, channel, response target, and fallback.
4. Specify the minimum case brief, evidence, containment, and customer update required before transfer.
5. Define acceptance, retained ownership, warm handoff, rejection, and no-response behavior.
6. Set communication cadence and who may approve refunds, workarounds, disclosures, or exceptions.
7. Add after-hours, multi-region, vendor, executive, and conflict-of-interest paths where needed.
8. Protect restricted data and keep customer-facing status separate from private investigation notes.
9. Test the policy with historical and simulated cases, including ambiguous and cascading failures.
10. Assign policy ownership, training, audit sampling, and review triggers.

## Boundaries

Do not publish private contact details or route sensitive data through unapproved channels. Never require
customer anger or commercial value as the only escalation trigger. Preserve the current owner's responsibility
until the receiving owner accepts it.

## Done

- Every trigger maps to an available role, authority, channel, response target, and fallback
- Handoff evidence, acceptance, communication, and retained ownership are explicit
- Historical and simulated cases have been tested against the policy
- The published policy has an owner, review date, access rules, and verified contact paths

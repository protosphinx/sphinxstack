# respond-to-a-job-rejection

Close the process before extracting lessons from incomplete evidence.

## When to use

- Use after application, interview, assessment, reference, or final-stage rejection.
- No reply is required when the message is automated, unsafe, or provides no useful contact path.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the role, employer, stage, sender, decision, and whether any action, reimbursement, data, or returned material remains.
2. Allow time before responding if the result is emotionally charged.
3. Send a short acknowledgment when appropriate, thanking the person for specific time or context.
4. Ask once for bounded job-related feedback if the relationship and process support it; make refusal easy.
5. Do not argue, demand protected information, threaten public action, or treat generic feedback as a complete explanation.
6. Update the tracker with decision, stage, timeline, known evidence, feedback, and future-contact preference.
7. Review controllable factors across targeting, application, interview, evidence, logistics, and process fit.
8. Change a search practice only when the evidence is credible or a repeated pattern exists.
9. Close or delete employer accounts and personal data where appropriate and permitted.

## Failure plan

- If feedback is discriminatory, retaliatory, fraudulent, or otherwise concerning, preserve the exact evidence and seek qualified support before responding.
- If no reason is provided, record uncertainty instead of inventing a cause.

## Done

- A professional response or recorded no-response decision closes the communication and outstanding logistics
- A rejection review distinguishes facts, feedback, uncertainty, patterns, next actions, and any evidence requiring escalation

# write-a-resume-after-a-career-break

A break needs honest chronology, not apology or invented continuity.

## When to use

- Use after care, health, study, relocation, redundancy, service, travel, detention, or another period away.
- The candidate controls whether and how private reasons are disclosed.

## Preconditions

- Gather the target role, full chronology, verified prior work, break-period activities the candidate chooses to use, current skills, and evidence.

## Procedure

1. Extract target requirements and select verified prior achievements that still demonstrate them.
2. Record dates accurately and choose a neutral or specific break label with candidate consent.
3. Include break-period study, care, projects, service, or freelance work only when true and useful.
4. Build current evidence through recent coursework, tools, projects, references, or practice where needed.
5. Write a concise summary focused on fit and readiness rather than defensiveness.
6. Use reverse chronology or a transparent hybrid format without hiding dates.
7. Check claims, currency, privacy, keywords, accessibility, references, and candidate comfort.

## Failure plan

- If a skill is stale, qualify it and plan refresh evidence.
- If an application demands sensitive information unlawfully or irrelevantly, seek qualified support rather than overshare.

## Worked example

A caregiver returning to operations lists a consented “Family caregiving” period, leads with prior process improvements, and adds a recent inventory project as current evidence.

## Done

- A return-to-work resume document records complete chronology, consented break wording, relevant achievements, current evidence, and target fit
- Date, privacy, skill-currency, source, role-type, accessibility, reference, and candidate-approval checks verify truthfulness

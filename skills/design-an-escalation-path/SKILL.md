# design-an-escalation-path

Escalation transfers attention and authority, not ownership of the facts.

## When to use

- Use for service, project, safety, security, customer, vendor, legal, financial, or people issues.
- Do not require people to escalate through a person implicated in misconduct, retaliation, or conflict.

## Procedure

1. Define issue categories, severity, harm, time sensitivity, materiality, and mandatory escalation triggers.
2. Map first response, specialist, management, executive, independent, emergency, and external paths.
3. Assign role-based primary and backup contacts, authority, availability, acknowledgment, and action service levels.
4. Specify the minimum evidence, safe communication, privacy, and need-to-know rules.
5. Provide alternate and protected paths for conflict, absence, accessibility, language, safeguarding, and retaliation concerns.
6. Define response actions, status cadence, handoff, decision record, de-escalation, and closure criteria.
7. Rehearse urgent, out-of-hours, conflicted, failed-contact, and cross-jurisdiction cases.
8. Review missed triggers, delays, false escalations, user harm, and outdated contacts.

## Done

- An escalation matrix and test report record triggers, levels, contacts, backups, authority, service levels, channels, alternatives, and closure
- Trigger, contact, conflict, accessibility, out-of-hours, response-time, handoff, rehearsal, and update checks verify the path

# evaluate-a-merchant-services-contract

Model the contract against representative transaction and failure scenarios. This workflow is not legal advice.

## When to use

- Use for requests for proposal, negotiation, renewal, or processor consolidation.
- Obtain qualified payments, security, privacy, tax, finance, and legal review.

## Procedure

1. Build a fact base for volume, ticket size, cards, wallets, channels, countries, currencies, fraud, refunds, chargebacks, subscriptions, and settlement timing.
2. Map needed authorization, tokenization, routing, authentication, recurring, refund, dispute, reporting, reconciliation, payout, and support capabilities.
3. Normalize interchange, assessment, processor, gateway, cross-border, foreign-exchange, authentication, token, PCI, chargeback, refund, minimum, and ancillary fees.
4. Model base, growth, adverse mix, high-dispute, multi-currency, outage, and early-termination scenarios.
5. Review reserves, holds, netting, settlement, payout accounts, setoff, liability, indemnity, fraud, chargeback, and insolvency terms.
6. Assess security, PCI responsibilities, data ownership, permitted use, subprocessors, residency, breach, deletion, portability, and audit rights.
7. Test uptime, authorization latency, incident response, support, reporting, maintenance, remedies, change control, and service-credit terms.
8. Review term, renewal, price changes, exclusivity, volume commitments, assignment, termination, migration assistance, token portability, and survival.
9. Record deviations, dependencies, negotiation positions, owners, approvals, and residual risks.
10. Reconcile the final executed version to the economic model and implementation obligations.

## Done

- A total-cost model and contract issue register record scenarios, fees, reserves, capabilities, risks, negotiation positions, and approvals
- Rate-card, transaction, settlement, security, service-level, version, and exit checks verify that the executed terms match the decision

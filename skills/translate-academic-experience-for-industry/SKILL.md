# translate-academic-experience-for-industry

Translate context and contribution without inflating authority or commercial impact.

## When to use

- Use for resumes, profiles, applications, portfolios, or interviews during an academic-to-industry move.
- Do not rename a thesis as a product, students as direct reports, a grant as revenue, or collaboration as management.

## Preconditions

- Gather target roles, complete academic history, verified projects, outputs, tools, stakeholders, constraints, and feedback.

## Procedure

1. Extract target problems, capabilities, decisions, collaboration, domain, tools, and results.
2. Inventory academic examples with context, role, authority, action, scale, result, and evidence.
3. Map research to problem framing and analysis, teaching to facilitation, and service to operations only where evidence supports it.
4. Replace unexplained academic terms while retaining technically meaningful language.
5. Write bullets with truthful ownership and distinguish individual, team, supervised, and institutional outcomes.
6. Add concrete artifacts or links that a nonacademic reviewer can assess.
7. Test comprehension with a target-domain reader and revise without erasing specialist depth.

## Failure plan

- If no direct target evidence exists, identify a small real project rather than manufacturing equivalence.
- If a translation changes the factual claim, restore the original scope.

## Worked example

A doctoral researcher describes designing a reproducible analysis pipeline and coordinating external data access, but does not call the work product ownership.

## Done

- A translation report and revised resume record target capabilities, source experiences, bounded mappings, evidence, artifacts, and wording
- Authority, ownership, outcome, jargon, chronology, link, reader-comprehension, and candidate-approval checks verify accuracy

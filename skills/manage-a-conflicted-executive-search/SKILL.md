# manage-a-conflicted-executive-search

Independence must exist in authority, information, and decision records.

## When to use

- Use for chief executive, board, public-interest, regulated, founder-succession, or other high-impact searches with material conflicts or confidential market information.
- Use a standard structured hiring process when conflicts, disclosure, succession, and governance risk are not material.

## Preconditions

- Establish governing-document, fiduciary, employment, listing, regulatory, privacy, background-check, compensation, and disclosure advice appropriate to the organization.
- Preserve succession plans, role mandate, conflicts, prior commitments, search-firm terms, candidate sources, communications, market-sensitive information, scorecards, expenses, and approvals.
- Name an independent search chair or committee with authority to recuse conflicted participants and obtain separate advice.

## Procedure

1. Approve an **independent mandate and governance** record covering role outcomes, authority, committee, quorum, recusals, advisers, timeline, confidentiality, candidate care, emergency succession, and appointment approval.
2. Build **conflict management** around a register for directors, investors, incumbent leaders, executives, advisers, search firms, candidates, references, compensation providers, and related parties. Apply recusal across the full influence chain, including role design, adviser selection, sourcing, slate control, informal contact, interviews, references, compensation, recommendation, and vote, with access and contact logs.
3. Classify each conflict, mitigation, information restriction, recusal, independent review, residual risk, and approval. A disclosure alone is not always a sufficient control.
4. Separate nomination, assessment, reference, compensation, and final approval authority where concentration would undermine independence.
5. Create a current role and success profile from strategy, risk, stakeholders, culture, regulation, succession, and transition needs rather than tailoring criteria to a favored person.
6. Define evidence, rubric, interview structure, work or scenario exercise, reference plan, background scope, accommodations, and comparable decision rules before substantive candidate review.
7. Manage candidate sourcing through authorized, documented channels and reconcile whether the search firm has off-limits, referral-fee, candidate-ownership, or competing-client conflicts.
8. Protect candidate identity and market-sensitive information with need-to-know access, coded materials where useful, secure systems, retention, communication protocols, and leak response.
9. Give each serious candidate equivalent role, process, data-use, conflict, compensation-framework, timeline, and disclosure information unless a documented job-related reason differs.
10. Record assessment evidence by criterion, source, recency, contradiction, uncertainty, and independence. Verify claims and give candidates a route to correct material errors.
11. Conduct references and background checks only at the authorized stage, with appropriate notice or authority, consistent scope, restricted data, and contextual review.
12. Build a **candidate assessment and decision** record that includes alternatives, conflicts, recusals, dissent, unresolved risk, compensation independence, approval, and why the selected evidence fits the mandate.
13. Negotiate through authorized representatives with versioned terms, no undisclosed side arrangements, and independent compensation review.
14. Coordinate **confidentiality and disclosure** across the candidate, employer, current employer where relevant, regulators, market announcements, workers, partners, and succession operations.
15. Rehearse appointment failure, leak, candidate withdrawal, failed check, vote deadlock, counteroffer, emergency interim leadership, and transition before final announcement.
16. After appointment, close candidate data and expenses, notify participants respectfully, execute access and transition controls, and review search effectiveness without rewriting the record.

## Failure plan

- If the decision body cannot achieve independent quorum, pause appointment or use the authorized alternate process.
- If criteria change to favor a known candidate, document the change, reassess all candidates, and obtain independent approval before proceeding.
- If confidential market information leaks, preserve evidence, restrict further access, activate disclosure and incident authority, and protect candidates from speculation.
- If a selected candidate fails a material condition, use the rehearsed alternate or interim plan rather than suppressing the evidence.

## Worked example

A listed company's board must replace its chief executive quickly. The chair favors a longtime associate, one search partner has a referral-fee connection, the incumbent controls internal candidate information, and a pending acquisition makes names market-sensitive. An independent committee records recusals and adviser conflicts, approves role criteria before reviewing people, gives candidates comparable assessment and correction rights, separates compensation review, preserves dissent and evidence, rehearses interim succession, and coordinates appointment disclosure only after every condition passes.

## Done

- A search mandate and conflict register records authority, criteria, committee, advisers, conflicts, mitigations, recusals, confidentiality, succession, and approval rules
- A candidate assessment and decision record proves comparable evidence, verification, correction, references, checks, alternatives, dissent, compensation independence, and final authority
- An appointment, disclosure, and transition report verifies conditions, versioned terms, market and stakeholder communications, interim fallback, access, candidate closeout, and post-search review

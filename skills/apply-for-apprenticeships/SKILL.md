# apply-for-apprenticeships

Help someone into a paid-to-learn path: apprenticeships
in the trades — electrical, plumbing, carpentry, HVAC, welding,
automotive, and dozens more. The system is real but bureaucratic,
and it runs on prerequisites, application windows, and aptitude
tests that nobody explains to eighteen-year-olds. The bar: they know
their actual local options, what each requires, and the first
application or prerequisite step is done.

## Ground rules

- Everything verified and current. Program names, union locals,
  wages, and windows change; check the official source for their
  region before telling them anything, and link what you find.
  Never state a wage or requirement from memory.
- Nothing invented on any application — apprenticeship programs
  verify diplomas, transcripts, licenses, and work history, and a
  fabricated document ends the path permanently. If a prerequisite
  is missing, the plan is to earn it, and you help schedule that.
- Present the real trade-offs flatly: years to journeyman, physical
  demands, starting wage versus finished wage, waitlists. Their
  call, made with real numbers.

## Trade and route

Ask what draws them — working with hands, specific trade, or just
"paid to learn" — and what their region offers. Then map the routes
that exist where they live, checking each: the official registry
(apprenticeship.gov in the US, or the national equivalent), union
joint-apprenticeship programs (JATCs and locals for their trade and
area — often the best pay and training), non-union and employer
programs (contractors, utilities, manufacturers), and pre-
apprenticeship programs, the honest on-ramp when they are not yet
competitive. Build a short list with links, wages as posted, and
application windows.

## Prerequisites, honestly

For each shortlisted program, list what it demands: minimum age,
diploma or equivalent, specific math credits, driver's license,
physical requirements, aptitude test, sometimes a drug screen. Mark
each one met or not met. For every gap, the concrete fix — the
equivalency exam, the math class, the license appointment, a pre-
apprenticeship — with a date attached. This gap list is the real
plan; treat it as a deliverable.

## Apply

For programs open now: gather the paper (ID, transcripts,
references), fill the application together with them typing, and
rehearse the aptitude test if there is one — most trades test basic
math and reading, and a practice run removes the fear. Union
programs often require an in-person interview or ranked intake;
prep it with interview-prep, aimed at reliability and willingness
to learn, the two things panels actually score. For programs whose
window is closed, the application-ready packet gets built now and
the open date goes on their calendar. Everything lands in their
tracker (job-application-tracker builds one).

## Done

- A verified short list of local programs with wages and windows
- Every prerequisite marked met or scheduled
- First application submitted or packet ready with a calendar date

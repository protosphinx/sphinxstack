# recover-from-a-published-media-error

Correct the record in proportion to the harm and reach.

## When to use

- Use for factual, identity, translation, privacy, edit, rights, safety, accessibility, graphic, caption, or technical errors already distributed.
- Use a routine silent file replacement only for a trivial issue whose history and harm do not require visible correction.

## Preconditions

- Establish editorial, affected-person, fact, legal, privacy, rights, platform, syndication, accessibility, support, communications, and security authority.
- Preserve exact published files, pages, posts, metadata, captions, transcripts, thumbnails, timestamps, audience reach, caches, partner deliveries, downloads, comments, and source evidence.
- Stop automated republishing or promotion when it can amplify harm.

## Procedure

1. Start a **published media error timeline** with detection, reporter, versions, channels, reach, decisions, contacts, corrections, and verification.
2. Perform an **error and harm assessment** across what is wrong, evidence, affected people, safety, privacy, reputation, rights, accessibility, financial impact, virality, search, and ongoing exposure.
3. Contact affected people through a safe qualified channel where appropriate, without making removal or confidentiality promises that cannot be kept.
4. Build a **claim, version, and distribution reconciliation** with immutable source and version identifiers linking every assertion and media segment to all descendant edits, languages, masters, derivatives, platforms, embeds, syndicates, downloads, caches, and archives.
5. Apply **evidence preservation** to the erroneous state under restricted access before changing it; do not delete the only evidence or rewrite audit history.
6. Choose containment: pause ads, unpublish, restrict, replace, remove a segment, disable embeds, stop syndication, issue a warning, or keep available with an immediate correction.
7. Prepare a correction that identifies the material error, supplies accurate information, dates the change, explains affected versions, and avoids repeating unnecessary harm.
8. Repair the authoritative master, captions, transcript, audio description, translations, graphics, metadata, thumbnails, articles, clips, feeds, search markup, and future templates.
9. Propagate **correction and recovery** through owned platforms, distributors, partners, caches, downloads where recall is possible, social posts, newsletters, archives, and customer files.
10. Support affected people with specific follow-through such as identity correction, privacy response, source protection, contextual update, or accessible replacement.
11. Verify public and restricted destinations independently and record unreachable or retained copies.
12. Review cause across sourcing, edit, fact-check, translation, version, approval, automation, platform, and monitoring; implement and test controls.

## Failure plan

- If removing the media creates a greater immediate public-safety risk, preserve a bounded accessible notice while qualified owners decide.
- If the affected person cannot be reached, continue proportionate correction without exposing more private information.
- If a platform cannot update the asset, publish a visible linked correction and pursue provider escalation.
- Do not frame a correction as complete while known derivatives or partners still serve the error.

## Worked example

A documentary excerpt identifies the wrong person, a translated caption changes the allegation, and the clip has been embedded, syndicated, downloaded, cached, and promoted. The team stops promotion, preserves each version, assesses identity and safety harm, contacts the affected person safely, reconciles sources and derivatives, corrects the master plus access tracks, issues a clear dated correction, notifies partners, verifies propagation, records residual copies, and repairs the identity and translation review controls.

## Done

- A published media error timeline records detection, evidence, harm, versions, containment, affected-person contact, correction, distribution actions, and verification
- A claim, version, and distribution reconciliation proves sources, edits, languages, masters, derivatives, platforms, partners, caches, downloads, reach, and residual copies
- A correction, harm, and recovery report verifies accurate replacement, visible notices, accessible versions, affected-person support, propagation, root cause, control changes, and effectiveness

# write-a-scholarship-essay

Build the essay around specific lived evidence. The applicant owns the story, judgments, and final language.

## Procedure

1. Capture the exact prompt, eligibility rules, word limit, audience, deadline, and evaluation criteria.
2. Inventory relevant experiences, actions, obstacles, choices, results, learning, and future plans without embellishment.
3. Match each possible story to the prompt and select one with clear personal agency and reflection.
4. Write a one-sentence answer and outline a beginning, turning point, evidence, meaning, and forward connection.
5. Draft in the applicant's natural voice using concrete scenes and verifiable details.
6. Make the scholarship connection specific without flattering the organization or repeating its website.
7. Revise for prompt coverage, focus, chronology, agency, reflection, and word economy.
8. Read aloud, check every factual claim, and have the applicant approve the final text.

## Failure plan

- Never invent hardship, leadership, identity, service, awards, dialogue, dates, or impact.
- Do not recycle one essay without adapting it to the actual prompt.
- If a story involves another person's private experience, minimize detail and obtain appropriate consent.
- If the draft sounds unlike the applicant, return to their spoken phrasing and recorded facts.

## Done

- The essay directly answers the verified prompt within the word limit
- Every material claim is checked against the applicant's evidence or testimony
- The applicant has read, revised, and approved the submitted version

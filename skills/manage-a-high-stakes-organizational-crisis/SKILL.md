# manage-a-high-stakes-organizational-crisis

Unify decisions without collapsing independent safety, legal, investigative, and governance duties.

## When to use

- Use for existential or multi-domain crises with material harm, uncertainty, and executive-level decisions.
- Activate qualified emergency, safety, security, finance, legal, regulatory, privacy, safeguarding, communications, and board support.

## Preconditions

- Establish crisis lead, board or governing authority, independent safety and investigation authority, and domain leads with deputies.
- Define people at risk, essential services, jurisdictions, entities, materiality, privilege, disclosure, evidence, spending, and stop authority.
- Use secure resilient coordination and alternate channels if normal systems or leadership paths may be compromised.

## Procedure

Complete **unified command and human protection**, **facts, evidence, and decisions**, **stakeholder and legal communication**, and **recovery and accountability** throughout the crisis.

1. Open a **crisis timeline and command register** with events, sources, uncertainty, harms, services, decisions, authority, actions, communications, evidence, and timestamps.
2. Protect life, health, safety, vulnerable people, employees, customers, and essential services before reputation or financial optimization.
3. Separate command, investigation, safeguarding, legal advice, financial control, communications, and board oversight while defining interfaces.
4. Establish verified facts, hypotheses, unknowns, confidence, owners, and next evidence; prevent rumor or desired narratives from becoming fact.
5. Preserve physical, digital, financial, operational, communication, access, and decision evidence with legal and privacy controls.
6. Build an **impact, obligation, and action reconciliation** across people, customers, services, money, data, assets, regulators, partners, claims, and jurisdictions.
7. Stabilize essential operations through tested continuity, safe reduction, alternate suppliers, emergency funding, access controls, and one-owner in-flight actions.
8. Identify immediate legal, regulatory, market, employment, safety, privacy, insurance, contractual, and public-disclosure duties with qualified owners.
9. Communicate verified facts, uncertainty, protective action, service state, and update timing consistently to affected groups; correct errors visibly.
10. Manage conflicts, recusals, whistleblowers, retaliation risk, executive succession, board independence, and investigation integrity.
11. Develop time-bounded survive, stabilize, recover, restructure, sell, close, or other options with people, service, financial, legal, and trust consequences.
12. Apply a cross-workstream consequence gate before material containment or recovery: assess effects on human and product safety, cyber exposure, liquidity, vendor dependencies, customer harm, whistleblower protection, essential services, and regulatory deadlines. Record decision, alternatives, dissent, assumptions, authority, reversibility, triggers, and owner.
13. Transition from crisis command through explicit criteria, residual-risk acceptance, owner handoff, support, and monitoring.
14. Publish or retain an accountable review, remediate root controls, compensate or support affected people where due, and test reforms.

## Failure plan

- If leadership is implicated or conflicted, transfer decisions to independent governing authority and protect evidence plus reporters.
- If facts cannot support a public claim, state what is known, unknown, and being done rather than speculate.
- If resources cannot sustain all services, apply lawful transparent life, safety, and essential-service priorities.
- If the crisis changes materially, reopen command scope and decisions instead of defending an obsolete plan.

## Worked example

A company faces a product-safety event, cyber compromise, liquidity shock, vendor outage, executive conflict, whistleblower allegations, customer harm, regulator deadlines, and intense media attention at the same time, while internal communications and facts are unreliable. The board establishes independent command and investigation, protects people and essential services, reconciles obligations, preserves evidence, communicates verified uncertainty, manages conflicts, chooses recovery options, and tests reforms before normal governance resumes.

## Done

- A crisis timeline and command register verifies events, facts, uncertainty, harms, services, authority, decisions, actions, communications, and evidence custody
- An impact, obligation, and action reconciliation proves people, customer, service, financial, data, asset, regulator, partner, claim, remedy, and unresolved status
- A recovery, accountability, and reform report demonstrates continuity, legal and disclosure handling, conflicts, options, decisions, handoff, affected-person support, remediation, and tested control effectiveness

# video-resume

Make a 60-second video introduction with someone — them,
on camera, saying who they are and what they have done. Use this only when the
application explicitly requests or accepts a video, or when the person has
confirmed that the recipient welcomes one. An unsolicited video can expose
personal information and introduce bias; do not add it to every resume by
default. The session ends with a reviewed video and a controlled share link.

## Privacy and consent

- Do not state an exact age, home address, school schedule, identity number,
  or other private detail. Use only the contact information already approved
  for the application.
- Use an unlisted or access-controlled link. Explain that anyone with an
  unlisted link may still copy or forward it.
- The person decides whether the benefit is worth the exposure. If the
  application does not accept video, stop and use the written resume.

## Script: their words, their facts

- Everything said must be true and match the written resume (build
  it first with the build-resume skill if it does not exist).
- Structure, four beats, ~60 seconds total:
  1. Who: name, and the one-line version of what they do or study.
  2. Proof: the strongest one or two real things — a project with a
     link, a job, a result. Specifics carry this beat; "I built X,
     it does Y, here is where it lives."
  3. Want: what they are looking for, concretely.
  4. Close: name again, "thanks", done. No catchphrases.
- Write it by interview: ask, they answer out loud, you transcribe
  and tighten. Read the draft back — if any sentence sounds like a
  LinkedIn post instead of like them talking, rewrite it in what
  they actually said. Word count check: ~140–150 words is a minute.
- They rehearse from beats, not from the full text. Memorized
  scripts read as recited; beats keep it a person talking.

## Setup

Phone camera, and it is enough:

- Horizontal, at eye level (stack of books works), arm-and-a-half
  away. Lens at eye height matters more than any other setting.
- Light: face a window, daytime. Never a window behind them.
- Background: a plain wall or a tidy real corner of their room.
  Real beats staged; messy beats neither.
- Audio: quiet room, or wired earbuds as the mic — run the
  record-clean-audio checks. Speech must remain clear on ordinary speakers.
- Clothes: whatever they would wear to an interview for this
  target. A 5-second test clip to check framing and sound first.

## Takes and edit

- Full run-throughs only, no stopping mid-take: fumbles included,
  straight to the end, then again. Take 1 is a warm-up by
  definition. Most people land it between takes 3 and 6; past 8,
  break, because tired takes get stiffer.
- Pick the take where they sound most like themselves — not the
  one with zero fumbles. A small stumble and a recovery reads
  human.
- Edit lightly in CapCut or DaVinci free: trim to start on their
  first word and end after the close, level the audio, done. No
  music, no titles, no transitions. Optionally cut between the
  best two takes if the framing matches.
- Watch it together once at phone size and once with sound off —
  posture and framing should hold up mute.

## Ship

1. Export 1080p MP4.
2. Upload to YouTube as unlisted — findable by link only, not
   searchable, not on their channel page for subscribers to trip
   over. Title it "Firstname Lastname — Introduction".
3. Caption it (captions-and-subtitles skill) — recruiters watch
   muted at desks.
4. Put the link only in the application field or message where video was
   requested or confirmed as welcome. Test it from a logged-out browser.

## Done

- The recipient accepts video and the person made an informed sharing choice
- Link works logged out, captions are correct, and every claim matches the
  written application
- No unnecessary personal information appears in speech, background, title,
  description, or account profile

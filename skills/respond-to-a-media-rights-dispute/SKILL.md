# respond-to-a-media-rights-dispute

Separate the claimant, right, asset, version, use, and remedy before acting.

## When to use

- Use for formal notices, platform claims, contributor disputes, contract conflicts, mistaken takedowns, attribution demands, or suspected unauthorized media use.
- Obtain qualified legal advice for deadlines, jurisdiction, privilege, exceptions, settlement, or litigation decisions.

## Preconditions

- Establish rights, legal, editorial, production, distribution, platform, finance, insurer, communications, and affected-person authority.
- Preserve the exact notice, delivery metadata, claimant identity, asset, published versions, source files, edits, contracts, licenses, releases, invoices, credits, platform records, revenue, and communications.
- Set one controlled response channel and protect deadlines without admitting facts not yet established.

## Procedure

1. Start a **media rights dispute timeline** with receipt, deadlines, claims, uses, actions, holds, decisions, and communications.
2. Establish **dispute scope and rights inventory** by verifying the claimant and notice independently and identifying each asserted work, recording, image, performance, likeness, mark, confidential item, right, territory, term, platform, and requested remedy.
3. Build a **rights chain and usage reconciliation** from creator and source through assignment, license, contributor release, edit, derivative, upload, syndication, advertising, archive, revenue, and current availability.
4. Match the exact disputed file and segment to source hashes, audio or visual fingerprints, timecode, edit decisions, captions, credits, and license version so renamed, cropped, transcoded, and different-duration derivatives remain discoverable.
5. Maintain **evidence preservation** and competing hypotheses: valid claim, partial right, wrong asset, wrong claimant, expired or limited permission, platform mismatch, contract ambiguity, exception, fraudulent notice, or internal error.
6. Apply proportionate **containment and use decisions**: hold new distribution, disable a disputed segment, pause paid promotion, preserve evidence, quarantine derivatives, or maintain use under authorized advice.
7. Do not destroy files, alter records, contact represented claimants informally, or submit a platform counter-notice without authorized factual and legal review.
8. Assess affected participants, sources, audiences, customers, partners, revenue, accessibility versions, translations, archives, and downstream licensees.
9. Choose response: acknowledge, request specificity, correct credit, replace, edit, obtain permission, pay agreed fee, dispute, counter, restore, notify partners, or negotiate.
10. Propagate the approved action through masters, platforms, embeds, ads, syndication, downloads, social clips, thumbnails, captions, archives, customer files, and future templates.
11. Verify each destination and reconcile revenue, fees, credits, refunds, royalties, claims, takedowns, restoration, and retained exceptions.
12. Complete **resolution and remediation** by repairing rights intake, contract metadata, approval, version linkage, cue sheets, attribution, platform policy, and recurrence monitoring.

## Failure plan

- If a deadline precedes complete analysis, preserve rights and request or take the narrowest authorized protective action.
- If a claimant cannot be verified, do not expose private records or pay; preserve the notice and use official verification channels.
- If a removal creates a new accessibility or factual error, publish a verified replacement or clear notice rather than silently breaking the work.
- If downstream copies cannot be recalled, document residual reach and continue correction and notification efforts.

## Worked example

A global campaign receives a platform claim and a lawyer's notice alleging that one music recording, an archival image, and a contributor's likeness exceed their licenses. Contracts conflict, several localized edits use different durations, paid ads are live, partners downloaded masters, and platform deadlines are short. The team preserves the notice and every version, maps each right and use, pauses unsupported promotion, verifies the actual segments and claimant, resolves each asset separately, propagates replacement or restoration, reconciles payments and credits, and fixes the rights-version linkage that allowed the dispute.

## Done

- A media rights dispute timeline records notice, verification, deadlines, claims, evidence, containment, advice, decisions, communications, and resolution
- A rights chain and usage reconciliation proves asset, source, ownership, permissions, versions, edits, territories, terms, platforms, credits, revenue, and downstream distribution
- A resolution and corrective-action report verifies removals or restoration, replacements, credits, payments, partner notices, residual reach, rights controls, and recurrence monitoring

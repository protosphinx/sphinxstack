# manage-a-business-renewal-calendar

A renewal date is often too late to act. Anchor work to the earliest notice, review, procurement, migration, or approval deadline.

## When to use

- Use for vendor contracts, leases, insurance, domains, certifications, permits, support, and recurring services.
- Verify current terms from the governing contract or authority instead of relying only on invoices or prior calendars.

## Procedure

1. Define entities, accounts, locations, systems, and commitment types in scope.
2. Inventory each item with owner, vendor or authority, agreement locator, term, renewal date, notice deadline, amount, currency, and payment method.
3. Record auto-renewal, price adjustment, minimum term, termination method, data-export, transition, and proof-of-notice requirements.
4. Calculate internal review dates that allow usage review, alternatives, negotiation, security or legal review, budget approval, and migration.
5. Assign a primary and backup owner with escalation before every critical date.
6. Attach evidence links without exposing credentials or unnecessary sensitive records.
7. At each review, classify renew, renegotiate, replace, consolidate, cancel, or investigate with rationale and approvals.
8. Send notices through the contractually accepted channel and preserve delivery evidence.
9. Reconcile invoices and access after the decision; close only when the intended term and price are confirmed.
10. Audit the calendar periodically against payables, statements, contracts, domain records, and licensing systems.

## Failure plan

- Do not cancel domains, insurance, security, access, or regulated services without dependency and authority checks.
- If terms conflict, preserve both and obtain authoritative interpretation.
- Never mark a renewal complete based on an email promise alone.
- Keep unknown dates visible and escalated.

## Done

- Every material commitment has verified terms, advance dates, owners, and evidence
- Decisions and notices occur before contractual deadlines
- The calendar reconciles to actual invoices, access, and renewed or terminated terms

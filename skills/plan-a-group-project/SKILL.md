# plan-a-group-project

Create one shared plan that makes dependencies, decisions, and contribution visible. Keep the students
responsible for the academic work while helping them coordinate and verify it.

## Inputs

- Obtain the assignment, rubric, deadline, required format, group members, accessibility needs, and course rules.
- Ask what work already exists, what each member can own, and when people are actually available.
- Confirm the allowed use of AI, external sources, peer editing, and shared documents.

## Procedure

1. Read the assignment and rubric together. Translate them into deliverables and observable checks without
   writing the graded content.
2. Agree on the project's central question, scope, audience, and source-of-truth folder or document.
3. Break deliverables into tasks small enough for one owner and one review point. Mark dependencies and integration work.
4. Assign work by interest, skill, availability, and learning goals. Check that invisible coordination and
   editing work are not concentrated on one person.
5. Set internal milestones before the official deadline: research complete, rough parts, first integration,
   feedback, revision, final check, and submission.
6. Define file naming, citation, formatting, decision, and communication rules. Record decisions where all
   members can see them.
7. Create a contribution log containing task, owner, status, evidence link, review, and blocker.
8. Integrate early. Use the rubric to check consistency of argument, terminology, citations, visuals, and transitions.
9. When a task slips, contact the owner privately, clarify the blocker, resize or reassign transparently,
   and update the plan. Do not conceal non-participation.
10. Rehearse the final presentation or submission process, including file access, timing, speaking roles,
   and a backup copy.
11. Hold a short retrospective and preserve an accurate contribution record if the course requests it.

## Boundaries

Do not write graded answers, fabricate sources, impersonate a student, or falsify contribution. Do not
publish classmates' schedules or private circumstances. Escalate persistent participation or safety issues
through the instructor's stated process rather than shaming a member.

## Done

- A shared plan maps every rubric deliverable to an owner, milestone, dependency, and evidence link
- The contribution log accurately records work, reviews, decisions, blockers, and reassignment
- An integrated draft is checked against the rubric before the final deadline
- Submission or presentation logistics, permissions, timing, and a backup copy are verified by the group

Then use slide-deck for the presentation structure or references-list for a clean source record.

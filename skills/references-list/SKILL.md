# references-list

Build a references list with someone who has probably
never been asked for one. The bar: three people who have agreed to
vouch for them, correct contact details confirmed, and a clean
one-page document matching their resume, ready to send the moment
an employer asks.

## Ground rules

- Nobody goes on the list without saying yes first. A surprised
  reference is a bad reference. The permission ask happens in this
  sitting, in the person's own words.
- Real people with real contact details they have verified — no
  guessed emails, no listing a manager who left, no relatives
  presented as employers.
- References are a separate document, sent only when requested.
  They do not live on the resume, and "references available upon
  request" earns its line nowhere.

## Choosing who

List every adult who has seen them work or follow through:
employers, shift supervisors, teachers, coaches, club advisors,
people they babysat or mowed lawns for. Rank by two questions —
what would this person say if called today, and can they speak to
work rather than character alone? Aim for three, one of them a
work-type reference if any exists. Friends and family are out.
If the list comes up empty, the fix is the same as a thin resume:
do something an adult witnesses (https://sphinxstack.com/skills/),
then come back.

## Getting the yes

For each pick, they draft a short ask — text, email, or in person:
what they are applying for, whether the person is willing to be a
reference, and which phone or email to list. Workshop the wording;
they send it themselves. While replies come in, keep building with
the confirmed ones. When someone agrees, confirm how they want to
be reached and their current title — do not guess either.

## The document

One page, same header (name, city, email, phone) and same fonts as
the resume so the two read as a set. For each reference: name,
title, organization, relationship in plain words ("shift
supervisor at Dairy Queen, summer 2025"), phone, email — details
exactly as the reference approved them. Export to PDF the same way
as the resume, named `references-<their-name>.pdf`.

## Done

- Three (or the posting's required number of) confirmed references
- Each said yes and approved their listed contact details
- One-page PDF styled to match the resume, ready to attach
- They know the etiquette: send only when asked, tell references
  which job to expect a call about, thank them after

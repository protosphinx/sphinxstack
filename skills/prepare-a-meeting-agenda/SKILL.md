# prepare-a-meeting-agenda

Design the meeting around work that benefits from synchronous attention. Move reading and routine status into pre-work.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the meeting owner, participants, duration, decision authority, and required outcome.
2. Test whether the outcome needs a meeting; cancel or replace it with an asynchronous artifact when it does not.
3. Write a one-sentence purpose and explicit outputs such as a decision, plan, diagnosis, or commitment.
4. Include only participants who contribute evidence, make the decision, execute the result, or are materially affected.
5. For each item, name the owner, question, pre-read, evidence, method, timebox, and expected output.
6. Put consequential or uncertain work before low-value updates and reserve time for synthesis.
7. Send accessible pre-work early enough to review and identify missing inputs before the meeting.
8. Add note ownership, decision rules, parking-lot handling, and the channel for follow-up.
9. Recheck that the timed agenda fits and that the needed authority and evidence will be present.

## Failure plan

- Do not use an agenda to disguise a predetermined decision as consultation.
- Never require sensitive information in broadly shared pre-work.
- If required evidence or decision authority is absent, rescope or postpone rather than perform agreement.
- Protect breaks, accessibility needs, time zones, and stated working-hour boundaries.

## Done

- Purpose, outcomes, participants, pre-work, owners, and timeboxes are recorded
- Decision authority and evidence readiness are confirmed
- The agenda is sent and opens correctly for every participant

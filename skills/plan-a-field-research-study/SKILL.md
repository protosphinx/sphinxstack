# plan-a-field-research-study

Operational feasibility and participant safety are part of the method.

## When to use

- Use for observation, interview, environmental, ecological, archaeological, ethnographic, or operational fieldwork.
- Do not enter sites, collect data, or represent authority without permission and appropriate review.

## Preconditions

- Define question, sites, populations, seasons, methods, partners, permits, ethics, hazards, languages, accessibility, and resources.

## Procedure

1. Map site owners, community authority, permits, gatekeepers, participants, bystanders, and vulnerable groups.
2. Design sampling, recruitment, consent, observation, measurement, refusal, withdrawal, and nonresponse handling.
3. Assess travel, weather, terrain, health, security, political, equipment, wildlife, lone-work, and safeguarding risks.
4. Create communications, check-in, emergency, evacuation, medical, incident, and stop plans.
5. Pilot instruments, translations, equipment, identifiers, storage, backup, and transfer.
6. Define daily provenance, quality, deviation, consent, custody, and debrief records.
7. Preauthorize bounded adaptations and require review for changes that affect inference or consent.
8. Plan site restoration, participant follow-up, data return, compensation, reporting, and exit.

## Failure plan

- Stop for unsafe conditions, invalid permission, compromised consent, equipment invalidity, or custody loss.
- Quarantine questionable data and record the deviation rather than repairing it silently.

## Worked example

A coastal survey defines tide and storm stops, land permissions, paired equipment checks, encrypted daily transfer, nonresponse logs, and community result return.

## Done

- A field-research plan documents access, sample, consent, safety, logistics, instruments, custody, adaptations, incidents, and exit
- Permit, ethics, equipment, translation, emergency, communication, backup, deviation, and site-restoration rehearsals verify readiness

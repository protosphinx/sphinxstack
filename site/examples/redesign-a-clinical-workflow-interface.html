Worked case: redesign medication workflows without hiding hazards

Trigger: A hospital must redesign medication ordering and administration across emergency, inpatient, pharmacy, and nursing workflows while alert fatigue, unit confusion, duplicate orders, hidden pending states, local safety workarounds, legacy devices, unreliable integrations, accessibility needs, and downtime procedures create patient risk.

Can we remove every local workaround?
Not until observation shows which adaptations prevent harm and which create it.
Are cleaner screens enough validation?
No. Simulate critical tasks, interruptions, integrations, accessibility needs, and downstream clinical effects.
Can rollback restore the old UI after new orders exist?
Only if authoritative orders, administration, results, and documentation remain reconciled.
What blocks a unit from launch?
Any severe unresolved hazard, missing representative validation, unsafe downtime path, or absent safety monitoring.

Failure rehearsal

A pharmacy interface times out after accepting an order while the nursing view still shows pending. Expansion stops, the authoritative order and audit state are reconciled, the approved downtime workflow protects care, and the integration plus status design is retested before the unit returns.

Questions people actually ask

Can policy documents replace observation?

No. Compare prescribed work with actual safe and unsafe adaptations.

Should every alert remain?

No. Each alert needs a defined hazard, action, priority, and effectiveness.

Who accepts residual risk?

The named qualified clinical and governance authority, not the interface team alone.

Can a low-risk unit prove all settings?

No. Each material role, patient risk, device, and care context needs appropriate evidence.

When can the old workflow retire?

After training, devices, integrations, records, support, and safety gates have verified dispositions.

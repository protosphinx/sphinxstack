Worked case: govern data behind credit and fraud decisions

Trigger: A lender wants one dataset for credit eligibility and fraud models across countries, combining historical approvals, repayment, manual investigations, bureau data, device signals, customer appeals, and prior-model scores, while past policies differed, rejected applicants lack outcome labels, identity and disability proxies may exist, vendor rights vary, and downstream teams want to reuse the data.

Can repayment be the label for everyone?
Rejected applicants have no comparable repayment outcome, and approvals reflect historical selection policy.
Can prior-model scores be normal features?
They create feedback and policy lineage; isolate, justify, and test their influence explicitly.
May another team reuse the release?
Only if its purpose, rights, population, limitations, and approved dataset generation support that use.
What blocks release?
Unresolved rights, invalid labels, unknown provenance, unsafe population gaps, split leakage, or missing appeal and stop controls.

Historical decisions are not automatically valid ground truth.

Failure rehearsal

A vendor cannot establish reuse rights for bureau-derived fields and one sparse disability-related subgroup has unstable error estimates. The release excludes the disputed source, records population uncertainty, restricts unsupported claims and uses, and requires new evidence before expansion.

Questions people actually ask

Does removing protected traits remove bias?

No. Proxies, selection, labels, access, and historical policy can preserve disparities.

Can synthetic data solve missing populations?

It cannot create verified real-world outcomes or erase collection bias.

Should train and test split randomly?

Prevent entity, temporal, household, and source leakage according to deployment conditions.

Can a sparse group be declared fair?

Report uncertainty and restrict claims rather than treating absence of evidence as parity.

What happens after deletion?

Propagate a new generation and identify affected descendants, models, and evaluations.

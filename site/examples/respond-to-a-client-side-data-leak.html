Worked case: contain a browser data leak across third parties

Trigger: After a tag-manager release, a session-replay and analytics chain sends form values, account identifiers, password-reset tokens in URLs, and browser-storage fields to several third parties, while CDN and vendor logs retain requests and consent behavior differs by country.

Should we just remove the replay tag?
Stop unsafe collection, but preserve deployed code and trace every field, recipient, log, cache, and downstream copy.
Do URL tokens make the incident broader?
Yes. Check browser history, referrers, analytics, CDN logs, vendor logs, support tools, and caches.
Can a vendor deletion promise close it?
Require scoped evidence for stored copies and downstream recipients, then record any residual exposure honestly.
What protects users now?
Contain collection, rotate or invalidate exposed secrets, restrict recipient access, support affected people, and communicate from verified facts.

Failure rehearsal

A vendor can restrict access but cannot prove deletion from backups. Responders preserve that limitation, continue contractual and privacy handling, protect affected accounts, state residual risk accurately, and monitor for misuse rather than closing the incident on a verbal assurance.

Questions people actually ask

Can payload samples be copied into a ticket?

Use minimized restricted evidence; do not reproduce sensitive fields unnecessarily.

Should every session be revoked?

Base protection on exposed secrets, affected users, misuse risk, and qualified incident judgment.

Does consent make secret leakage acceptable?

No. Consent does not authorize unrelated or insecure exposure.

Can disabling analytics end the incident?

No. Stored copies, downstream sharing, logs, user harm, and root cause remain.

When is scope complete?

When fields, populations, time, recipients, copies, access, deletion, and uncertainty reconcile.

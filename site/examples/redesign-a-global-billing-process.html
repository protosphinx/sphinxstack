Worked case: replace global billing without corrupting customer balances

Trigger: A global subscription company must redesign billing across contracts, usage, seats, multiple currencies, taxes, discounts, credits, invoices, collections, revenue schedules, payment methods, refunds, and regional legal terms while legacy and new engines overlap and duplicate invoices, lost credits, or misstated balances are unacceptable.

Can both billing engines issue invoices during the dual run?
No. One engine owns mutation; the other calculates and renders in shadow mode.
What identity survives retries and cutover?
Use a durable business key plus generation epoch, and fence every stale worker.
Can rollback delete new invoices?
Never delete issued financial history. Preserve documents and payments, then reconcile or correct forward.
What proves a cohort is ready?
Contract, line, tax, balance, ledger, collection, notification, and document parity with no unexplained material difference.

Dual calculation is safe only when financial mutation has one owner.

Failure rehearsal

A stale worker issues invoices after a regional cohort moves to the new engine. The team stops the wave, fences the old generation, preserves both invoice versions, prevents collection on the duplicate, traces notifications and ledger postings, corrects customer balances, and reruns the parity gate before resuming.

Questions people actually ask

Can the shadow engine send customer emails?

No. Notifications are customer-facing mutations and remain with the authoritative engine.

Can old invoice numbers be regenerated?

No. Preserve issued numbering and link corrections through governed references.

How should credits move?

Reconcile each credit's origin, remaining amount, currency, tax effect, application, and customer balance.

What happens during financial close?

Coordinate freeze windows, cutoffs, ledger reconciliation, and explicit approval before changing authority.

When can the legacy engine retire?

Only after dispute, tax, history, retention, audit, access, and recovery duties have proven owners.

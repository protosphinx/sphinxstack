Worked case: find hidden concentration

Trigger: A critical service appears diversified across vendors, but contracts and architecture may hide common cloud, geographic, platform, capacity, specialist, and subcontractor dependencies with untested exits.

We use four vendors, so can we report low concentration?
Not yet. Vendor count is not independence. I need the dependency inventory, ownership, hosting, routes, subcontractors, specialists, and outcome mapping.
Two vendors say they can take the full workload within a week.
Treat that as an unverified claim until the substitutability test covers reserved capacity, data, integrations, approvals, skills, security, accessibility, and transition time.
The contracts contain exit clauses.
Contract rights help, but exit feasibility also needs usable exports, knowledge, staffing, acceptance, parallel operation, and rollback evidence.
What would reduce the risk?
A mitigation must change the dependency path or prove a workable alternative, then survive a scenario exercise and monitored trigger.

Multiple contracts can still share one failure path.

Failure rehearsal

During a regional platform outage, the named backup supplier cannot accept data because it uses the same identity service and has no reserved capacity. The team records the common-mode failure, maintains minimum manual service, rejects the paper-only mitigation, and redesigns the alternative before reducing residual risk.

Questions people actually ask

Is supplier count a concentration metric?

It is one input, but common ownership, infrastructure, geography, capacity, skills, and routes determine independence.

Can contract exit rights prove recoverability?

No. Operational exit also needs accessible data, working integrations, people, capacity, acceptance, and rollback.

How should unknown subcontractors be scored?

Keep them as open exposure with an owner and due date rather than assuming independence.

Must every service have two live vendors?

No. Compare proportional mitigations such as redesign, inventory, capacity, portability, manual service, and accepted residual risk.

What proves reduced concentration?

A changed dependency path or an exercised alternative that meets minimum service under a realistic common-mode scenario.

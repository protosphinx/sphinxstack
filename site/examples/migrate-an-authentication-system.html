Worked case: move identities without guessing account ownership

Trigger: A live business application must move two million accounts from a legacy username and password store to an external identity provider, but duplicate emails, shared mailboxes, local MFA, dormant administrators, service identities, active sessions, and conflicting recovery data make automatic linking unsafe.

Can we link every account that has the same email address?
No. Email text can be duplicated, recycled, shared, or unverified. Define a canonical subject and quarantine ambiguous ownership for proof-based review.
Could users keep all old sessions until they expire?
Only if their subject, tenant, assurance, and privilege remain provable. High-risk cohorts should reauthenticate through the new verifier.
Leadership wants one irreversible weekend cutover.
Stage cohorts, compare outcomes, set pause thresholds, and rehearse rollback before any broad wave.
What proves readiness?
Reconciled identities and roles, successful critical journeys, resolved exceptions, support readiness, and a rollback rehearsal with preserved evidence.

Failure rehearsal

A pilot shows that suspended legacy administrators are active after provisioning. The wave pauses automatically. Target sessions for the affected mapping are revoked, the source-status rule is corrected, the pilot is rebuilt, and identity and privilege counts are reconciled. The rollback path restores login for unaffected users while preserving every target-side event.

Questions people actually ask

Can email be the permanent user ID?

No. Use an immutable internal subject and treat verified email as a changeable attribute.

Must every user reset a password?

Not always. Choose secure transfer, rehash, federation, or reset from source capability and risk.

What happens to active sessions?

Define acceptance overlap, issuer changes, privilege refresh, revocation, and forced reauthentication by cohort.

How are account collisions resolved?

Quarantine them and use approved evidence with stronger controls for privileged accounts.

When should a migration wave stop?

Stop when ownership is ambiguous, privileges drift, recovery weakens, or reconciliation exceeds the approved threshold.

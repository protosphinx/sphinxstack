Scripted example of a real session.

The exam is days away and I haven't started

That is the situation the plan is built for. It works backwards from exam day, puts the "could not start it" topics first because they need the most passes, and fills the hours you actually have rather than the hours a syllabus wishes you had. Most of the time goes to practice problems and explaining from memory, because rereading is the weakest use of the days you have left. You can also build the practice tools yourself:

What you end up with

A dated plan from today to exam day, plus a weak-spot list that grows out of real practice sessions. Here is one, written eight days out.

Example
CHEM 2 · EXAM SAT MAR 14
Written Fri Mar 6  ·  one hour weeknights, two weekend  ·  weak spots: equilibrium, gas laws
THE PLAN
Fri 6 — equilibrium: explain the concept from notes in my own words, then 6 practice problems 1
Sat 7 — equilibrium, second pass: 10 problems, no notes · gas laws: 6 problems
Sun 8 — mixed set, units 4–6, timed 40 min · log every miss 2
Mon 9 — Sunday's misses, re-solved from scratch
Tue 10 — gas laws retest · stoichiometry: 15-minute confidence check only 3
Wed 11 — full practice exam under exam conditions
Thu 12 — Wednesday's misses plus the whole weak-spot list, second pass
Fri 13 — light: read through the weak-spot list, then sleep 4
WEAK-SPOT LIST (RUNNING)
Mar 6 · equilibrium constants — never learned it · rescheduled Sat 7 + Thu 12
Mar 6 · partial pressures — mixed up with concentration · rescheduled Tue 10
  1. "Could not start it" goes first. It needs the most passes, so it gets the most days between first contact and the exam. Weak topics always appear at least twice, spaced days apart.
  2. Every miss gets logged with its kind. Never learned it, mixed it up, or ran out of time: each kind gets a different fix, and each miss gets rescheduled into the plan the moment it happens.
  3. "Could teach it" gets minutes. The plan spends time where the exam will hurt, so the strong topic gets a short confidence check instead of an evening.
  4. The last day is light on purpose. The plan works backwards from exam day, and the night before is review of the weak-spot list only.

Anatomy of a plan block

The skill writes every block to this standard, because "study bio" is a wish and this is a task:

Tue 7–8pm: equilibrium · 10 practice problems, no notes · retest Friday
  • A A date and an hour, written into the calendar you actually check.
  • B One named topic, chosen from your own could-teach / could-pass / could-not-start ratings.
  • C Retrieval work with a count, so "done" is checkable.
  • D The second pass, scheduled days later, before the first pass even happens.

Questions people actually ask

Will it answer my take-home exam or write graded essay answers?

No. If you bring a take-home, a graded quiz, or homework due for credit, it says so once and then offers the legitimate version: practice on similar ungraded problems, an explanation of the underlying concept, or review of an attempt you already made. As the skill puts it, an exam passed on borrowed answers is a course you fail later.

How does the quizzing actually work?

One question at a time, from your real materials: your notes, the textbook, past exams if they exist. It waits for your answer before saying anything, then tells you exactly what was right, what was wrong, and why. Every session ends with you re-answering your misses from memory.

What if the exam is only three days away?

The same plan, compressed. The topics you rated "could not start" still go first, the retrieval-over-rereading rule matters even more, and the last day stays light. Fewer passes fit, so the weak-spot list decides everything that makes the cut.

Do I need past exams or a topic list?

No, but if either exists the skill wants it, because it outranks everything else. Without one, coverage comes from the syllabus or the teacher's own words. The skill refuses to guess the format.

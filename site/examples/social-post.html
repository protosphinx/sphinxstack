Scripted example of a real session.

Isn't posting about my own stuff cringe?

Cringe is a style problem and styles can be cut. What reads as cringe is the costume: false humility on one side, hype voice on the other. A plain statement of what you made, with a picture and a working link, reads as confidence, and it's the post you'd want to see from someone else. The skill also keeps you honest on frequency: one post per real event. An account that only announces real things builds exactly the reputation you want.

  1. The before asks the reader to do the believing. "Probably nothing" begs for reassurance, the rockets fake excitement, and five hashtags spray at nobody in particular. Every element is a costume.
  2. The after is facts a stranger can check. The thing, then the honest itch it scratches, in words you'd use out loud. Nobody owes the post a read; the first sentence earns the second.
  3. The image does the stopping. On every platform the picture stops the scroll, so capturing a good one is part of the session, and the link gets tested logged out before posting.

Anatomy of the first sentence

The workshop pushes your opening line into this shape:

I built a site that shows where the 42 bus actually is, live. busmap.example.dev
  • A The thing, first. No journey preamble, no "so I've been working on something".
  • B What it does, specifically enough that the right reader stops scrolling.
  • C The link, working, tested logged out. The post exists so this can be clicked.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Will my post sound like AI wrote it?

The skill treats machine tells as cuts on sight: "I'm excited to share", rocket emoji clusters, rhythmic feature lists. If it sounds like a press release it gets scrolled past like one. The standard is the read-aloud test, would you say this sentence to a classmate, and you write every word that survives it.

Which platform should I post on?

The one where people who'd care about your thing actually are, and where you already have any presence: X or Bluesky for code, Instagram or TikTok for visual and physical things, LinkedIn for professional stuff, Show HN if the project genuinely stands alone. One platform on day one; cross-posting can come later if it lands.

What if I get zero likes?

Silence is the most likely outcome for a first post, and the skill says so before you post. It means nothing about the work. The post now exists as the link you send when anyone asks what you've been doing, and it gets saved next to the project as part of its public record.

How often should I post?

One post per real event. Ship something, announce it, done. When the next real thing ships, run the skill again. No content calendar, no posting for the algorithm.

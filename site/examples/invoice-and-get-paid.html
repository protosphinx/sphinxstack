Scripted example of a real session.

Feels rude to ask for money you earned?

That feeling is the whole reason invoices exist. An invoice is a normal, boring document that states the agreed facts: what was done, what it costs, how to pay, and by when. Once it exists, "asking" becomes "sending a PDF with two polite sentences", and following up becomes re-sending it. The skill drafts every message in the ladder with you, and you send them from your own account.

What you end up with

A numbered one-page invoice, sent as a PDF named invoice-001-alvarez.pdf, with its due date tracked. Here is the whole thing.

Example
INVOICE 001
Date: Nov 3, 2026  ·  Due: Nov 17, 2026 (14 days) 1
FROM
Jordan Reyes  ·  (512) 555-0134  ·  jordan@example.com
TO
Maria Alvarez
WORK 2
Lawn mowing, front and back yard, Oct 26  ·  $30.00
Hedge trimming along driveway (agreed add-on, Oct 26)  ·  $10.00
TOTAL DUE: $40.00
HOW TO PAY 3
Cash, or check payable to Jordan Reyes.
Thank you! Questions: text or email above.
  1. Numbered and dated. Numbers start at 001; nobody needs to know it is your first. The due date is explicit ("due on receipt" also works), and it goes somewhere you will actually see it again.
  2. Every line was agreed beforehand. The hedge trimming appears because it was discussed on the day. An invoice is a restatement of the deal that already happened.
  3. Payment spelled out exactly. "Check payable to Jordan Reyes" leaves nothing to figure out. If it were an app, this line would read like "Venmo @handle". No tax fields unless you were told to add them; tax rules vary by place, so those questions go to IRS.gov and, for minors, a parent.

Anatomy of an invoice line

Every line of work the skill writes follows this shape:

Lawn mowing, front and back yard · Oct 26 · $30.00 · matches the price agreed before work started
  • A Plain description a stranger could understand. No jargon, no padding.
  • B When it happened, or quantity and hours where those are the unit.
  • C The price for that line, adding up to a total nobody has to hunt for.
  • D The rule behind the line: it restates the agreement. Surprise line items are how first clients become last clients.

Questions people actually ask

Do I need a business, a tax ID, or a logo to invoice?

No. Your name and contact, the client's name, the lines of work, a total, how to pay, and a due date. A logo is optional decoration. Tax fields only go on if you were told to add them; sales tax and self-employment tax rules vary by place and situation, so those questions go to IRS.gov and, if you are a minor, a parent or guardian.

What do I actually make the invoice in?

The fastest honest options: a Google Doc or Word invoice template, or a clean one-page HTML invoice built with the agent that prints to PDF. Free generators like Wave and Zoho's free tool exist too; compare them on their official sites rather than taking anyone's word for a pick. Either way the output is a PDF named like invoice-001-clientname.pdf.

How do I receive money if I'm under 18?

Cash on delivery and checks work at any age. Peer-to-peer apps (Venmo, PayPal, Zelle and similar) usually require a parent on the account for minors, and rules vary, so check the app's own terms and ask your bank with a parent. If you have no way to receive money yet, that conversation happens with a parent and the bank.

What if they just never pay?

The ladder runs: nudge the day after the due date, a direct follow-up a week later, and at two weeks a parent gets looped in and the plain question gets asked by phone. For a first small gig, that is usually as far as escalation sensibly goes. The durable lesson is to take half upfront next time on anything big.

Is this financial or tax advice?

No. The skill teaches what goes on an invoice and how to follow up politely; it says so plainly in the session. The agent never handles payments or logs into anything financial, and specific tax questions belong with IRS.gov, your bank, and for minors a parent or guardian.

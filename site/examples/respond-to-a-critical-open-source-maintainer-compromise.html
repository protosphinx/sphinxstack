Worked case: restore a project without sacrificing its people

Trigger: A widely used cryptographic library loses two maintainer accounts to a targeted takeover while one maintainer is being extorted, a malicious version reaches package registries and Linux distributions, release signing is compromised, project chat is impersonated, and downstream vendors demand an immediate safe release.

Can we demand public proof from the maintainer?
No. Use a verified private safety route and separate human protection from project authority.
Can the remaining account publish a fix?
Only after independently establishing source, governance, builder, registry, and signing trust.
Does removing the malicious version close impact?
No. Trace distributions, caches, installs, execution, credentials, and downstream products.
What proves recovery?
Protected maintainers, clean artifacts, precise advisories, consumer remediation, new trust, and anti-capture governance.

Project recovery cannot endanger a coerced maintainer.

Failure rehearsal

An emergency responder attempts permanent sole control. A bounded multiparty recovery role expires and full governance requires independent acceptance.

Questions people actually ask

Does account control prove authorship?

No.

Should extortion be public?

Only through safe authorized disclosure.

Can the same signing key recover?

No when its trust is compromised.

Who receives advisories?

Registries, distributors, vendors, and affected consumers.

When can normal governance resume?

After legitimate authority and human safety are independently accepted.

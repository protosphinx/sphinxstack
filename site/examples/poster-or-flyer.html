Won't plain type look boring?

On screen, up close, maybe. On a wall, from ten feet, by someone walking past, big plain type is the loud option. A poster gets about one second of a stranger's attention, and everything competing with the key line spends that second for you. The skill still gives you the choice: it proposes two rough layouts with a one-line trade-off each, and the thumbnail test settles the argument with evidence instead of opinion. Your pick wins, and only the winner gets refined.

  1. Three reading distances, three sizes. The hook is huge, the facts are medium, the detail is small. That order is the entire design.
  2. Date and place get the second-loudest slot. A curious passerby needs exactly these two lines to show up.
  3. The QR earns its space. It links to a real signup page and was tested by scanning the printed sheet, since screens scan more easily than paper.
  4. Specs stated so it reprints correctly. Anyone on the club can send this file to any printer next term and get the same flyer.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Do I need Canva or Figma?

No. The default build is one HTML page sized to the real sheet, exported by print-to-PDF with fonts embedded. If you'd rather work on a canvas, Figma or Canva free tiers both work; the skill uses whichever one you will be able to edit again later.

What size should I make it?

A4 or US Letter for flyers, A3 or 11x17 for posters, and the skill works at the real print size from the first draft. Designing at one size and scaling to another is where soft images and broken margins come from.

Can a print shop print it?

Yes. For a shop the skill adds 3mm bleed and keeps images at 300 DPI at print size; for a home or school printer, no bleed is needed. Either way the export is a PDF with fonts embedded, and one test page gets printed and checked before any bulk run.

Why insist on a printed test page?

Because paper is the medium and the screen is only a preview. The test page catches soft images, ink-heavy backgrounds, and type that is smaller than it looked, and the skill has you proofread the key facts out loud from the print, at distance, before you pay for fifty copies.

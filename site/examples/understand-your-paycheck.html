Scripted example of a real session.

Feel dumb asking why the check is small?

Nobody is born knowing what FICA is, and the stub uses abbreviations on purpose that nobody ever explains. The goal of this session is that you can explain every line yourself, in your own words, and walk into the payroll office with a short written list of real questions instead of a vague worry. Asking "what is deduction code MEAL" in week one reads as sharp, and catching an hours error early is exactly what payroll people expect new hires to do.

What you end up with

An annotated copy of your own stub, each line labeled in plain English you wrote, plus a questions list saved where you will find it at tax time.

  1. Check gross against your offer. Hours times rate, or the salary fraction. Errors here are rare but real, and week one is the cheapest time to catch one.
  2. Withholding is prepayment. The money went ahead to the IRS on your behalf. Whether filing gets any of it back depends on your real totals, which is a question for IRS.gov's own tools rather than for anyone's guess.
  3. Unrecognized deductions get chased. Anything beyond tax (meals, uniform, a retirement plan) must be something you agreed to. If you don't recognize it, it goes on the list.
  4. Year-to-date columns are free accounting. They answer "how much have I made and paid so far" with no spreadsheet at all.

Questions people actually ask

Will I get the withheld money back?

Maybe; it depends on your real totals for the year. Students earning little often end up owing nothing and getting withholding refunded after filing, but the skill will not promise a refund. IRS.gov's interactive "Do I need to file" tool answers it for your actual numbers, using the W-2 your employer must send by January 31.

What is a W-4 and did I fill it out wrong?

The W-4 is the form from your hiring paperwork that tells the employer how much federal tax to withhold. For a single person with one job and no dependents, the straightforward filling is usually correct. "Should I change it" is a question for the official Tax Withholding Estimator on IRS.gov, with your real numbers in it.

Does the agent log into my payroll portal?

No. You open the stub yourself, on paper or in the portal, and read the lines out. The session produces labels and questions; you keep control of every account.

My employer took out something I never agreed to. Now what?

It goes on the questions list, named by its deduction code, and the list goes to your employer's payroll contact or your manager. Deductions beyond tax must be things you agreed to, so "what is this code and did I opt in" is a completely normal question. Minors should loop in a parent or guardian too.

Is this tax advice?

No, and the skill says so whenever questions get specific. It will not compute your taxes or promise outcomes. It shows you what each line means and where the official answers live: IRS.gov, ssa.gov for FICA rates, your payroll contact, and for minors a parent or guardian.

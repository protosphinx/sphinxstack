Scripted example of a real session.

Everyone has an opinion about my major

Parents, friends, and viral articles all argue from vibes and one anecdote. This skill's rule is that every statistic on the sheet comes from a source opened during the session: Scorecard field-of-study data, the BLS handbook, a college's own outcomes page, a real catalog's degree requirements. Your interests go on the sheet with the same seriousness, because which classes you lose track of time in is evidence too. What the skill refuses to do is decide for you.

  1. Outcomes carry the spread, and what the major feeds. A single median hides both. When a major leads to many occupations, the sheet says so instead of pretending one salary number describes it.
  2. Upper-division titles are the test. The skill pulls the real sequence from a real catalog and reads the junior and senior titles with you, because that is where "I like the idea of this" gets confirmed or dies.
  3. Reactions are data. They go on the sheet in the student's words, including which reasons belong to other people, named as such.
  4. Close calls get an escape plan. If two majors stay close, the sheet records how long the decision can wait and which shared freshman courses keep both doors open.

Questions people actually ask

Will it write my essay or my career-class assignment about this?

No. Graded work stays yours, whatever the class. What the skill hands you is the evidence: a sheet of verified numbers, real course sequences, and your own recorded reactions, which is most of what such an assignment needs. If you draft it, the skill can annotate your draft.

Will it just tell me what to major in?

No, deliberately. It lays the evidence flat and names what it shows, and the choice is yours, because the skill's own rule says a major picked by someone else gets abandoned sophomore year.

Where do the numbers come from?

Sources opened during the session: College Scorecard's field-of-study data, the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, a college's own outcomes page, and the actual degree-requirements pages of real catalogs. No earnings figures from memory and no "everyone knows" claims.

What if I'm torn between two majors?

The sheet says so plainly, and then records the practical facts: many schools do not require declaring for a year or more, and some freshman courses count toward both paths. A close call with a known decision date beats a coin flip.

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

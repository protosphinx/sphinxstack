Worked case: move operational meaning, not only telemetry bytes

Trigger: A company must leave its observability vendor in four months, but thousands of producers, custom parsing, incompatible histograms, sampled traces, security searches, rarely used incident queries, dashboards, paging rules, retention duties, and source export limits make apparent data parity unreliable.

Can matching ingestion volume prove the migration?
No. Equal bytes can still produce different metric math, parsing, alert timing, access, retention, and investigation results.
Could we translate every dashboard automatically?
Translate the intended decision and validate its result. Syntax conversion can preserve a chart while changing its meaning.
Should both systems page during dual write?
Keep one authoritative route until target parity passes, and deduplicate any planned comparison notifications.
When can the old platform be deleted?
After history, legal duties, incident access, reconciliation, and the rehearsed rollback window are complete.

Validate decisions, not storage volume.

Failure rehearsal

Dual write shows matching request counts, but the target reconstructs a latency histogram with incompatible boundaries and pages two minutes late. The wave pauses, source paging stays authoritative, the query is redesigned from the service objective, and three past incidents are replayed. Cutover proceeds only after timing and values pass together.

Questions people actually ask

How long should dual write last?

Long enough to cover peaks, deploys, rare paths, known incidents, retention behavior, and target failures.

What is semantic equivalence?

The target preserves the intended meaning across types, units, aggregation, missing data, parsing, sampling, and time.

Can low-use queries be dropped?

Not before their incident, audit, security, and legal value is reviewed with owners.

Which platform should page?

Keep one authoritative route until target alert parity and routing are proven.

What blocks source retirement?

Unresolved evidence, retention, legal, export, access, reconciliation, or rollback obligations.

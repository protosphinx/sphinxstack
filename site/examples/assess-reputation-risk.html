Worked case: assess a controversial partnership

Trigger: A proposed partnership may create operational, labor, environmental, and public-trust harms, but evidence is incomplete and leaders want a single sentiment score to approve it.

The sentiment tool says the partnership is seventy-eight percent positive. Is that enough?
No. The sample may not represent affected stakeholders, and visibility is not the underlying risk. I need sourced facts, stakeholder expectations, harm scenarios, and decision triggers.
Can communications fix the downside after we sign?
Only some information gaps. Communications cannot repair unsafe labor, operational dependency, environmental harm, or a promise the contract prevents us from keeping.
What should the approval record contain?
A sourced risk assessment, response option record, conditions, owners, residual-risk authority, and an effectiveness review tied to stakeholder outcomes.

Reputation follows conduct and evidence, not a sentiment score.

The decision evidence package

Failure rehearsal

A positive public sample hides supplier workers, nearby residents, and operations teams who face most of the exposure. The assessment rejects the single score, expands stakeholder evidence, models correlated harm, compares contract changes and non-partnership options, and gives the effectiveness review authority to stop the arrangement.

Questions people actually ask

Is reputation risk the same as negative attention?

No. It concerns whether stakeholders have reason to trust future conduct; attention can reveal but does not define the harm.

Can sentiment data be used?

Yes, as a bounded sample with source, coverage, method, and limitations, not as a universal approval score.

Which stakeholders matter most?

Start with people exposed to the decision and legitimate expectations, including groups with less power or visibility.

What is a reputation control?

A credible control changes conduct, harm, evidence, accountability, or repair rather than merely changing the message.

When should the decision be revisited?

At predeclared triggers such as new evidence, control failure, repeated harm, changed exposure, or missed commitments.

Worked case: hire through lawful worker models in twelve countries

Trigger: A technology company wants one hiring process for employees, interns, employer-of-record staff, and contractors in twelve countries, but employing entities, worker classification, salary disclosures, work authorization, background checks, works-council duties, data transfers, accessibility, benefits, and offer terms differ while one ATS and two agencies handle every candidate.

Can we classify everyone outside our entities as contractors?
No. Review the actual relationship and use only a lawful, operationally ready worker model.
Should every country use the same background check?
No. Limit each check by local authority, job relevance, sequence, notice, and contestability.
Can monitoring demographics stay in recruiter view?
No. Separate optional monitoring from selection and protect small groups.
What proves a country is ready?
Verified worker model, fair process, approved offer, candidate-data controls, and a rehearsed worker handoff.

Failure rehearsal

An offer names a contractor, but the planned schedule, control, exclusivity, and tools match employment. Signature pauses, the classification owner reviews the actual relationship, and the hire waits until a lawful entity or alternative model is ready.

Questions people actually ask

Can one global policy replace local review?

No. It should define principles, ownership, and controlled local variation.

Are agencies responsible for candidate privacy?

They have duties, but the hiring organization must still govern purpose, access, transfer, retention, and incident handling.

Can unsupported locations hire informally?

No. Pause until a lawful, operationally ready model exists.

Should accommodations change the scored criterion?

No. They should provide an equitable way to demonstrate the same job-related outcome.

When does candidate data become worker data?

At a defined, purpose-limited handoff with appropriate access and retention controls.

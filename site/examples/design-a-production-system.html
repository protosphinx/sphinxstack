Worked case: design a launch-ready notification service

Trigger: We need an architecture for a service that will have real users and on-call support.

We need email and push notifications for account activity. Can you design the stack?
First I need the critical journey, delivery target, expected peak, sensitive data, and the failures support can tolerate.
Password-reset email is critical. Product messages can wait. Peak is estimated at 150 messages a second.
Then those are separate service classes. I will label the traffic estimate and design password resets around correctness and bounded delay.
Should this be microservices?
That is not a requirement. We will start with responsibilities and failure modes, then choose the smallest boundary that meets them.

A design review begins with constraints, not vendors.

Evidence set

  • System diagram: API, queue classes, worker, provider adapters, status store, and trust boundaries.
  • Trade-off record: one service now, with the condition that justifies a future split.
  • Failure walkthrough: provider outage, duplicate delivery, and bad deployment from signal to recovery.

Failure rehearsal

The email provider returns errors for ten minutes. The critical queue age alert fires, workers stop retrying aggressively, and the service changes to the approved second provider. The team checks one password-reset journey and the queue drain rate before closing the event. Product messages remain queued so they cannot consume the recovery capacity.

Questions people actually ask

Does a production design require microservices?

No. Service boundaries follow responsibilities, independent change, and failure isolation. One process is often the most supportable first design.

How accurate must the traffic estimate be?

It must be labeled and useful for a decision. Show the arithmetic, test the likely peak, and record what observation would change the design.

What belongs on the diagram?

Components, external actors, stores, data flow, dependencies, and trust boundaries. Leave out detail that does not affect a decision.

Who approves the architecture?

The person accountable for delivery and operation, with review from owners of security, data, and affected systems where needed.

When is the design finished?

When another engineer can implement the first slice and walk important failures from detection to recovery without inventing missing policy.

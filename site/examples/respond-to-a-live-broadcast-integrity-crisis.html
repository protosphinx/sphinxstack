Worked case: stop a compromised signal without losing the truth

Trigger: A global awards stream is hijacked through a remote graphics account while a synthetic presenter clip, false emergency caption, altered translation, and edited social excerpt spread before the clean program recording is recovered.

Should we delete the hijacked recording?
No. Stop distribution, restrict harmful access, and preserve every version in an independent evidence store.
Can we say the whole program was fake?
Only classify the sources, segments, signals, and derivatives that evidence supports.
What reaches the audience?
A versioned notice with known facts, unknowns, affected routes, corrections, and accessible safety guidance.
What proves recovery?
Clean identities and production paths, independently monitored output, acknowledged downstream corrections, and reconciled residual exposure.

Questions people actually ask

Should the compromised feed stay public?

Restrict harmful access while preserving controlled evidence.

Can a holding slate hide uncertainty?

No. State what is known and why normal programming stopped.

What about captions and translations?

Treat each as a separately versioned editorial route.

When can broadcasting resume?

After trusted sources, clean control, monitored output, and rehearsed stop paths pass.

Who reviews the response?

Independent editorial, security, safety, accessibility, labor, and governance reviewers.

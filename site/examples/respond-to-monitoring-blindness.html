Worked case: operate safely when green means unknown

Trigger: After a collector configuration deploy, regional dashboards remain green while request and error volumes collapse toward zero, one alert route stops paging, buffered telemetry may expire within hours, and operators cannot tell whether a customer-facing failure is hidden inside the monitoring outage.

The dashboard is green. Can deployments continue?
No. Near-zero demand and errors indicate unknown coverage, not health. Declare the blind spot and pause changes that require missing evidence.
How do we tell whether the service is also failing?
Use independent synthetics, direct bounded checks, infrastructure counts, business completions, provider status, and customer reports.
Should we replay every buffered alert after repair?
Not blindly. Backfill with known ordering and duplicates, then suppress uncontrolled paging while preserving incident evidence.
When can normal change resume?
After each affected region and critical signal passes end-to-end injection and independent service-state verification.

Unknown coverage is an operating condition.

Questions people actually ask

Does missing data mean zero?

No. Missing, delayed, partial, and zero data must remain distinct states.

Should every change freeze?

Restrict changes whose risk depends on missing evidence; allow only bounded, authorized recovery work.

What counts as alternate evidence?

Independent user journeys, infrastructure signals, business records, direct checks, provider status, and customer reports.

Can buffered telemetry be backfilled?

Only with understood ordering, duplication, retention, and downstream alert consequences.

How is restoration proven?

Inject controlled signals through producer, pipeline, query, dashboard, alert, and notification paths.

Why won't it just write the essay?

Because the essay goes out under your name to a committee that reads thousands of them, and schools check. The skill's line is hard: it never drafts sentences for the essay, not even the opening, not an example to rework. What it will do is quote your own spoken words back to you when you say something usable, propose cuts, and run the draft through structure, line, and read-aloud passes until it holds. The essay that survives is one you wrote and can talk about in any interview. If getting words down is the struggle, these build the muscle:

  1. Opens on a scene. The structure pass asks whether the draft opens on a scene rather than a thesis. Draft one opened with "Perseverance has always been important to me"; the line pass marked it as a sentence any applicant could write, and the writer cut it.
  2. Material only this writer could supply. The paper phone came out of the interview, in the writer's own words. The agent's contribution was pointing at the sentence and saying "that one".
  3. The ending answers the opening. The read-aloud pass checks that the ending lands somewhere the opening did not: this one ends on how the writer now explains things, which the crying scene set up without promising.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, and the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot can all run it in a chat.

Will it write any part of the essay?

No. Not the opening, not a sample paragraph to rework. Every kept sentence has to originate with you. The closest it comes is quoting your own spoken words back to you when you say something worth keeping, and proposing cuts to what you wrote.

My life is boring. What would I even write about?

The interview is built for exactly this. It hunts the specific over the impressive: something you did that nobody assigned, what you actually do with unscheduled time, a moment this year you still think about. "I like helping people" becomes usable when it turns into one afternoon and one person. If nothing real surfaces, the skill says the honest thing: go do something worth writing about first.

How many passes does a draft go through?

Four kinds: a structure pass (does it answer the prompt, does anything happen), a line pass (marking every sentence any applicant could have written), a read-aloud pass (you read it out loud and fix wherever you stumble), and mechanics last (word count, spelling, school names correct). A draft is done when your sentences have survived at least two of them.

Can colleges tell when AI writes an essay?

The skill assumes they can and refuses to create the problem. Committees read thousands of essays, schools run checks, and a caught essay costs the application. The workshop route removes the risk entirely, because there is nothing to catch: you wrote it.

Worked case: plan a support operation

Trigger: Demand is rising, service is slipping, and leadership wants a hiring number without separating nominal headcount from skill-constrained effective capacity.

Tickets grew 30 percent. Should we hire three people?
Not from that number alone. I need arrivals by work type, backlog, throughput, service targets, skills, schedules, shrinkage, quality, and hiring ramp.
We have ten agents, so that is 400 hours a week.
That is nominal time. Training, leave, meetings, escalations, support work, and skill coverage determine effective capacity.
Can we target full utilization until the backlog clears?
That removes recovery capacity and makes shocks unsafe. I will model staged options with sustainable load, service impact, cost, and triggers.

Headcount is an input, not a capacity conclusion.

Failure rehearsal

A forecast assumes every employee is interchangeable and available for all scheduled hours. The review stops the hiring recommendation when specialist coverage and leave make the service promise impossible. The team rebuilds effective capacity by skill and tests hiring, cross-training, demand shaping, and scope options before approval.

Questions people actually ask

Why not divide demand by average productivity?

Average productivity can hide skill constraints, variation, quality, interruption, and time-to-capability.

What is shrinkage?

It is scheduled time unavailable for direct demand, such as leave, training, meetings, maintenance, and support.

Should utilization be maximized?

No. Operations need recovery capacity for variation, incidents, learning, and safe work.

How should uncertain demand be handled?

Use explicit scenarios and staged triggers rather than one precise forecast.

When is the plan complete?

When the model reconciles, options are decision-ready, triggers have owners, and actuals will be reviewed.

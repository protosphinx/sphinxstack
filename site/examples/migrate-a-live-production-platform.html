Worked case: move a live network without double publishing

Trigger: A live events network must move remote contribution, switching, graphics, captions, ads, DRM, chat, analytics, players, recordings, and archives from a mixed legacy stack to a new production platform across venues and regions without double publishing, losing accessibility, or disrupting protected events.

Can both platforms publish during the rehearsal?
Not to the same public destination. Establish one fenced publication authority.
Is video and audio parity enough?
No. Verify captions, graphics, ads, rights, security, players, analytics, controls, recordings, and operators.
Should the first live test be a flagship event?
No. Start with synthetic and low-risk shows after production-shaped rehearsal.
What if rollback would interrupt a show already live?
Keep the authoritative live signal and recover the supporting planes around it.

Two paths may still share one failure.

Failure rehearsal

The new platform loses its caption vendor in one region while video remains healthy. The audience receives the approved accessible fallback, the affected destination stays on the verified path, recordings preserve the program, and migration pauses until caption failover passes under load.

Questions people actually ask

Are two encoders always redundant?

No. They may share power, network, account, source, or operator.

Can shadow output include real ads?

Only in an isolated destination that cannot create billing or audience effects.

Who decides cutback?

The named event authority using rehearsed stop conditions.

Must operators train on failures?

Yes. Normal use does not prove takeover and recovery.

When can legacy retire?

After events, configurations, recordings, credentials, contracts, and recovery gates pass.

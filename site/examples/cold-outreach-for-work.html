Worried it reads as spam?

It reads as spam when it could have been sent to anyone. The bar this skill holds is the opposite: the message ships only when it is specific enough that this one recipient is the only person who could have received it. Every claim in it must be true, the ask must be small, and the recipient's time gets respected: under 120 words, no attachments unless asked, one follow-up ever, public contact info only.

What you end up with

Five to ten targets, each with a true reason and one small ask, and the messages sent. Here is one that shipped.

Example
TO: hello@spokeandwheel.example.com
Subject: question from a student  ·  74 words  ·  sent June 12, one follow-up June 19 1
THE MESSAGE
Hi Mr. Alvarez,
I'm Theo, 16, a sophomore at the school up the hill. You've fixed my bike five times in two years, and I've watched you explain a bottom bracket to a customer like it was the most interesting thing in the world. 2
Do you ever take on part-time help? I'm free weekends, I learn fast, and I'd sweep floors to start. 3
No worries at all if not — I'll keep coming in either way. 4
Theo Nakamura  ·  theo@example.com
  1. The subject is plain and honest. "Question from a student" beats anything clever, because it is exactly what this is.
  2. Specificity only this recipient could receive. Five repairs, the bottom bracket moment. If a line could go to any other shop, it gets cut.
  3. One small ask a stranger can grant. Part-time help, or even just an introduction. Never "give me a job".
  4. The easy out lowers the cost of replying. A no is allowed, so a yes gets easier.

Anatomy of a cold message

Four parts, under 120 words, all of them true:

I'm Theo, 16, a sophomore at the school up the hill · you've fixed my bike five times and I've watched you explain a bottom bracket · do you ever take on part-time help? · no worries at all if not
  • A Who you are, one line. Age or school is fine; honesty about being young disarms.
  • B The specific true reason you wrote to this recipient. The line that proves it isn't a template.
  • C The one ask, sized to what a stranger can actually grant.
  • D The easy out. It costs nothing and reads as respect.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Will it send the messages for me?

No. Everything goes from your own accounts, under your own name, sent by you while the session is open. The agent drafts and workshops with you and never messages anyone as you.

Should I ask for a job or for advice?

Depends on the target. A small business gets the work ask, kept small. Someone whose career you admire gets the advice ask first: fifteen minutes of questions gets replies where "hire me" gets silence, and a chat with someone doing the work is a real prize.

How do I find their email?

Public sources only: the address on the website, the contact form, the counter of the shop. The skill will not scrape addresses or pass around personal numbers, and if no public channel exists, walking in is the honest alternative.

What if they never reply?

One follow-up about a week out, ever, and silence after that means done. That cap is a feature: it keeps you from becoming the person who emailed six times, and it points your energy at the next target.

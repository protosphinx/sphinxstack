Worked case: restore a subscription platform

Trigger: Backup jobs are green, but nobody has proved that they can restore a usable system.

Our database snapshots succeed every night. Can we close the backup audit?
Not yet. I need an isolated target, selected backup points, keys, application versions, reconciliation queries, and a safe plan for external side effects.
We can restore into staging, but staging sends email through the real provider.
Then restoration must block outbound email before application startup. A restore that contacts customers is not a safe test.
Can we just compare the row count?
Row count is one signal. We also need relationships, permissions, money totals, subscriptions, authentication, jobs, and critical user journeys.

A completed backup job is input evidence, not restore proof.

The restore evidence package

LayerVerificationFailure condition
Backup chainTimestamp, checksum, encryption key, and retentionMissing increment or unavailable key
DataCounts, money totals, foreign keys, and subscription stateUnexplained mismatch
ApplicationSign-in, read, write, billing preview, and scheduled jobCritical journey cannot complete

Required evidence

  • Backup inventory: selected points, artifacts, checksums, encryption, keys, retention, and expected recovery point.
  • Restore transcript: exact versions, commands, timings, warnings, failures, and operator decisions.
  • Reconciliation report: data invariants, permissions, application behavior, jobs, and critical user journeys.

Failure rehearsal

The team selects the newest eligible snapshot without checking it manually first. The restore stops when an incremental segment fails its checksum. Operators preserve the failed target and transcript, leave original backups immutable, and repeat only after the broken chain has an owned diagnosis. They do not quietly choose a different snapshot and report success.

Questions people actually ask

Does a green backup job prove recoverability?

No. It proves that the job reported success, not that artifacts, keys, dependencies, and application behavior can be restored.

How often should restores be tested?

Set frequency from data criticality, change rate, obligations, and recovery risk. Test again after material platform or backup changes.

Can the restore use production?

Only under an approved recovery event. Exercises should use an isolated target that cannot overwrite authority or trigger external side effects.

What should reconciliation include?

Include structural integrity, business invariants, permissions, application behavior, jobs, and representative user journeys.

When has the backup policy failed?

It has failed when selected backups, keys, time, data loss, or restored behavior do not meet the stated objectives.

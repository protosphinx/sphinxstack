Worked case: keep payroll and customer service safe during supplier collapse

Trigger: A critical vendor providing payroll, identity, cloud hosting, customer messaging, and regional logistics suffers a cyber incident while financial distress limits staffing, subcontractors stop work, data exports are incomplete, some transactions are pending, contractual notices conflict, and the next payroll plus customer delivery cycle is hours away.

Should every service move to an alternate now?
Only after capacity, compatibility, authority, data, safety, security, and in-flight ownership are proven.
Can both suppliers process pending items?
No. Assign every payroll, order, message, payment, and record to one processing owner.
What if insolvency threatens our data?
Activate legal, escrow, continuity, evidence, and access measures while protecting people and service.
What closes the incident?
Reconciled obligations, stable continuity, chosen recovery or exit, closed access and assets, and tested remediation.

One owner must control every in-flight action.

Failure rehearsal

An alternate payroll provider accepts a file while the failed vendor's bank submission remains indeterminate. The team stops the alternate release, traces bank status by employee and payment, protects wages through a controlled continuity path, and prevents duplicate pay.

Questions people actually ask

Can a contract cure notice wait?

Track contractual deadlines in parallel with immediate people and service protection.

Should vendor access be revoked immediately?

Restrict proportionately while preserving evidence, safe continuity, and recovery authority.

Can a spreadsheet export prove data recovery?

No. Verify completeness, schema, history, integrity, permissions, and downstream usability.

When should customers be told?

Communicate verified impact, protective action, service state, and update timing.

Can the vendor return after recovery?

Only after trustworthy state, control remediation, obligations, and a new risk decision are proven.

Worked case: protect candidates from a convincing fake employer

Trigger: Candidates receive job offers from a lookalike careers domain after convincing video interviews, then are asked to pay for equipment and upload identity documents, while fake postings copy real roles, a recruiter mailbox has an unexplained forwarding rule, payment destinations span countries, and nobody knows how many people were contacted.

Should we wait until we know every victim before warning people?
No. Publish confirmed indicators and safe contact routes now, while stating uncertainty.
Can we coordinate through the recruiter's mailbox?
Not until its access and forwarding state are trusted.
Should takedowns happen before evidence capture?
Preserve the minimum lawful evidence first unless immediate harm requires faster removal.
What does recovery include?
Trusted recruiting systems, specific victim support, corrected public channels, monitoring, and reconciled open harms.

People, not brand optics, come first.

Failure rehearsal

A platform removes a fake post before preserving its application form, but candidate emails retain links and receipts. The evidence ledger records the loss, alternate sources establish exposure, the verified warning remains active, and victim outreach does not overclaim complete scope.

Questions people actually ask

Should victims contact the suspected recruiter?

No. Use a separately verified employer channel.

Is copied public content proof of an account compromise?

No. Preserve both copy and access hypotheses until evidence resolves them.

What if identity documents were uploaded?

Provide specific identity and account protection support based on the actual documents and jurisdiction.

Can the employer promise all fake posts are gone?

Only if monitoring supports that claim; otherwise state confirmed removals and uncertainty.

When can the incident close?

After system trust, victim outcomes, duties, public corrections, and recurrence controls reconcile.

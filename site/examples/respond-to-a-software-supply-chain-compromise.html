Worked case: rebuild trust after a malicious release

Trigger: A popular library discovers a maintainer token theft, malicious release, mutable CI action, poisoned shared cache, stolen signing key, and auto-update distribution into developer tools and production services.

Can we publish a patched build now?
Only after source, builder, inputs, caches, signing, artifact differences, and consumer scope are independently trusted.
Does removing the package end exposure?
No. Trace execution, credentials, deployments, downstream builds, data, and persistent effects.
Can the old signing key announce recovery?
Use an independently authenticated channel and establish new signing trust.
What proves recovery?
Clean provenance, compared artifacts, precise advisories, adopted replacements, revoked trust, rotated secrets, and monitoring.

Recovery cannot reuse the compromised trust path.

Failure rehearsal

The rebuild matches source but uses the poisoned shared cache. The team discards it, rebuilds with empty controlled caches, and analyzes differences before release.

Questions people actually ask

Can telemetry prove no execution?

No. State sampling and coverage limits.

Should all releases be revoked?

Scope by independently reconstructed provenance and exposure.

Can a checksum prove safety?

It proves identity, not benign behavior or trusted origin.

Who receives an advisory?

Affected consumers and downstream distributors need actionable verified guidance.

When can releases resume?

After clean trust, artifact comparison, independent approval, and protective guidance.

Worked case: consolidate a global publishing estate

Trigger: A global organization must move public sites, authenticated help, policies, product documentation, media, downloads, forms, search, structured data, localized pages, feeds, newsletters, and app content from several legacy CMS platforms to one operation while URLs, rights, accessibility, workflows, scheduled releases, user permissions, and regional obligations differ.

Can we bulk import everything and fix gaps later?
No. First inventory stable identities, owners, rights, URLs, locales, access, workflows, releases, and dependencies.
How do we avoid overwriting live edits?
Use immutable transforms, delta capture, one publication authority, and explicit target-edit ownership.
Can redirects be sampled?
Sample for exploration, then reconcile the complete in-scope URL and canonical set.
What is the cutover gate?
Proven content, rights, accessibility, links, search, access, workflow, release, monitoring, and rollback parity.

Failure rehearsal

A regional team edits a policy in the target while a legacy delta arrives. The migration stops that item, preserves both versions, resolves publication authority, records the chosen merge, and reruns parity without silent loss.

Questions people actually ask

Can old URLs be retired?

Only with verified disposition, redirects where appropriate, and preserved legal or archival obligations.

Does visual similarity prove parity?

No. Verify semantics, accessibility, rights, access, workflows, search, forms, and metadata.

Who may publish during cutover?

One explicit authority per content item and phase must control publication.

Can translations inherit the source decision?

Track each locale's accuracy, rights, obligations, and release state separately.

When can legacy systems close?

After retention, access, evidence, rollback, integrations, and ownership are discharged.

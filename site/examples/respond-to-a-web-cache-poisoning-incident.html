Worked case: remove attacker-controlled cache variants

Trigger: Users in several regions receive attacker-controlled redirects and headers from a shared web cache only for certain languages and devices, while origin responses appear clean, cache keys and normalization are undocumented, multiple CDN layers are involved, purge results disagree, and authenticated pages may share cacheable fragments.

The origin looks clean. Can we call this a false alarm?
No. Capture affected edge variants and trace every cache layer before drawing that conclusion.
Should we purge everything immediately?
Preserve minimum safe evidence first when possible, then bypass affected shared caching and purge every dependent layer.
One region now returns the right page. Are we restored?
No. Verify cold and warm variants, regions, providers, protocols, and authenticated isolation independently.
What if bypassing the CDN overloads origin?
Shed noncritical load or serve a verified static safe response while keeping the poisoned path disabled.

A purge receipt is not restoration evidence.

Failure rehearsal

The outer CDN reports a successful purge while its shield tier still serves a poisoned language variant. Bypass remains active, the shield key and dependent objects are enumerated, each tier is purged, and canary restoration waits for independently verified hashes and headers.

Questions people actually ask

Why preserve a poisoned response?

A controlled sample can prove the affected variant and cause, but do not spread or retain more harmful data than necessary.

Does a clean origin prove users are safe?

No. Intermediate and stored representations can remain poisoned.

Is a global purge sufficient?

No. Verify every tier, key family, region, and dependent object.

What if authenticated fragments were cached?

Treat possible cross-user delivery as a confidentiality incident and preserve access evidence.

When can shared caching resume?

After reviewed key repairs and independent canary checks prove correct variants and isolation.

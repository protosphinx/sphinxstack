Scripted example of a real session.

Do I really have to ask people first?

Yes, every time, and the ask happens during the sitting, in your own words. A reference who gets a surprise call fumbles it; one who agreed, knows which job to expect a call about, and approved exactly how they are listed gives the answer that gets you hired. The skill also refuses guessed emails, managers who have since left, and relatives presented as employers, because employers check all three.

What you end up with

A one-page document styled to match your resume, listing people who said yes. It stays in your pocket and goes out only when an employer asks.

  1. Header matches the resume. Same name, city, email, phone, same fonts, so the two documents read as a set when they land in the same inbox.
  2. Title and organization as the reference approved them. Marcus confirmed "Aquatics Manager" is his current title. Nothing here is guessed.
  3. Relationship in plain words. "My shift supervisor, summer 2026" tells the caller exactly why this person's opinion counts. A lawn client is a real reference when the relationship is stated honestly.

Each entry answers the caller's questions before they dial:

  • B The organization, so the caller knows the context before dialing.
  • C The relationship in plain words. This is what makes the entry credible.
  • D Contact details exactly as the reference chose to be reached.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, and the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot can all run it in a chat.

Should "references available upon request" go on my resume?

No. The skill's rule is that the phrase earns its line nowhere. References live in this separate document, and it goes out only when an employer actually asks, which is usually late in the process.

Can I use a family member if they employed me?

No. Friends and family are out, including relatives presented as employers, because a checked reference that turns out to be your aunt costs you the job. The neighbor you mowed lawns for, the parent you babysat for, the teacher who graded you for two years: those all count, and most people have more of them than they think.

I can only think of one person. Now what?

Start with that one: get the yes, list them properly, and keep building. If the posting requires three and you genuinely have one, the fix is the same as a thin resume: do something an adult witnesses, then come back. The skill will say this plainly rather than pad the list with weak names.

When do I actually send the document?

Only when asked. Then two more pieces of etiquette the skill drills: tell each reference which job to expect a call about, and thank them afterward whether or not you got it. People who are warned give better answers, and people who are thanked say yes next time.

Worked case: recover high-value accounts without excluding legitimate users

Trigger: A global financial platform redesigns recovery for users who lost devices while attackers use SIM swaps, compromised email, stolen identity documents, help-desk social engineering, and coerced access, and legitimate users include displaced people, changed names, disabled users, survivors, enterprise administrators, and accounts holding high-value assets.

Can a government ID settle recovery?
Not alone. Assess channel integrity, account history, document risk, current threats, and proportionate independent review.
Should recovered accounts withdraw immediately?
High-risk cases need risk-based cooling, notices, holds, and stepped reauthorization.
What if the usual phone is monitored by an abuser?
Use a protected contact path and controls that do not alert the adversary.
How do we judge quality?
Measure both takeover and wrongful exclusion across risk, disability, language, connectivity, and support paths.

Failure rehearsal

A recovery passes document verification but the email and phone were recently changed, the device is new, and a large withdrawal follows. The platform fences the recovered session, blocks the high-risk action, preserves evidence, routes independent review through a safe contact path, and reverses recovery-created access if compromise is confirmed.

Questions people actually ask

Should recovery be instant?

Speed must be proportional to risk and cannot replace safe identity and asset controls.

Can support override the flow?

Exceptional recovery requires stronger independent authority, evidence, delay, and audit.

Are biometrics required?

No. Minimize sensitive proof and provide lawful accessible alternatives.

What happens to old sessions?

Review or revoke them under a new session epoch and reauthorize high-risk capabilities.

How are wrongful denials found?

Monitor appeals, abandonment, support, accessibility, language, and calibrated decision outcomes.

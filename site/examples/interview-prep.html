Never done an interview and terrified?

The fear mostly comes from never having said the answers out loud. So that is the whole method: mock rounds under mild pressure, in character for this specific employer, with blunt notes between rounds. Not "good job", but which answer rambled, what got mumbled, which story fell flat. Every answer stays true; if you want to inflate one, the skill refuses and digs for the real story underneath, which is almost always better anyway.

What you end up with

An answer bank of true stories mapped to likely questions, and a day-of checklist. Both are yours to keep for every future interview. Here is the bank after a session.

  1. Rounds happen before notes harden. Two full mock rounds minimum, one curveball included, two or three fixes per round. The stopping point is when answers sound like you on a good day; a memorized script that sounds dead gets flagged too.
  2. One good story covers three or four questions. The bank maps each story to the questions it honestly answers, so fifteen likely questions need only four or five stories.
  3. "Any questions for us?" gets real ones. Drafted together, actually wanting the answers. Coming up empty there reads as not caring.

Anatomy of an answer that lands

Every story in the bank gets shaped to this, kept under a minute:

I ran the ticket table at our school fair · a customer started yelling about a refund · I stayed calm, explained the policy, offered an exchange · he took it, and the teacher asked me back next year
  • A Situation, one line. Just enough to place the listener.
  • B The problem, stated plainly. This is what makes it a story.
  • C What you did. The only part that is actually about you, so it gets the detail.
  • D How it ended. The part first-timers always forget to say; land it and stop.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

What if I genuinely have no stories?

The session mines for them: school projects, sports, family responsibilities, informal jobs, anything you have built or handled. Everyone who has finished school has enough material; it arrives unsorted, and sorting it is the first half of the prep.

Will it just give me answers to memorize?

No, on purpose. Memorized answers sound dead in the room, and the skill flags that as a failure mode. It shapes your true stories with you and rehearses until the telling sounds like you on a good day.

Does the prep change by job type?

Yes, and generic prep is wasted prep. A grocery interview gets customer scenarios, an internship panel gets "walk me through this project", a trades intake gets reliability and safety. The fifteen likely questions are written for your actual interviewer.

Can we rehearse out loud?

If your agent supports voice, run the mock rounds by voice; it is the closest thing to the real room. Typed rounds still work, and the day-of checklist and answer bank come out the same either way.

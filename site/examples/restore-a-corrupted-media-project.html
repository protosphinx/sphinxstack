Worked case: recover an edit without overwriting the evidence

Trigger: A documentary project stops opening after a drive error, the newest autosaves disagree, source files and proxies share names, two plugins are unavailable, backups have unknown dates, the review export contains edits missing from the last readable timeline, and delivery is due in three days.

Can we keep opening files until one works?
No. Stop writes, image or copy the failing storage, hash the evidence, and work only from a protected duplicate.
Which autosave should become the master?
Preserve all candidates and compare timestamps, timeline content, review exports, logs, and editorial authority before choosing.
Could we replace the missing plugins silently?
No. Reconstruct dependencies and record every substitution plus its audible or visual difference.
What proves the project is restored?
Source relinking, timeline comparison, sync, effects, color, audio, captions, full export, and a clean-system open.

Preserve first, reconstruct second.

Questions people actually ask

Should the damaged drive be repaired first?

No. Preserve a read-only image or verified copy before any changing operation.

Can a review export rebuild an edit?

It can support reconstruction when combined with source-traceable logs and editorial approval.

Are proxies acceptable sources?

Only as documented substitutes when originals cannot be recovered and delivery permits them.

What if plugins are unavailable?

Preserve settings, test approved replacements, and document every resulting difference.

When can damaged evidence be discarded?

Only after authorized closeout, verified archive, and retention needs are satisfied.

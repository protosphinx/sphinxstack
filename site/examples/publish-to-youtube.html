What if nobody watches it?

Early numbers will be small, and the skill says so up front instead of promising growth. What the first week actually tells you: the impressions click-through rate says whether the title and thumbnail promise is landing (under about 2% points at the packaging), and the retention graph says where people leave (a cliff in the first 30 seconds means the opening). Views are the least useful number on the page. The skill also refuses the shortcuts: no sub4sub, no view-buying, no engagement pods, because they misrepresent your audience and get channels flagged. The loop that works is reading retention, fixing the next video, and uploading again.

Questions people actually ask

Do I need some subscriber count before any of this matters?

No. The upload fields work the same on day one. The single unlock to know about: verifying the account by phone number enables custom thumbnails if the option is missing.

Which numbers should I actually check, and when?

Two, after a week: impressions click-through rate, which tests the title and thumbnail promise, and average view duration with the retention graph, which shows where people leave. The skill teaches you to read both.

Is sub4sub or buying views ever worth it early on?

No. Both misrepresent your audience to the recommendation system and can get channels flagged. The loop that works is reading retention, fixing the next video, and uploading again.

Do tags matter?

Little, and they cost nothing: the topic and its plain synonyms, done. Tagging trending topics the video is not about helps nobody and reads as spam.

Should I publish public, scheduled, or unlisted?

Public, or scheduled for a time your people are awake. Unlisted is for resume links and private shares. A launch on unlisted is a launch nobody can find.

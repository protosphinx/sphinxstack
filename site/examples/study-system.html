I always abandon systems by week two

Most study systems die because they were written for an imaginary week. This one starts by asking about your job shifts, your practices, and what is honestly already behind, and only then places blocks. The blocks are sized to how each course is actually graded, catch-up time is scheduled instead of hoped for, and when the conversation continues the next week, whatever you skipped tells the skill what to resize. A week that exists on your real calendar, built around your real life. Here is one, one week into the term it was made for.

  1. Capture the same day. One spot per course where everything lands the day it happens. Plain text files work; a paper notebook with a page per lecture works. Boring tools survive.
  2. Soccer nights get twenty minutes. The skill asked about fixed commitments first and sized these blocks to fit. A plan you flinch at will be skipped, so it gets fixed before the week starts.
  3. Chem gets the most time. It carries the heaviest grading weight and it is the course that is behind. Catch-up is a named block on the calendar instead of a guilty feeling.
  4. The week ends by planning the next one. Whatever got skipped this week is the input for resizing next week.

Questions people actually ask

Will it do my homework or write my essays?

No. The skill's first rule says your coursework stays yours. Paste a graded assignment and ask for the answer and it declines, then offers what it does instead: explain the concept, quiz you on it, or review an attempt you made first. It holds this even when you push.

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Do I need a flashcard app or special tools?

No. The skill keeps the tools boring on purpose: a folder of text files, a paper notebook, a dated checklist, the calendar you already check. If you already like a flashcard app, it uses that; nothing new is required.

I'm already three weeks behind. Is it too late?

Being behind is an input, and the skill asks about it directly. The weekly plan includes named catch-up blocks for whatever you said is behind, sized to fit the hours you really have. What it won't do is pretend the backlog isn't there.

What happens when I skip blocks?

The skill expects it. If the conversation continues the next week, what got skipped is treated as information: a block you skip twice was the wrong size or the wrong time, and it gets resized instead of rescheduled on hope.

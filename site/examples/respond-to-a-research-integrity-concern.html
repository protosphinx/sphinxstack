Worked case: preserve evidence under conflicted authority

Trigger: A junior researcher reports manipulated microscopy images, duplicated data, disputed authorship, and retaliation by a principal investigator who controls the evidence systems, while logs will expire before a sponsor deadline and institutional leaders want the figures replaced informally.

Should we ask the principal investigator to explain before copying the server?
Not when evidence may disappear. Use lawful independent preservation before notice.
Can the figures simply be replaced?
No. Bound every allegation, affected output, participant risk, sponsor duty, and required correction.
Does a disputed authorship claim prove misconduct?
No. Test it against the applicable standard and complete records while protecting everyone from retaliation.
What closes the case?
Fair findings, corrected outputs, protected people, verified remediation, review rights, and approved closeout.

Allegations are not findings.

Failure rehearsal

The normal research office reports to an implicated leader. The case is routed to an independent institutional authority, sponsor, and protected channel as policy permits. Evidence custody and interim student protections continue without announcing a conclusion.

Questions people actually ask

Is an honest error misconduct?

Not automatically. Apply the governing definition, evidence, and required mental-state standard.

May the respondent see the evidence?

Provide fair notice and response opportunity while protecting reporters, witnesses, privacy, and evidence integrity.

Can confidentiality be guaranteed?

No. Explain need-to-know, policy, legal, and reporting limits accurately.

What if several institutions are involved?

Coordinate authority, custody, reporting, and affected outputs without abandoning uncovered scope.

When are interim actions appropriate?

When authorized and needed to prevent continuing harm, without treating them as a finding.

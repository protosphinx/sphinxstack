Why not just let the AI rewrite it?

Because the rewrite isn't the deliverable, the skill is. A pasted-in fix leaves you unable to defend a single sentence in an interview, and it leaves the next draft exactly as hard as this one. Readers have also learned to discount machine-flavored writing on sight, so the safest voice you can submit is your own, tightened. After a few passes you run the sweeps solo and bring only the hard calls back.

What you end up with

The edited draft, submitted, plus the pass that produced it. Here is one paragraph through all four sweeps.

Example
BEFORE, 78 WORDS 1
I think that one of the things that makes me sort of unique is the fact that I am able to teach myself new things. For example, there was a situation where improvements were made to my bus tracking project by me over the course of a summer. I just really feel that this demonstrates my passion for technology and my commitment to seamless continuous learning and growth as a developer.
AFTER, 34 WORDS 2
I teach myself what I need. Last summer my bus tracker showed wrong arrival times, so I learned enough statistics to fix the estimate. Version three has been right within two minutes since August.
  1. The before is hedges holding hands. "I think", "sort of", "the fact that", "just really feel": cut in sweep one with no information lost. "Improvements were made by me" hides the actor, and "seamless continuous learning" is brochure language nobody says out loud.
  2. The after fronts the point and proves it. Claim first, then the story with an actual fix and an actual number. Every verb is doing work: teach, showed, learned, fix. Less than half the length, and it's the version that survives an interviewer asking "tell me more about that".

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Will the edited version sound like AI wrote it?

No, for a structural reason: every changed word is typed by you. The read-aloud sweep exists partly to catch machine-sounding writing, rhythmic triple lists, mirror sentences, words like "seamless" that no one says. If a sentence sounds like a brochure, you rewrite it as speech. What comes out is your voice with the padding removed.

What kind of draft should I bring?

A real one with a real destination: an application answer, a post, an email, a README. "Better" means better for that reader, so the skill asks who reads it before anything gets touched. Practice text with no destination teaches much less, and the draft must go out at the end.

How much will actually get cut?

In the deletion sweep, losing around a quarter of the draft is normal for a first pass, and it doesn't mean the draft was broken. Warm-up openings, things said twice, and hedges account for most of it. Nothing with information in it goes.

Do I have to read it out loud? Actually out loud?

Yes, actually out loud, and it's the sweep people skip and then regret. Every stumble and breath-run-out marks a spot the eye forgave. It's also the most reliable machine-tell detector you own.

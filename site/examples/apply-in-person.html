Anatomy of the counter script

Thirty seconds, four moves, all true:

Hi, is a manager available? · I'm Sam, I live nearby and I'm looking for work — I'm free weekends and two evenings · here's my resume · is the store hiring at the moment?
  • A Ask for the decision maker first. If they're out: leave the resume, get the manager's name and usual hours.
  • B Name, plus the two facts a manager cares about: you're local and your true availability.
  • C The resume goes into their hands. Paper still works here.
  • D The direct question. If the answer is no: thanks, leave the resume anyway, ask if that might change.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Do people actually still get jobs this way?

Yes, especially at local shops, cafes, and restaurants, where many openings never get posted anywhere. A manager meets a polite local kid with a resume in hand and a real availability answer, and that is the whole interview some places need.

What if they say they're not hiring?

That round gets rehearsed on purpose. The reply is thanks, leave the resume anyway, and ask whether that might change. Resumes left on a no have turned into calls a month later, which is why the visit still goes in the log with a follow-up date.

Can the agent call the shop for me first?

No. It never calls or messages a business pretending to be you. It preps the route, the script, and the rehearsal; the walking and talking are yours.

What do I wear?

Clean and plain, one step neater than the store's own staff. The session covers this in the day-of prep, along with printed resumes and a pen for on-the-spot forms.

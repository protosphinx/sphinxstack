Worked case: move global work without losing local obligations

Trigger: A multinational company is moving finance, support, procurement, technology operations, compliance, and customer administration from country teams into regional hubs and global shared services while entities, works councils, unions, languages, licenses, tax, data residency, vendors, systems, peak periods, and customer obligations differ.

Can we standardize every country on one process?
Define global standards and explicit local variations from legal, customer, language, control, and operating evidence.
Should easy countries pilot first?
Include representative complexity, demand, local exceptions, and peak periods or the pilot proves little.
What transfers at the handoff?
Open work, decisions, customers, payments, contracts, records, access, knowledge, obligations, and one accountable service state.
What blocks retirement?
Any role, system, vendor, process, meeting, access, or local duty without a proven durable owner.

The operating model is how work and decisions actually move.

Failure rehearsal

A regional hub meets average demand but fails month-end volume and a country license requires local approval. The program stops that wave, keeps the country process authoritative, restores capacity, updates decision rights, reruns the peak simulation, and consults the qualified local owner.

Questions people actually ask

Is an organization chart enough?

No. Map decisions, processes, interfaces, capacity, systems, controls, and outcomes.

Can consultation happen after design?

Follow applicable participation and consultation duties at the required stage.

Should all local exceptions disappear?

No. Keep justified local variation with authority and review.

How are hidden workloads found?

Observe actual work, exceptions, peaks, workarounds, handoffs, and unpaid coordination.

When are benefits credible?

After service, people, controls, cost, customer, and risk outcomes stabilize.

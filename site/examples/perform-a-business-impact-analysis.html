Worked case: prioritize continuity recovery

Trigger: Leaders need recovery priorities across public safety, revenue, customer records, suppliers, facilities, and scarce specialists, but interviews conflict and proposed recovery objectives ignore shared dependencies.

Every department marked its system critical within one hour. Can we rank by revenue?
Not safely. I will define critical outcomes, preserve sourced estimates, model time-dependent harm, and map shared recovery dependencies.
The customer database owner promises a thirty-minute recovery.
That is a candidate objective, not demonstrated capability. We must test identity, network, keys, replicas, specialists, data loss, and minimum-service paths.
What should leadership approve?
Dependency-tested objectives, a recovery priority record, minimum service, backlog recovery, funded gaps, and explicit acceptance of unresolved risk.

Criticality depends on outcomes and time, not volume of advocacy.

Failure rehearsal

The highest-ranked application restores on schedule but cannot authenticate users because the shared identity service and hardware-key custodian were ranked later. The team revises dependency-tested objectives, exercises a minimum-service path, and updates the recovery priority record with the scarce specialist and backlog constraints.

Questions people actually ask

Is a business impact analysis a system ranking?

No. It analyzes time-dependent harm to critical outcomes and the dependencies needed to sustain or restore them.

Who sets recovery objectives?

Accountable business, service, risk, and obligation owners approve candidates that operational evidence shows are credible.

Can interview estimates be used?

Yes, if they retain source, assumptions, range, confidence, and a plan to validate consequential claims.

What is minimum service?

It is the smallest safe, authorized outcome that can reduce harm while full recovery continues.

When is the analysis complete?

When priorities and objectives are dependency-tested and unresolved gaps are funded or accepted by named authority.

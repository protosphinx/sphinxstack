Worked case: rotate a privileged production credential

Trigger: A broadly privileged production credential appeared in source history and build logs, but its consumers, active sessions, downstream credentials, and safe revocation order are incomplete.

The string is gone from the latest commit. Can we close the alert?
No. Repository cleanup does not revoke authority. I will preserve evidence, bound the exposure, inventory consumers, and plan a sequenced cutover.
Can we revoke it immediately and see what breaks?
We will contain quickly, but an unplanned revocation could disable critical recovery paths. I need the issuer, enforcement points, dependencies, and incident authority.
What proves the rotation is finished?
Consumer cutover evidence, old-secret rejection proof, healthy scoped journeys, session revocation, and a residual-copy search tied to the exposure record.

Removing a copy is not revoking a credential.

Failure rehearsal

Revocation breaks an undocumented settlement worker while a copied session remains valid. Responders preserve containment, invoke the approved fallback, move the worker to the independent replacement, revoke sessions at the identity authority, and repeat old-secret rejection proof before closing the incident.

Questions people actually ask

Is deleting the leaked value enough?

No. Revoke the authority, replace consumers, terminate relevant sessions, and account for residual copies.

Should the old and new secrets overlap?

Only when an authorized, time-bounded overlap is safer and monitored during a controlled cutover.

Do we rewrite repository history?

Only through a coordinated decision that preserves incident evidence and accounts for every downstream clone and artifact.

What if a consumer is unknown?

Keep the incident open, use telemetry to discover use, and choose containment with the accountable service and incident owners.

When is rotation complete?

When replacement paths work, old authority and sessions fail, service checks pass, and residual risk has an owner.

Where to go from here

Assess scope with Triage a security alert, reduce future exposure with Manage secrets safely, and record remaining risk with Document a security exception.

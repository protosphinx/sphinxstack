Scripted example of a real session.

My list is nine people. What's the point?

Sending to nine real readers is a real send, and at that size you get the thing big lists lose: replies. The skill has you answer every one personally, because at small scale replies are both the reward and the growth engine. A newsletter is a promise kept on a schedule, and the only way to learn the craft is to send. If there's no list at all yet, the session sets one up on a free tier and seeds it with a few real people who said yes.

What you end up with

One issue, sent to the real list. Here is the skeleton of an issue that works.

Example
SUBJECT 1
Three transit APIs that looked free and one that actually is
CORE PIECE 2
I spent two days looking for a transit API that wouldn't ask for a credit card. Found three dead ends and one winner, and the winner was hiding behind the worst documentation of the four.
...the actual API names, the actual signup links, the actual rate limit that matters, told as the story of the hunt...
TWO SHORT ITEMS 3
A city-data catalog I keep going back to  ·  link, one line on why
Update: the bus map now guesses arrivals. Rush hour guesses still bad.
ENDING 4
What API paywall have you hit? Reply and I'll dig for a free route around it next issue.
  1. The subject says what's inside. Written last, picked from five candidates, most concrete one wins. No clickbait: the open a trick buys costs a reader who trusted you.
  2. One core piece, known first-hand. Written to one named reader, with checkable specifics. One real link and one real number beat three paragraphs of reflection.
  3. Secondary items stay small. Two at most, each a link plus one honest line of why. More sections would thin all of them.
  4. The ending invites a reply. A question readers can answer, or a plain "see you next issue." No triple-flourish sign-off paragraph.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat. Buttondown and Substack both have free tiers that cover a small list.

Will my newsletter sound like AI wrote it?

Inbox readers delete on the first whiff of template, so the edit pass hunts exactly those tells: "In this issue we'll explore...", rhythmic lists, empty intensifiers. You write every line to one named reader, and anything you wouldn't say to that person gets rewritten by you. That's also just what good newsletter voice is.

I don't have a list yet. Can I still use this?

Yes, that's a supported starting point. The session sets up a free tier on Buttondown or Substack, whichever you can see yourself using, and seeds the list with a few real people who said yes: friends, family, a teacher. Sending to five real readers is a real send.

How long should an issue be?

Shorter than the reader expected. One core piece, at most two short secondary items, an ending that invites a reply. The edit pass cuts every sentence that adds no information, which usually shrinks a draft noticeably.

What if I can't keep a schedule?

The skill closes by making the schedule concrete: next issue's topic and date written at the top of a new draft before the laptop closes. A newsletter is only a newsletter after issue two, so the real commitment is one more issue, on a date you just picked.

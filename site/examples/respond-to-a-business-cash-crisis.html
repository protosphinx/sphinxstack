Worked case: protect payroll without misusing restricted funds

Trigger: A company has payroll due in twelve days, a major customer is late, a lender covenant is near breach, tax and critical supplier payments are due, inventory orders are noncancelable, and the founder wants to delay wages and move restricted client funds into operations.

Can we use the client balance temporarily and replace it after collection?
No. Exclude restricted funds unless qualified advisers establish explicit lawful authority for that use.
Should we delay payroll before paying suppliers?
Do not improvise priorities. Build the obligation register and obtain employment, tax, insolvency, and contractual authority.
The customer says payment is coming this week.
Record the evidence, earliest realistic date, and downside case. A promise is not cleared cash.
What must happen today?
Verify unrestricted cash, freeze unapproved commitments, rank lawful actions, contact advisers and the lender, and set daily decision triggers.

Protect restricted funds and preserve decision options.

Failure rehearsal

The expected customer payment misses its promised date and the lender has not approved a waiver. The daily model crosses its payroll threshold. The team does not move restricted funds or quietly delay wages. It triggers qualified restructuring advice, executes already approved reversible actions, updates affected stakeholders with verified facts, and preserves every decision and approval.

Questions people actually ask

What counts as available cash?

Verified bank and settlement balances that are legally and operationally unrestricted, net of known holds and required reserves.

Can management choose which legal obligations to delay?

Only within documented authority informed by qualified jurisdiction-specific advice.

How often should the forecast update?

Daily, or more often when a receipt, payment, facility, covenant, or protection threshold can change the decision.

Should customers and workers be told immediately?

Use role-specific, timely communications based on confirmed facts, legal duties, and the harm created by delay or speculation.

When does a cash crisis require insolvency advice?

As soon as the facts may trigger director duties, inability to pay obligations, creditor harm, or loss of viable options.

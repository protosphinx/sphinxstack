We have no idea what we can afford

Most families don't, which is why cost gets equal billing with admissions from the first minute of this skill. Sticker prices are nearly useless; the number that matters comes from each school's own net price calculator run with your family's real figures, and that is the number the sheet records. What you end up with

A comparison sheet where every row is a school you would genuinely attend and every number was looked up during the session. Here is a draft mid-build, with fictional schools standing in.

Meets full demonstrated need per its aid page · net if admitted $9,800/yr
  1. Net price is the only price on the sheet. This school's sticker is more than double this figure; the sheet carries the calculator's output from the family's real numbers, because that is what the year would cost.
  2. Safeties earn the label twice. Once on the numbers, once on the question "would you actually go?" A safety that fails the second test gets replaced, not tolerated.
  3. Over-budget rows get flagged in the open. This one gets resolved before the list is final: either aid closes the gap or the school comes off.
  4. Under about 20% acceptance is a reach for anyone, whatever the numbers say. The label protects you from treating a lottery as a plan.

Questions people actually ask

Will it write my application essays?

No. Essays and personal statements are yours to write; the skill will annotate a draft you wrote and tell you honestly where it loses the reader. What it produces is the list itself: verified numbers, honest labels, and cost math your family can look at.

Where do the numbers come from?

From sources opened during your session: each school's own admissions pages, its Common Data Set, its net price calculator, and the federal College Scorecard. The skill refuses to quote acceptance rates or prices from memory because they drift every year.

How many schools should end up on the list?

Eight to twelve, shaped roughly as 2–3 safeties you would happily attend, 4–5 matches, and 2–3 reaches. Every school has to pass the same test regardless of label: you would actually go.

What if my parents won't talk about money?

The skill still asks you to find out what the family can pay, because every net price calculation depends on it. It can help you plan that conversation, but it will not build the sheet on a guess; a list priced on hope collapses in April.

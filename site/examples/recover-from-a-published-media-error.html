Worked case: correct a misidentified person across every version

Trigger: A documentary excerpt identifies the wrong person and a translated caption changes the allegation after the clip has been embedded, syndicated, downloaded, cached, promoted, and adapted into accessible and social versions, while the affected person reports escalating harm.

Can we silently replace the main video?
No. Preserve the error, assess harm, issue a visible correction, and trace every version and destination.
Should we repeat the allegation in every notice?
Only the minimum needed to identify and correct the error without amplifying harm.
Can we promise the downloads are gone?
No. Record recall attempts and residual copies honestly.
What else must change?
Masters, captions, transcript, description, translations, graphics, metadata, social edits, partner files, and review controls.

Failure rehearsal

A syndication partner cannot replace its downloaded copy immediately. The primary source and accessible versions are corrected, the partner publishes a linked notice, the affected person receives an honest status, and the residual copy stays open in the reconciliation until verified.

Questions people actually ask

Should the original error be deleted?

Preserve restricted evidence while removing or correcting public harm.

Is an edited timestamp enough notice?

No. A material error needs a clear dated correction.

Who contacts the affected person?

A qualified safe contact owner with accurate limits and support.

Must accessible versions be corrected?

Yes. Captions, transcripts, description, and translations are part of the publication.

When can recovery close?

After sources, versions, distribution, harm support, residual reach, and controls reconcile.

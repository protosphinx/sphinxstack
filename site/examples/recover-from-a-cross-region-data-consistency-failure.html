Worked case: reconstruct one authoritative business history

Trigger: A network partition and automated failover leave two regions accepting orders, payments, inventory reservations, account changes, and refunds under different clocks; replication later overwrites some newer records while external payment and shipping systems retain both effects.

Can latest timestamp win?
No. Reconstruct causal and operation order from epochs, versions, sequences, transactions, and durable external outcomes.
Can replication resume first?
Fence writes and preserve both histories before automated overwrite continues.
What about duplicate charges?
Reconcile the payment provider before choosing internal state and customer repair.
What proves restart?
A new reconciled generation, atomic authority, stale-epoch rejection, corrected externals, canary writes, and rollback.

Wall-clock recency is not causal authority.

Questions people actually ask

Can last write win?

Only when domain semantics explicitly make it safe.

Should both regions go read-only?

Fence the smallest safe domain while protecting evidence.

Can counts reconcile?

Counts do not prove entity or outcome correctness.

What about clocks?

Record them but use causal evidence.

When can failback start?

After authority, replication, invariants, and rollback pass.

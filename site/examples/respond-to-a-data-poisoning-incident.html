Worked case: contain poisoned inputs and repair affected decisions

Trigger: A fraud and eligibility platform discovers that an upstream partner feed was compromised, labels were altered through a feedback tool, suspect records entered training and test sets, shared features reached several models, and deployed decisions have affected customers for weeks.

Should we retrain the latest model without the partner feed?
First quarantine suspect generations and trace every record, feature, model, metric, decision, and downstream copy.
Can a good offline score clear the rebuild?
No. Validate provenance, label integrity, independent evaluation, subgroup outcomes, decision impact, and adversarial resistance.
What happens to past customers?
Protect, notify, appeal, reverse, or re-evaluate through accountable domain processes while preserving original evidence.
What blocks automation?
Unknown trusted data, unresolved descendants, invalid labels, unsafe decisions, or failed authenticity and behavior tests.

A clean rebuild must include decision remediation.

Failure rehearsal

The team cannot prove which feedback labels were altered because logs rolled over. It keeps all descendant model generations quarantined, expands the affected population, moves high-impact decisions to qualified review, rebuilds from a verifiable earlier boundary, and records residual uncertainty.

Questions people actually ask

Can poisoned rows simply be deleted?

Only after descendants, joins, features, models, metrics, and decisions are traced.

Should the source vendor investigate alone?

No. Preserve independent evidence and verify claims against your own lineage and outcomes.

Do test-set records matter?

Yes. Poisoned evaluation can hide unsafe models or create false confidence.

Can old decisions be overwritten?

No. Preserve originals and link reviewed corrections or remediation.

When can automation resume?

After trusted provenance, rebuild, independent evaluation, decision protection, and monitoring pass.

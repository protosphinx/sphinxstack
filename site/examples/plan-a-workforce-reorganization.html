Worked case: test a rushed headcount target

Trigger: Leadership wants to remove fifteen percent of roles in six weeks using manager rankings after an automation forecast, but work demand, critical skills, contractor dependencies, jurisdictional duties, disability accommodations, redeployment options, service continuity, and the claimed savings are unreconciled.

Finance needs names next week. Can managers rank their teams now and we validate later?
No. Names cannot precede a legitimate design, reconciled work model, alternatives, lawful process, and fair selection controls. Manager ranking alone invites inconsistency and hidden bias.
The automation forecast says fifteen percent of work disappears.
Treat that as a hypothesis. Map tasks, adoption, exceptions, supervision, error recovery, new work, skills, contractors, and minimum service before converting it into role impact.
Could we announce consultation after the board approves the target?
Not if decisions are already fixed where meaningful consultation is required. Preserve real alternatives, criteria, evidence, influence, and time for representation.
What should leadership approve first?
The problem, evidence, option comparison, process, authorities, continuity limits, and decision gates, not a hidden list of people.

Start with work and outcomes, not a percentage and names.

Failure rehearsal

A proposed team removal appears cheap until the model exposes one contractor dependency, two regulated duties, unassigned accessibility work, and no coverage for a critical approval. The option is paused, work is redesigned, redeployment and training are tested, savings are recalculated, and continuity is exercised before any people decision.

Questions people actually ask

Can leadership set a savings target?

Yes, but the route must still be evidence-based, lawful, fair, and honest about service and implementation costs.

Can performance ratings select roles?

Not automatically. Criteria must link to future work, use reliable comparable evidence, handle accommodations and conflicts, and receive qualified review.

What counts as an alternative?

Process repair, demand reduction, redeployment, retraining, vacancies, attrition, phased change, technology redesign, and other viable options.

When should consultation happen?

Through the applicable process while meaningful options can still be considered and changed.

How are savings verified?

Reconcile implementation, contractors, severance, vacancies, productivity, risk, service, and actual recurring cost after transition.

Where to go from here

Model demand with Plan capacity and staffing, test service consequences with Perform a business impact analysis, and sequence implementation with Plan a change freeze.

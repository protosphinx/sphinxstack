Anatomy of a good short answer

Forms that ask "why do you want this job" get this shape, never boilerplate:

I shop here every week, so I know the store · I want steady weekend work while I'm in school · I can start right away · I've kept the same babysitting job two years
  • A A true, specific connection to this place. If you can't name one, say what genuinely draws you.
  • B What you actually want, plainly. Employers hiring entry level expect exactly this.
  • C The practical facts they are scanning for: start date, hours.
  • D One piece of evidence you are reliable. True and small beats grand and vague.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Will it fill in and submit the form for me?

No. It preps every answer so the form holds no surprises, and you type or paste and you click send. Anything submitted under your name should have passed through your hands.

What if I don't meet a requirement?

The posting gets read together first, and the hard requirements get checked against your reality before anything else. A genuine blocker means moving to the next opening instead of wasting the application. A soft gap gets the honest answer plus what you are doing about it.

The site made me create an account. Is that normal?

Common, unfortunately. Many employers use application systems that force an account. You create it with your own email and password, and the login goes in your log so the callback six weeks later isn't a mystery.

How many applications per sitting?

At least one fully submitted with the confirmation seen. Quality of the prep matters more than the count, and a logged, tailored application beats three rushed ones.

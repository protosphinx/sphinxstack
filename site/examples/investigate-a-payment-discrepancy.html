Worked case: trace a diverted supplier payment

Trigger: A large supplier payment went to a changed bank account after an email request, but approval, beneficiary, settlement, ledger, access, and recovery evidence conflict while another payment is queued.

The supplier says it was not paid. Should I ask the person who emailed the new bank details?
No. Preserve the message and verify the supplier through an independently known contact. I will open a restricted chronology and pause only the queued payment through an authorized owner.
Can finance reverse the ledger entry now?
Not until the bank, settlement, beneficiary, invoice, and ledger states are reconciled. A premature edit could corrupt evidence without recovering funds.
What about the mailbox that sent the approval?
Preserve its logs and map the access boundary before resets. Security can then contain access without erasing the trail.
When is this closed?
Only after authorized recovery action, accounting correction, access repair, affected-party communication, and recurrence monitoring are verified.

Preserve first, reconcile across systems, then recover with authority.

Failure rehearsal

A well-meaning administrator resets the mailbox and deletes forwarding rules before logs are preserved. The case lead stops further changes, records the evidence loss, preserves remaining provider and identity logs, narrows conclusions, and requires independent approval for recovery decisions.

Questions people actually ask

Should we contact the sender of the new bank details?

No. Verify through a previously trusted channel so the suspected channel cannot control the answer.

Can we accuse an employee or supplier?

No. Record facts and hypotheses, protect due process, and leave findings to authorized investigators.

Should the next payment be released?

Pause it through authorized controls until identity, beneficiary, and instructions are independently verified.

Can accounting correct the entry immediately?

Preserve the original state and reconcile settlement before making an approved, traceable correction.

What proves recovery?

Verified fund disposition, reconciled records, repaired access and controls, appropriate communication, and monitored recurrence.

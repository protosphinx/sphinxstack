What you end up with

Under 250 words, three paragraphs, every sentence true and in the person's own voice. Here is a finished one. The reader spends thirty seconds on it, and thirty seconds is enough when the letter is this direct.

Example
JORDAN AVILA
jordan@example.com  ·  (555) 014-2276  ·  March 4
Maplewood Public Library — Library Assistant (part-time) 1
I'm applying for the library assistant role because I already spend most of my afternoons at Maplewood, and I would rather be useful there than just occupy a chair. 2
Your posting asks for someone organized and comfortable helping people. Last spring I volunteered on the return cart: I reshelved about two hundred books a week, kept the holds shelf in order, and walked patrons to the sections they couldn't find. My homeroom teacher also has me manage our class supply closet, which stays stocked because I track it in a spreadsheet. I'm comfortable with the catalog computers; I use them weekly as a patron. 3
I'd welcome the chance to interview, and I can work weekends. Thank you for reading. 4
Jordan Avila
  1. Company and role named exactly. The skill checks both twice: a letter with the wrong library name on it is a rejection with extra steps.
  2. An honest opening. "I already spend my afternoons there" survived the workshop because it is true and specific. The manufactured version ("I have long admired your institution") would have been flagged and cut.
  3. The evidence paragraph. Each claim maps to a requirement in the posting and each is checkable: a named volunteer role, a real weekly number, a task a reference could confirm. This paragraph is the whole argument.
  4. One closing sentence. It asks for the interview and adds one useful fact. No restated resume, no closing flourish.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, and the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot can all run it in a chat.

Will it write the letter for me?

No. You write every sentence that makes a claim about you. The agent proposes the three-paragraph structure, interviews you for the honest opening, and offers line edits you accept or reject individually. Handing you paragraphs to sign is against its rules.

What if I don't match the posting's requirements?

The skill says so instead of papering over it. A cover letter cannot fix a resume gap; if nothing in your experience answers the posting, the honest fix is building something real first, and the skill points you at projects that produce checkable evidence.

How long should a cover letter be?

Under 250 words, three paragraphs: one sentence on why you are applying, one paragraph connecting your real experience to the posting's needs, one closing sentence asking for the interview. The reader gives it thirty seconds either way.

Do I need a new letter for every job?

Yes, when a posting asks for one, because the letter is built from that posting's actual requirements. Reusing a letter means sending claims aimed at a different job. The session takes one sitting, and the file is named for the company so versions never mix.

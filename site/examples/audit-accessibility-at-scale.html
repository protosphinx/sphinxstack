Worked case: audit a large digital portfolio

Trigger: An organization wants a portfolio-wide accessibility claim from automated scans even though it has hundreds of templates, authenticated journeys, documents, custom widgets, sparse ownership, and unresolved user complaints.

The scanner reports ninety-seven percent, so can we claim conformance?
No. The denominator and coverage are unclear, automation misses many barriers, and complaints contradict a broad claim. I need an inventory, risk-based sample, manual tests, and user evidence.
Can we test only high-traffic public pages?
Traffic informs sampling but cannot exclude authenticated, low-volume, safety-critical, document, or complaint-linked journeys.
How do we prove repair?
Retest the exact barrier and representative instances of its root pattern, then monitor regression and provide alternative access while repair remains open.

Automated coverage is evidence, not conformance.

Failure rehearsal

An automated fix removes duplicate IDs but leaves an authenticated approval flow unusable by keyboard and screen reader. The team reopens the barrier, provides an alternative route, expands the component-level sample, and requires manual and participant-informed verification before closure.

Questions people actually ask

Can automation prove accessibility?

No. It finds some machine-detectable failures and needs manual and user-centered evidence.

How should pages be sampled?

Cover templates, components, tasks, states, risk, variation, recent change, and known complaints.

Are low-traffic journeys lower priority?

Not when they carry high consequence, legal duty, essential access, or severe barriers.

What closes a finding?

Exact retest plus representative root-pattern checks and regression evidence.

What happens while repair is open?

Provide a usable alternative, clear support, an owner, and a dated remediation path.

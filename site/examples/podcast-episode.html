This skill is designed for exactly that problem: make one episode, from recording to publication, ideally in one long sitting. What you end up with

One published episode playing on your own phone from a real RSS feed. Here is the episode sheet from the session above.

Example
EPISODE SHEET — "THREE STORIES FROM MY GRANDMOTHER"
Format: interview  ·  Target: 10–20 min  ·  Two phones, clap sync
BEATS, ON PAPER 1
1. Open: "I've been promising to record my grandmother for two months. Today I finally did."
2. The crossing: how she got here, what she packed, what she left
3. The bakery: the first morning, the best regular
4. First winter: the coat story
5. Close: what surprised me, and she gets the last word
EDIT 2
Noise reduction from room tone · restarts cut at the clap marks · awkward first minute trimmed · "um"s kept · Loudness Normalization to −16 LUFS · MP3 at 160 kbps
PUBLISH 3
Host: free tier with a real RSS feed, account created by you, your credentials
Title says what it is, so a stranger learns something before pressing play
Cover art: 3000x3000, show name readable at thumbnail size
Check: RSS URL opened in a browser, feed added to the phone's podcast app
  1. Beats, never a script. Five lines you glance at, so the episode stays a conversation. A script read aloud sounds like a script read aloud.
  2. Edited lightly on purpose. The skill chases restarts and dead ends, and leaves the person in. Sanding out every hesitation removes the reason anyone listens.
  3. The feed is the finish line. Directories can take hours to list a new show, but the RSS feed is live the moment you publish, and hearing it play on your own phone is the proof.

Questions people actually ask

Do I need a mic or a mixer?

No. A phone per person a hand-span away, in a quiet soft room, is enough for a first episode. The record-clean-audio skill covers the whole setup, including levels and room tone.

How do I record someone who isn't in the room?

Record the video call, and ask the guest to also record a voice memo on their own phone as backup. Free remote-recording tiers exist too. For a first episode, the call recording is usually fine.

Where does it get hosted, and is that free?

Free hosts with real RSS feeds exist: Spotify for Creators, RSS.com's free tier, or Acast free. You create the account yourself; the agent walks you through the fields and stays away from your credentials.

Should I edit out every um and pause?

No. Cut the restarts, the dead ends, and the awkward first minute, and keep natural pauses. A first episode that sounds like a person beats one sanded to nothing.

How long until it shows up in Spotify or Apple Podcasts?

The RSS feed itself is live immediately, and you can add it to any podcast app by URL right away. Directory listings can take hours or a few days to appear.

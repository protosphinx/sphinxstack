Scared they'll pull the offer if you ask?

A polite, single, honest ask delivered with visible enthusiasm does not sour a first job. What sours things: bluffing. The skill will not script an invented competing offer or a fake deadline, ever; if you ask for a bluff it refuses and builds the honest ask instead. And it will tell you plainly when the strongest move is no ask at all, because accepting a fair offer cleanly is just as respectable as negotiating one.

What you end up with

The offer written down with its gaps found, the numbers checked against real local rates, and a response scripted in your words. Here is the offer sheet from that session.

REALITY CHECK 3
Minimum wage that applies to me: checked this week  ·  current local cashier postings: $13–$15 range  ·  a 16-hour week at $13.50 ≈ $216 before deductions, no travel cost (bike)
MY ONE ASK, SCRIPTED 4
"Thank you so much — I'm excited to accept. One thing: I can't work past 9 on school nights. Can the schedule hold that? Either way, I want the job."
IF THEY SAY YES
Accept, then confirm wage, hours, and start date in writing by replying to the offer message · close out the tracker · thank-you note to Priya, who referred me
  1. Everything gets written down. Spoken offers evaporate. The first move is capturing every term, including the vague ones.
  2. "Up to" is not a number. Undefined terms become questions to ask before accepting, and asking them is normal.
  3. "Is this okay pay?" gets a number. The applicable minimum wage plus current local postings for this role, checked this week, and what a typical week actually pays after costs.
  4. One ask, with the true reason attached. Schedule is the lever that realistically moves at entry level. The script leads with enthusiasm and ends with wanting the job regardless of the answer.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Will asking really not lose me the job?

A single respectful ask, wrapped in real enthusiasm and ending with "I want the job either way", is a normal part of accepting work. What the skill guards against is the version that does cause damage: demands, lists of asks, and bluffs.

Should I say I have another offer to get more?

Only if it exists. The skill never bluffs: no invented competing offers, no fake deadlines. If you ask it to script one it refuses and builds the honest ask instead, because a bluff that gets called ends worse than a low wage.

Is it weak to just accept?

No. If the offer is fair, at posted rate, for your first job, taking it cleanly and being excellent for three months is often the strongest play, and the skill says so plainly. A review-in-90-days conversation is also often the easiest yes to get.

What can someone with no leverage actually ask for?

Schedule is the big one: protected school hours, guaranteed minimum hours, specific days. Start date moves too. A wage bump is realistic only with something concrete and true behind it, like a certification, direct experience, or the offer sitting below going rates you can point to.

Worked case: protect candidates after recruiting data exposure

Trigger: An applicant-tracking vendor exposes resumes, interview notes, disability accommodations, reference contacts, background-check links, salary history, and rejection scores through a stolen recruiter session and public export URL across several countries.

Should we wait for the vendor’s final report?
No. Contain access, preserve evidence, and protect candidates where verified or plausible harm is immediate.
Do access logs define the whole breach?
No. Reconcile public-link behavior, downloads, caches, recipients, vendor gaps, and each data field.
Can hiring continue?
Quarantine decisions that used compromised data or scores until independent review.
What proves recovery?
Protected candidates, scoped exposure, corrected identity and decisions, safe notices, repaired vendor controls, and monitoring.

Recruiting continuity never outranks candidate protection.

Failure rehearsal

A candidate’s accommodation could expose a disability. The team uses a safe private channel and avoids repeating the sensitive field in broad notice.

Questions people actually ask

Does no download log prove safety?

No.

Should references be told?

Notify affected contacts through verified bounded guidance.

Can breached scores be reused?

Only after independent integrity and fairness review.

What if the vendor is silent?

Escalate and state uncertainty.

When is recovery complete?

After candidate and decision remediation plus verified controls.

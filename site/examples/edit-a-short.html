But my footage is boring

Raw footage always looks boring, because it contains all the waiting. The skill works with your footage only: nothing pulled from elsewhere, no stock clips pretending to be yours, no "wait for it" caption over nothing. It finds the two seconds that actually work, opens with them, and cuts the rest hard. If the footage really is thin, the honest answer is a shorter video, and these projects exist to give you something worth filming.

Anatomy of the first two seconds

The skill builds the opening to this standard:

motion already happening at frame one · a caption that raises a true question · subject inside the safe zone · cut before the moment resolves
  • A No intro, no logo, no "hey guys". The thing is mid-action when the video starts.
  • B "Took all afternoon to get this" is true and makes you want the payoff. Invented stakes are banned.
  • C Captions and platform UI eat the bottom quarter and the right edge, so the action lives above and left of them.
  • D The grab finishes at the end of the video. The opening only promises it.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free. The editors it uses are too: CapCut on phone or desktop, or DaVinci Resolve's free tier. The edit matters more than the editor.

Can it add stock clips or footage from other videos?

No. The skill cuts your footage only. Stock b-roll pretending to be yours is exactly the kind of faking it refuses. If your footage is thin, it makes a shorter video.

What about music?

Music comes from the platform's own licensed library, added at upload time, or from genuinely royalty-free sources. The skill will not rip a track, because that gets videos muted or taken down.

My video is horizontal. Is it ruined?

No. The skill crops to the action for a 9:16 frame, or stacks a punch-in when one crop cannot hold the subject. Most phone footage survives this better than people expect.

Do captions really matter on a 40-second video?

Yes. Most short-form video plays with sound off, so anything spoken gets captioned, auto-generated then fixed word by word. The captions-and-subtitles skill goes deeper if you want it.

Feels like homework about homework?

A tracker earns its keep the first time it catches an overdue follow-up or stops you applying to the same store twice. It stays small on purpose: one sheet, no tabs, in the tool you already use, opened twice a week for five minutes. Rejections and silence get logged too, because a full tracker is a record of effort, and watching the rows accumulate is what keeps a long hunt going.

What you end up with

One sheet that runs the whole hunt. Here is a tracker mid-hunt, sorted so the red rows float to the top.

  1. One row per application, whatever the channel. Online, walk-in, referral, and cold message all live on the same sheet, so nothing has a second home to rot in.
  2. Red is the to-do list. A conditional-format rule turns past-due follow-up dates red. Open the sheet, act on the red, done.
  3. Notes hold the human details. Who referred you, what the manager said, which login the application system forced you to create.
  4. Closed rows stay. Four closed, five active is a normal-looking hunt, and seeing the effort on one screen beats the sinking feeling of not knowing.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Do I need a special app?

No. A spreadsheet in whatever you already use is the right answer for almost everyone, because it syncs to your phone and costs nothing. A tiny local web app is on offer only if you are technical and actually want one; the tracker must stay smaller than the job hunt.

What statuses should I use?

Seven cover everything: to apply, applied, followed up, interview, offer, rejected, no answer. A dropdown keeps them consistent, and color by status makes the sheet readable at a glance.

Should I log rejections and silence?

Yes, deliberately. A full tracker is a record of effort, and a hunt with closed rows on it looks like what a real hunt looks like. Marking a silent application closed is a decision, and making it on purpose beats letting it haunt you.

I already sent a bunch of applications untracked. Too late?

That is the expected starting state. The session reconstructs the history from memory and your sent email, enters every row, and assigns each open item a real follow-up date. Ten minutes of archaeology and the hunt is on one screen.

Worked case: coordinate a citywide festival

Trigger: A three-day city festival spans four venues and an online stream when severe weather, a rail shutdown, an access failure, a missing child report, a medical surge, supplier delays, and false social posts create simultaneous decisions under fragmented authority.

The festival director wants every decision to wait for them so messaging stays consistent.
That creates a dangerous bottleneck. The command structure needs delegated primary and alternate authority for safety, safeguarding, access, venue, medical, communications, and operational decisions.
Should the command chat include every report so everyone sees the same picture?
No. The common operating picture needs decision-relevant sourced status, while personal, medical, safeguarding, and security detail stays in restricted channels.
The rail issue and weather may strand one venue while a missing child case is open.
Triage the safeguarding case through its qualified lead, model venue time-to-harm and transport capacity, and record each decision, owner, communication, and review time in one live log.
When can the command center close?
After every incident, participant need, supplier handoff, public commitment, evidence item, welfare concern, and corrective action has an accepted disposition.

Shared awareness does not mean uncontrolled disclosure or centralized delay.

Failure rehearsal

The festival director becomes unreachable as weather closes one site, radio traffic fails, and false evacuation instructions spread online. Alternate authorities use backup channels, venue safety leads control immediate action, communications correct only verified facts, and the next shift accepts every open decision through a timed handoff.

Questions people actually ask

Is the command center above emergency services?

No. It coordinates event operations and defers immediately where emergency or venue safety authority applies.

Should every team see every incident detail?

No. Share the minimum decision-relevant status and restrict personal, medical, safeguarding, and security information.

Who can pause the event?

Named primary and alternate authorities should have pre-agreed thresholds and must not wait for a single unavailable executive.

How often should status meetings run?

Use short cycles matched to event tempo without delaying urgent response or overwhelming operators.

What closes an incident?

Verified participant and service disposition, evidence, communication, follow-up ownership, and accepted residual risk.

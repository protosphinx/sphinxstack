Evidence set

  • Versioned cases: inputs, allowed context, expected properties, source, and risk tag.
  • Scorecard: baseline and candidate by slice, plus safety, latency, cost, and variance.
  • Release decision: hold the candidate until policy accuracy recovers; owner and disable condition recorded.

Failure rehearsal

Reviewers discover that half the policy cases were used while editing the prompt. Those cases are marked contaminated and removed from the release comparison. The candidate is rerun on the held-out set, and the earlier result remains stored with a warning rather than quietly replaced.

Questions people actually ask

How large should the evaluation set be?

Large enough to cover important capabilities and failure slices, but small enough to run and inspect regularly. Add evidence as the product learns.

Can one score summarize the result?

No. Averages can hide severe regressions. Report blocking measures and meaningful slices alongside any overall number.

May a model judge another model?

Yes for some scaled review after calibration against humans. Report disagreement and keep human authority for high-impact judgments.

What is the baseline?

The current production version or manual process that the candidate must improve without violating release thresholds.

When should a production failure join the set?

After privacy review, add it to a protected regression set with its source, expected behavior, and risk label.

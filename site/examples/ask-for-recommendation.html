What if no teacher actually knows me?

The skill ranks recommenders by one question: who has seen you at your best, specifically? One concrete story is enough, and most people have more witnesses than they think (a coach, a shift supervisor, a club advisor all count when the application allows it). If truly no adult has seen you do anything, the skill says so plainly: that is the real gap, and a few weeks of doing something visible fixes it.

What you end up with

Two things leave the sitting: the ask, sent, and a one-page brag sheet each recommender keeps. Here is the brag sheet. Every line on it is a fact the recommender could verify, because they will sign their name to a letter built from it.

Example
BRAG SHEET — for Ms. Okafor
from Devon Park  ·  devon@example.com  ·  AP Chemistry, period 3
WHAT THE LETTER IS FOR 1
State university engineering applications. Due Nov 1. Submitted through the Common App invite you'll get by email this week.
MOMENTS YOU SAW 2
The titration lab report — I rewrote it three times after your first-draft comments until it earned the A. You told me most students stop at one rewrite.
Lab partner rescue, March — when my partner was out for two weeks, I ran our bench alone and caught the two of us up on the missed labs after school.
RESULTS IN YOUR CLASS
A- both semesters  ·  4 on the AP practice exam in April
WHAT I'M APPLYING TOWARD 3
Chemical engineering. Your class is where I found out I like the version of chemistry with math in it.
Resume attached.
  1. Logistics up top. The deadline and the submission mechanics are the part busy recommenders lose track of, so the sheet leads with them.
  2. Specific moments the recommender witnessed. The skill interviews for these and writes down only what the person confirms. Two concrete stories give a letter its paragraphs; adjectives give it nothing.
  3. One honest sentence, no essay. The "why" is there so the letter can point somewhere, in words the recommender will recognize as the student's.

Anatomy of the ask

The person drafts the ask themselves; the skill workshops it until it covers four things:

I'm applying to engineering programs this fall and your class is where I did my best work, so I'd love a letter from you. It would be due Nov 1. Totally fine to say no if this is a busy stretch.
  • A What they are applying for, plainly.
  • B Why this person specifically. A real reason, because the recommender will wonder.
  • C The deadline, so yes means an informed yes.
  • D The easy exit. A reluctant yes writes a flat letter; the exit makes the yes real.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, and the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot can all run it in a chat.

Will it write the ask for me?

No. It suggests what the ask needs to cover, you write the actual message, and it gives feedback until the message works. A teacher who knows you can tell when the words are not yours, and that costs you the warmth the letter needed.

The deadline is next week. Too late?

Tight but workable. The skill's rule is to say so honestly and move straight to sending today: pick the recommender with the most to say, get the brag sheet in their hands the same day, and offer to answer questions fast. Letters take time; a week is the minimum courtesy, and you give them all of it.

What if they say no?

Then the easy exit you built into the ask did its job, and you move to the next person on your ranked list. A no now beats a lukewarm letter later. This is why the skill has you list every adult who has watched you do something before choosing.

What actually goes on a brag sheet?

Only verifiable facts: what the letter is for and when it is due, two or three specific moments the recommender personally witnessed, your grades or results in that class or job if they are strong, and one honest sentence about what you are applying toward. An inflated sheet burns the recommender's credibility along with yours, so the skill writes down only what you confirm is true.

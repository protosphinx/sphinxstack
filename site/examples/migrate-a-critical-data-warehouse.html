Worked case: migrate a warehouse without silent historical drift

Trigger: A multinational company must move finance, product, risk, customer, and machine-learning data from a legacy warehouse to a new platform while batch and streaming ingestion continue, transformations and semantic metrics change engines, dashboards and regulatory extracts share tables, regions impose residency rules, and both warehouses must overlap without double ingestion or silent historical drift.

Can every consumer switch after row counts match?
No. Reconcile keys, values, history, metrics, access, freshness, reports, models, and consumer behavior at identical cutoffs.
May both warehouses ingest the stream?
Only with explicit offsets and generation ownership; uncontrolled double ingestion creates divergence.
What if rollback loses new target data?
Preserve target-created state and reconcile forward instead of discarding valid history.
When can legacy retire?
After jobs, consumers, shares, exports, holds, audits, retention, recovery, and lineage duties are proven complete.

Pin every consumer to one declared data generation.

Failure rehearsal

A streaming connector advances different offsets in the two warehouses while one dashboard mixes target metrics with a legacy extract. The team stops the cohort, fences one ingestion generation, identifies each consumer's source, reconciles the offset gap, and reruns parity before resuming.

Questions people actually ask

Are matching totals enough?

No. Errors can cancel; compare keys, fields, cohorts, history, and consumers.

Can metric definitions change during migration?

Freeze or version the change separately so platform parity remains testable.

Should history move in one job?

Use bounded repeatable partitions with manifests and reconciliation.

Can consumers query both sources?

Only through an explicit governed comparison, never an ambiguous production blend.

What proves security parity?

Test effective row, column, tenant, service-account, export, audit, and residency behavior.

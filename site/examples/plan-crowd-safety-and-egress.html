Worked case: decide whether a concert can open

Trigger: An outdoor concert expects eighteen thousand people through two approaches, but ticket arrival assumptions conflict, one exit is narrowed by construction, rain changes ground conditions, the step-free route shares emergency access, transport capacity drops after closing, and leadership still wants full capacity.

Ticketing says most people will arrive evenly, so can we keep the licensed capacity?
Not from that claim alone. We need a crowd profile and interval arrival range reconciled with transport, gate, search, queue, route, room, exit, and dispersal capacity.
The narrowed exit is still technically open.
Its usable width, downstream route, obstruction, surface, lighting, flow direction, emergency access, and qualified acceptance must be verified in the actual operating state.
Could mobility-device users wait in a refuge until others leave?
Only where the qualified plan provides protected refuge, assisted evacuation, communication, accountability, and emergency acceptance. It cannot be an improvised substitute for accessible egress.
What if evidence still does not support eighteen thousand?
Reduce capacity, routing, or scope, or delay opening. Leadership preference cannot replace verified flow, staffing, emergency access, and evacuation evidence.

Licensed maximum is not always the safe operating capacity.

Failure rehearsal

Heavy rain makes the step-free route unstable while an ambulance needs the shared lane and the narrowed exit receives opposing flow. Admission stops, qualified owners activate the reduced-capacity plan, assisted evacuation moves to the verified alternative, and reopening waits for route and staffing acceptance.

Questions people actually ask

Is licensed capacity the operating capacity?

Not necessarily. Actual routes, conditions, staffing, audience, queues, emergency access, and downstream transport can require less.

Can ticket scans prove crowd density?

They show entries but not distribution, queues, movement, unscanned people, or safe flow by themselves.

Who can stop admission?

Qualified primary and alternate authorities named in the plan should act at verified triggers.

What makes egress accessible?

Usable routes, communication, assistance, equipment, accountability, and emergency integration that are tested with representative needs.

Can an exercise be discussion only?

A tabletop helps, but critical route, communication, handoff, and assisted-evacuation assumptions also need executed tests.

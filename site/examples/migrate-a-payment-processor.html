Worked case: migrate payments without a duplicate debit

Trigger: A marketplace must migrate card, wallet, bank-debit, subscription, refund, dispute, token, webhook, payout, reserve, fraud, and reconciliation flows to a new payment processor across countries while old and new transactions overlap and no customer may be charged twice or lose a refund.

Can a timeout retry at the new processor?
Not until the old processor outcome is resolved. Record an indeterminate state and query by stable merchant reference.
Do historical refunds move to the new provider?
Usually no. The processor owning the original transaction keeps its refund and dispute lifecycle.
Can we copy card numbers ourselves?
No. Use approved PCI provider or network-token pathways and obtain fresh authorization where portability fails.
What makes rollback safe?
Stop new routing, fence the failed generation, preserve in-flight ownership, and never replay an ambiguous debit.

Every transaction remains owned by exactly one processor generation.

Failure rehearsal

A checkout request times out after the old processor may have authorized it while routing has switched to the new provider. The platform records an indeterminate outcome, blocks a second authorization under the stable payment intent, queries the old provider, resumes the owning state machine, and reconciles the customer, order, processor, payout, and ledger result.

Questions people actually ask

Can both processors receive live authorizations?

Only for separately assigned intents; never let both mutate the same payment intent.

What if tokens are not portable?

Keep the cohort on the old route or obtain new customer authorization through an approved flow.

How are delayed webhooks handled?

Verify signatures, reject replays, preserve event IDs, and apply explicit state-machine ordering rules.

Where do old disputes go?

Keep them with the processor that owns the original transaction unless transfer is formally supported.

When can the old account close?

After refunds, disputes, payouts, reserves, retention, reporting, and support obligations are proven complete.

I just want to know if it's good

A verdict wouldn't help you, because good is relative to a goal: a loud poster for a school fair is right to be loud. So the skill asks what the thing is for and who will see it, then judges against that, at the size the real audience sees it. What you get is capped at six failures, because a twenty-item critique flattens into noise, and the list comes ranked by impact for effort. The ranking is the judgment, and it is the part of a designer's eye you are borrowing until you grow your own.

What you end up with

A written critique, the top three fixes already live, and the rest saved. Here is the writeup for the portfolio above.

Example
CRITIQUE: PORTFOLIO SITE
Goal: web dev gigs from small businesses · Reviewed at phone size
WHAT WORKS 1
Project titles state what each project does. Page loads fast on mobile. One page total, which suits a busy reader.
WHAT FAILS AND WHY 2
1. Reading order. The photo wins the first glance; the offer line is bottom-right at the smallest size. Cost: an owner leaves without learning what you sell.
2. Consistency. Four fonts across one page. Cost: reads as unfinished, which is fatal for someone selling web work.
3. Contrast. Project titles are #999 on #EEE. Cost: invisible on a phone outdoors.
4. Two different corner radii on cards. 5. Uneven gaps between sections. 6. Decorative divider doing no work.
APPLIED TODAY 3
Fixes 1 to 3, each shown before and after, owner's call on each. The photo stayed; the offer line moved to the top at headline size. Site redeployed.
SAVED FOR NEXT SESSION 4
Items 4 to 6, as notes/critique.md in the repo. Owner's own re-critique afterward: "the gaps are what I'd fix next." That matches item 5.
  1. Strengths are specific. "Titles state what each project does" is repeatable on the next project. Vague praise gets you nothing; invented praise is banned.
  2. Every failure has three parts. Element, principle, cost. That structure is what makes a criticism teachable instead of just accurate.
  3. Talk becomes edits. The top three fixes went live in the same sitting, which is the difference between a critique and an opinion.
  4. The loop closes. The owner re-critiqued in their own words and independently found item 5. That is the eye getting sharper, on record.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Will it just be nice to me?

No. The skill's rule is that praise must be real: two or three specific strengths when they exist, and when little works, one true thing before moving on. It never invents compliments, because you would learn to distrust everything else it says.

My design is intentionally loud. Will it get marked down?

No, because critique happens against your goal. The skill asks what the piece is for and who it is for before judging anything, and it says explicitly that a loud poster for a school fair is right to be loud. Loudness that serves the goal is a strength; loudness that buries the date is a failure.

What if I disagree with a fix?

You try it both ways. The skill applies its version, you keep yours alongside, and you compare the two. Your taste decides, and whichever way it goes, the reason gets stated in one line so the pattern is learnable.

What can I bring to be critiqued?

Anything you actually designed: a site, poster, deck, profile, or logo, as a URL, file, or screenshot. It gets reviewed at the size the audience sees it, including on a phone if it is a web thing.

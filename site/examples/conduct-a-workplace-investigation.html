Worked case: investigate a conflicted senior-leader allegation

Trigger: A senior executive is accused of harassment and retaliation by several employees while the normal HR lead reports to the executive, messages may be deleted within days, one reporter fears immediate scheduling retaliation, accounts conflict, and leadership wants a quick confidential conclusion before a board meeting.

Can the HR lead investigate if legal reviews the final report?
Not without resolving the reporting-line conflict. The mandate needs an independent appointing authority, investigator, alternate, and decision route.
Should we promise everyone complete confidentiality?
No. Explain need-to-know handling and its limits. Absolute promises can conflict with fairness, safety, representation, and required reporting.
The reporter may lose preferred shifts next week.
Use proportionate anti-retaliation and scheduling controls now without treating the allegation as proven, and document who can review any change.
Can we finish before the board meeting?
Only if the evidence, interviews, respondent opportunity, contrary material, and review can be handled fairly. A calendar deadline cannot determine the finding.

Independence and fairness are operating requirements, not report wording.

Failure rehearsal

The investigator learns that the appointing leader discussed desired findings with the accused executive while messages are approaching deletion. Work stops, alternate independent authority preserves the evidence, the conflict and prior access are recorded, interim protections remain proportionate, and the process restarts with disclosure and fairness decisions from qualified owners.

Questions people actually ask

Does interim protection imply guilt?

No. It should reduce immediate harm and evidence risk while remaining proportionate, reviewed, and reversible.

Can anonymous reports be investigated?

Sometimes, but scope, corroboration, fairness, disclosure limits, and the ability to respond must be assessed carefully.

Should every witness see the full allegation?

No. Give enough context for reliable evidence while limiting contamination, privacy loss, and retaliation risk.

Who decides discipline?

The authorized decision-maker under policy and law, usually separate from fact finding unless the mandate says otherwise.

What happens after findings?

Communicate within lawful boundaries, repair systems, preserve records, support affected people, monitor retaliation, and provide review where required.

Questions people actually ask

Will it write my essays or fill in my applications?

No. The skill's own rule is the machine and never the content: it does not write personal statements, supplemental answers, or activity descriptions, and it does not fill in or submit applications. When an essay blocks a checklist item, you draft and it annotates, the same feedback rules as the research paper workshop.

Where does the requirement list come from?

From each school's own admissions page and your actual portal, opened during the session. Nothing is entered from memory or guesswork, and your job during updates is to read the portal's status screens out loud rather than answer from recall.

What about rec letters and transcripts I don't control?

Those rows get an owner column and top priority, because other people's lead time is what slips. Every request goes out in the first session; the email-a-professor skill covers how to make the ask well.

Nine schools feels like hundreds of tasks. Is it?

Fewer than it feels like. Duplicate work collapses: one main essay serving several schools is one row, and shared items like aid forms appear once. Sorted by deadline, you only ever look at the top of the sheet, and the three nearest deadlines live on your real calendar with reminders.

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Worked case: investigate a cross-system failure

Trigger: A consequential failure crossed technical and operational boundaries, evidence is incomplete, and leadership wants one person named as the root cause.

The operator clicked approve, so can we close this as human error?
Not yet. I need the observed outcome, evidence provenance, synchronized chronology, expected controls, actual path, and competing causal hypotheses.
The logs are incomplete, but everyone knows what happened.
Shared belief is not provenance. I will preserve witness accounts separately and keep hypotheses bounded by missing evidence.
Can the action be retraining the operator?
Only if evidence shows a training gap and the action interrupts the causal path. We must also test technical, process, workload, and control conditions.

The last visible action is not automatically the cause.

Failure rehearsal

A favored explanation depends on an application timestamp while the database and message broker use different clocks. Investigators preserve all three records, establish clock offsets, and rebuild the synchronized chronology. They retain two viable hypotheses until a controlled replay distinguishes them and do not name an individual cause.

Questions people actually ask

Is there always one root cause?

No. Consequential outcomes commonly require interacting events, conditions, control gaps, and impact amplifiers.

How should witness accounts be used?

Preserve them with source and timing, then compare them with records without treating memory as a log.

When is a causal hypothesis supported?

When evidence and safe tests match its predictions better than credible alternatives.

Is retraining a valid corrective action?

Only when a demonstrated training gap contributed and the action is tested for effectiveness.

When can the analysis close?

After bounded causes, tested control actions, residual risk acceptance, and later effectiveness evidence are recorded.

Worked case: prove tenant isolation across every surface

Trigger: A SaaS platform replaces inconsistent role checks while enterprise groups sync externally, users belong to several tenants, projects can be shared, support can impersonate, jobs and exports outlive sessions, caches omit tenant identity, and a prior bug exposed search results across customers.

Can we centralize every check?
Centralize semantics, but ensure each enforcement point fails safely and carries tenant plus policy identity.
Should old or new policy win in shadow disagreement?
Never choose the more permissive result automatically; classify and approve the change.
Does session revocation stop exports?
Not alone. Revoke jobs, caches, webhooks, subscriptions, tokens, and stored capabilities.
What proves migration?
Every enforcement surface, denial, revocation path, audit decision, exception, rollback, and support route is tested.

Tenant identity belongs in every decision and cache key.

Failure rehearsal

A revoked administrator retains a queued export and cached search token. The system quarantines both capabilities until separate reauthorization proves tenant and policy state.

Questions people actually ask

Are roles enough?

Often not; resources, relationships, tenant, context, and policy version matter.

Can support impersonate users?

Only through bounded, attributable, authorized, and reviewable controls.

Should ambiguous ownership allow access?

No. Deny and route to authorized resolution.

Can caches omit policy version?

No. Stale grants must not survive policy change.

What about false denials?

Provide safe appeal and support without bypassing isolation.

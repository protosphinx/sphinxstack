Worked case: replace the ledger before year-end

Trigger: A multi-entity company is migrating live general ledger, payables, receivables, payroll, inventory, fixed assets, tax, and multi-currency reporting from inconsistent account charts before year-end, while duplicate suppliers, open historical periods, manual overrides, missing audit attachments, and a looming source-retirement date make a direct cutover unsafe.

Can we import closing balances and switch at year-end?
Not safely. First inventory ledgers, subledgers, controls, history, attachments, roles, integrations, and statutory access.
What should happen to duplicate suppliers?
Preserve source identities, quarantine ambiguous matches, and require an approved mapping rather than merging by name.
The source contract has a hard retirement date.
Escalate the commercial constraint if reconciliation, parallel close, retained history, or rollback evidence is incomplete.
What proves the migration is ready?
Reconciled balances and subledgers, end-to-end control tests, a parallel close, and a timed cutover and rollback rehearsal.

Failure rehearsal

The second rehearsal produces a balanced trial balance, but supplier aging differs because credit notes lost their source-document relationships. The cutover pauses. Transformation rules are corrected, affected records are reloaded idempotently, aging and ledger balances reconcile, and the parallel close is repeated. A balanced ledger alone is not accepted as migration proof.

Questions people actually ask

How much history should move?

Move the detail needed for operations, law, audit, comparison, and support, while keeping approved retained-source access for the remainder.

Can charts of accounts be cleaned during migration?

Yes, through approved mappings that preserve traceability from every source account and historical report.

Is a matching trial balance enough?

No. Subledgers, tax, aging, currencies, controls, workflows, reports, attachments, and integrations must also be verified.

How long should the source remain available?

Until operational, audit, legal, retention, and rollback owners approve retirement based on evidence.

What makes a rollback credible?

A timed rehearsal that restores processing and reconciled balances without losing committed source activity.

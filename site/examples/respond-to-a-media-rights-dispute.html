Worked case: reconcile disputed music, image, and likeness rights

Trigger: A global campaign receives a platform claim and a lawyer's notice alleging that one music recording, an archival image, and a contributor's likeness exceed their licenses, while contracts conflict, localized edits use different durations, paid ads are live, partners downloaded masters, and response deadlines are short.

Should we delete every file immediately?
No. Preserve evidence, stop unsupported new use, and assess each right, asset, version, and destination separately.
Can one platform license cover partner downloads?
Only if its grant explicitly covers the entity, media, territory, term, derivative, and downstream use.
Should we counter the claim before the deadline?
Only after authorized factual and legal review supports that action.
What proves resolution?
Reconciled rights and uses, verified propagation, payments and credits, residual reach, and corrected controls.

Failure rehearsal

A localized ad uses eight seconds more music than its license and has already been downloaded by a partner. Paid promotion pauses, the exact edit is preserved, a licensed replacement is delivered, the partner confirms removal, and residual copies remain recorded instead of being declared gone.

Questions people actually ask

Does owning the video clear the music?

No. Composition, recording, performance, and other rights can remain separate.

Can an unverified claimant receive contracts?

No. Verify identity and authority before exposing private records.

Should all versions be removed?

Only where the disputed right and authorized response require it.

Can credit cure missing permission?

Not unless the actual right and agreement make attribution the remedy.

When is the dispute closed?

After resolution, propagation, obligations, residual reach, and preventive controls reconcile.

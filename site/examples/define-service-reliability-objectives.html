Worked case: define checkout reliability

Trigger: Teams want a ninety-nine-point-nine availability target, but retries, partial checkout failures, low traffic, maintenance, telemetry gaps, and contract language make the convenient metric misleading.

Our API returned a successful status, so can that be the availability indicator?
Not by itself. I will start with the checkout journey and define a valid event, a good outcome, observation point, exclusions, and telemetry limits.
Can we exclude planned maintenance and all third-party failures?
Only through a narrow, pre-approved rule that matches user impact. Dependencies and maintenance can still make checkout unavailable to users.
Then set ninety-nine-point-nine and alert on it.
First I will backtest candidate targets, reconcile incidents and low-volume periods, then rehearse burn alerts and the decisions a breach should trigger.

A convenient component metric is not a user outcome.

Failure rehearsal

The metric reports full availability while a payment dependency rejects half of completed carts and telemetry drops the failed events. The team treats the gap as unknown rather than good, repairs the denominator, reruns the historical backtest, and repeats the alert and breach rehearsal before approving the objective.

Questions people actually ask

What is the difference between an indicator and an objective?

The indicator measures a defined user outcome; the objective sets an approved target and window for that measure.

Can planned maintenance be excluded?

Only through a predeclared and justified rule that does not hide material user harm or conflict with commitments.

How should low traffic be handled?

Document minimum-event behavior and supplement sparse ratios with carefully governed journey checks or longer windows.

What happens when telemetry is missing?

Mark the measure incomplete or unknown according to policy; do not silently count unobserved outcomes as good.

Who approves the target?

Service and risk owners should approve it with the people responsible for user, business, and contractual consequences.

Won't my slides look empty without the text?

They will look confident, which is different. The paragraphs were never for the audience; they were a safety net for you, and they cost you the audience's attention, because people read faster than you talk and then wait for you to catch up. The skill moves the safety net to where it works: presenter notes in your own speaking voice, with the first line of each note being that slide's opener. The slide itself keeps one headline that states its point as a sentence. You stay covered, and the room listens to you instead of reading past you.

What you end up with

A deck you could present from in five minutes, plus the structure that got it there. Here is the slide list for the eight-minute talk above.

EIGHT SLIDES, ONE POINT EACH 2

NOTES FOR SLIDE 4 3
"This is the slide the talk exists for. 12 sites, every one positive. Point at the lowest bar: even the best site had three fibers per liter. Pause here."
CUT FROM THE OLD DECK 4
11 slides, including "History of Plastic" (three slides, no point related to the finding) and every slide whose headline was a label like "Methodology".
  1. The one thing is written first. It shapes every cut and becomes the closing slide, so the talk ends on the point instead of on "questions?".
  2. Headlines are sentences. "Every sample had microplastic fibers" makes the point even for someone who looks up once. "Results" makes no point at all.
  3. Notes carry the speaking. First line is the opener, in the presenter's own voice. The deck and the script stop competing for the same rectangle.
  4. The cuts are listed too. Eleven slides died on the list, where cutting is cheap. Real numbers stayed; the slide whose number didn't exist was cut rather than faked.

Anatomy of a slide that works

The skill builds every content slide to this shape:

Every sample we tested had microplastics · one chart, 12 sites, counts per liter · notes: opener plus two facts to say · nothing else on the slide
  • A The headline states the point as a sentence. A reader who glances up once still gets it.
  • B One chart or image, only when it carries the point. Its takeaway is the headline above it.
  • C Presenter notes hold the sentences you plan to say. They never appear on the slide.
  • D The empty space is deliberate. One point per slide means the next point gets its own slide.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

What tool does it build the deck in?

Whatever you will actually present from: Google Slides or PowerPoint if you have them, otherwise an HTML deck printed to PDF. Either way you end with a PDF export that survives any projector, the editable source in your account or repo, and notes attached to the slides or as a printable one-pager.

I don't have real numbers for a chart. Can it make the chart anyway?

No. The skill's rule is real numbers only: if you don't have the number, the slide gets cut rather than faked. A talk with six honest slides beats one padded to twelve with invented data someone might ask about.

How many slides should I have?

Roughly one per minute of your slot, and fewer is fine. The budget is set on the slide list before styling, so trimming happens where it costs one line instead of one finished slide.

Can it just restyle the deck I already have?

It will restructure first, because that is where most of the improvement is: every slide has to state its point in one line or merge with one that does. After that, the styling is deliberately plain: one font, two sizes, two colors plus an accent, or your brand kit applied exactly if you have one.

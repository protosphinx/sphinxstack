Worked case: move two petabytes without losing meaning

Trigger: A broadcaster must move two petabytes of masters, sources, projects, proxies, captions, transcripts, and rights records from tape and object storage into a media asset system, but filenames collide, checksums are incomplete, metadata schemas conflict, licenses expire, and source retirement is contractually scheduled.

Can we migrate by filename and compare total bytes?
No. Use stable archive identities, source manifests, checksums, metadata relationships, rights, and object-level outcomes.
Should incompatible masters be transcoded?
Preserve masters unchanged. Create documented proxies or access derivatives when the target cannot preview them.
The source contract ends on a fixed date.
A commercial deadline cannot replace fixity, retrieval, rights, legal, and rollback evidence. Escalate the constraint.
What proves a cohort passed?
Checksums, counts, metadata, relationships, rights, access, captions, restore, search, and representative playback reconcile.

Failure rehearsal

A wave has matching byte totals but seven captions are attached to the wrong programs after metadata transformation. The wave pauses, stable IDs and relationship manifests repair the mapping, and all affected programs are revalidated. Total size is never used as a completeness claim.

Questions people actually ask

Are checksums required for every object?

Generate or verify object-level fixity wherever preservation and integrity matter.

Can duplicate files be removed during migration?

Only after identity, relationships, rights, retention, and approved disposal are proven.

What should happen to corrupt sources?

Quarantine them and preserve the original plus every recovery attempt.

How are rights migrated?

Map ownership, territory, channel, duration, restriction, evidence, and expiry to the exact asset.

When can the source be retired?

After fixity, retrieval, legal, user acceptance, and rollback obligations expire.

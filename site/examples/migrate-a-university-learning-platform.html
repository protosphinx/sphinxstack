Worked case: move active learning without losing a learner

Trigger: A university moves active courses, submissions, quizzes, grades, accommodations, videos, discussions, integrations, and archives between platforms across campuses with different calendars while final assessments continue.

Can record counts prove the move?
No. Reconcile every consequential identity, state, permission, attempt, grade, extension, and learner journey.
Can both systems accept submissions?
Only with one recorded authority per assessment and a controlled state transition that rejects stale action.
What if rollback follows new grades?
Preserve target-only work and reconcile it under one official academic-record authority.
What is the launch gate?
Accessible journeys, exact record parity, fenced authority, tested continuity, monitoring, support, and usable rollback.

Each learner record needs one authority.

Failure rehearsal

A student submits after target cutover while a stale legacy integration changes the due date. The epoch fence rejects the stale write, preserves both records, checks the accommodation source, and protects the learner from penalty.

Questions people actually ask

Can grades be compared by total?

No. Reconcile each grade, component, rule, release state, and learner identity.

Should finals move midterm?

Only when continuity, accessibility, authority, support, and rollback gates pass.

What about cross-listed courses?

Test shared content, rosters, groups, submissions, and grade ownership explicitly.

Can legacy access end at launch?

Not until audit, appeals, retention, exports, support, and restore duties are accepted.

Who approves rollback?

The named academic and technical authorities use explicit impact thresholds.

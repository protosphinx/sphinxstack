Worked case: recover an account when its usual channels are compromised

Trigger: A workspace owner reports password-reset messages and an authenticator they did not add while logs show a new location, API token, bulk export, forwarding-rule change, and ownership transfer attempt, but the registered email and phone may both be controlled by the attacker.

Can support send a reset link to the registered email?
No. That channel may be attacker-controlled. Protect the account, preserve evidence, and use the approved independent proof path.
Should we delete the suspicious events to secure the account?
No. Revoke active capability while preserving immutable evidence and a clear timeline.
Can we just change the password?
That leaves sessions, API tokens, remembered devices, recovery links, federation grants, and malicious factors active.
When is control restored?
After independent identity proof, full session and token containment, fresh factors and recovery codes, reversal review, and recurrence monitoring.

Protect the victim without helping the attacker.

Failure rehearsal

The person reporting the takeover cannot use the registered email, and an attacker is actively contacting support from that address. Responders freeze ownership and export actions in a reversible state, preserve both support conversations, revoke active credentials, and move recovery to a previously verified organization administrator with two-person approval. No ownership transfer occurs until proof is complete.

Questions people actually ask

Should the whole account be disabled immediately?

Use the narrowest safe restriction that stops harm while preserving critical access and evidence.

Is a password reset enough?

No. Contain sessions, tokens, factors, devices, recovery links, integrations, and unauthorized changes.

How should identity be proven?

Use the approved risk-based evidence path and independent channels, with separation of duties for privileged accounts.

What evidence should be retained?

Preserve relevant authentication, session, token, recovery, profile, action, notification, and support evidence under policy.

When is the incident complete?

After control is restored, unauthorized effects are reviewed, recurrence is monitored, and control improvements are owned and verified.

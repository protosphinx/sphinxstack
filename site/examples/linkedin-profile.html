Short is fine; false is fatal. The skill's rule is that the resume is the source of truth and the profile matches it date for date, because interviewers compare the two. No inflated titles, no endorsement farming, no buzzword about section.   1. Custom URL claimed. Their name, no digits, and it goes on the resume header so the two documents point at each other.
  2. The headline is searchable, plain English. What they are plus what they are looking for. No pipes full of slogans.
  3. The about section is spoken language. The skill asks what you would tell a stranger at the job itself; the tightened answer becomes these four sentences, approved one by one.
  4. Experience mirrors the resume exactly. Same dates, same facts, same links. Where the two disagreed, the resume won or got corrected, because interviewers compare them.

Anatomy of a headline

The skill drafts the headline to this shape:

CS student at Jefferson High building small web tools that run live — seeking a summer 2027 internship
  • A What you are right now, in words a recruiter's search would use.
  • B What you actually do, backed by the entries below it.
  • C What you are looking for, so the right reader knows to message you.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch, and a basic LinkedIn profile costs nothing. The agent you paste the skill into may or may not be free: Gemini has the widest free tier, and the free plans of ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot can all run it.

Can it log into LinkedIn and do it for me?

No, by rule. It never handles your password and cannot drive your account. You keep the browser, it drafts each field, you paste the text in and read back what the saved section shows. The whole pass fits in one sitting.

I don't have a resume yet. Can I start with the profile?

No. The resume is the source of truth, and writing a profile from scratch invents. Run build-resume first; the profile session then becomes mostly copying, with each field reworded for the format.

Do I need a photo?

A plain, recent photo of your face helps if you have one. No photo beats a bad one, so the skill never pushes you to upload something blurry, cropped from a group shot, or five years old.

Should I add every skill and collect endorsements?

No. The skills list stays limited to what the profile itself evidences, and endorsement farming is against the skill's rules. A recruiter who sees forty endorsed-by-classmates skills discounts all of them.

Worked case: repair a backfill without overwriting valid concurrent data

Trigger: A transaction-pipeline backfill intended to correct six months of history overwrites current partitions, duplicates retries, drops tombstones, misreads old schemas, and propagates changes into revenue dashboards, customer statements, risk features, experiments, and regulatory extracts while normal ingestion continues.

Should we restore the affected partitions from backup?
Only if that does not discard valid concurrent writes; otherwise repair into a new isolated generation.
Can we rerun with fixed code?
First fence the failed generation and prove source versions, keys, ranges, schemas, descendants, and idempotency.
What about published reports?
Preserve prior evidence, identify affected decisions, restate through accountable owners, and link corrections.
What proves recovery?
Source-to-output parity, current-ingestion correctness, downstream reconciliation, bounded canaries, and tested prevention gates.

Repair through a versioned generation when rollback would erase truth.

Failure rehearsal

The source snapshot needed for one month no longer exists. The team does not fabricate exact history, marks affected outputs unavailable or qualified, identifies decisions that relied on them, restores only provable periods, and records the residual gap plus source-retention fix.

Questions people actually ask

Can a table restore undo the incident?

Not if valid concurrent writes or downstream actions occurred after the restore point.

Should bad rows be deleted first?

Preserve evidence and establish authoritative replacements before mutation.

Can corrected dashboards close the issue?

No. Reconcile exports, features, models, reports, statements, alerts, and decisions too.

How are late arrivals handled?

Keep normal ingestion authoritative and merge through stable identity and version rules.

What blocks expansion?

Unexplained differences, missing source truth, non-idempotent writes, unsafe capacity, or unresolved consumers.

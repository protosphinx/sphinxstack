What if nobody shows up?

A club is people, so the skill refuses to let recruiting wait for a poster or a perfect plan. During the session you name three real people who would plausibly come, from the group chat where the interest already lives, and you message each one the pitch with a direct ask before closing. The pitch itself is built to recruit: three sentences about what the club actually does at a meeting, said out loud until it sounds like you. Concrete activity recruits better than any mission statement, and the same pitch works on the sponsor, the members, and the charter form.

Questions people actually ask

What does my school require to make it official?

Usually a faculty sponsor, a member minimum, and a form. The skill finds your school's actual rules with you instead of guessing. Outside school, a library or community-center room usually just takes an ask.

What if the club already exists?

The skill checks and tells you honestly: joining the existing club and leading it later may beat founding a duplicate. You decide with that on the table.

Is "founder and president" really worth much on a resume?

It is one of the strongest lines a young resume can carry, and one of the most faked. This skill makes it true: named members, a scheduled meeting, a charter in progress, all verifiable.

What if only three people come to meeting one?

Then three people came to a real meeting, which is more club than most ideas ever become. The pick was a club you would attend even if you weren't running it, so the meetings hold value at any size while word spreads.

Do I need a website or an Instagram for it?

Not to start. People, a pitch, and a scheduled meeting come first. A club page where members find meeting times is a natural next project, and building it is itself a resume line.

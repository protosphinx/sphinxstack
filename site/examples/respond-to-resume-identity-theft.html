Worked case: contain employment impersonation

Trigger: An employer receives a resume using a professional's name, photo, certifications, portfolio, and references from a lookalike email domain, while fake interviews are collecting identity documents, a career account has a new recovery method, and nobody knows how many employers or candidates were contacted.

Should we contact the victim through the profile email?
Not until it is trusted. Authenticate through an independent safe route.
Can platforms delete the profiles immediately?
Preserve headers, files, URLs, IDs, logs, and targets first when safe, then coordinate containment.
Should we publish the suspect's identity?
No. Warn targets with verified indicators without public accusation or exposing the victim's documents.
What proves recovery?
Secured accounts, corrected records, protected targets, restored credentials and profiles, plus recurrence monitoring.

Protect the real person and every secondary target.

Failure rehearsal

A platform sends recovery to the email controlled by the impersonator. The team stops that route, uses a previously verified issuer and employer channel, preserves the failed attempt, and adds the platform to the exposure and correction record.

Questions people actually ask

Should the victim send identity documents to every platform?

No. Use minimal evidence and only verified secure routes.

What if job seekers paid the impersonator?

Prioritize warnings and direct them to appropriate payment, fraud, and official support channels.

Can search results be corrected instantly?

No. Preserve requests, track indexing, and monitor recurrence while authoritative records are restored.

Should the impersonator be contacted?

Only under an authorized security or legal strategy; do not tip them off or retaliate.

How long should monitoring continue?

For a defined risk-based period covering accounts, domains, applications, credentials, and search.

Worked case: audit a global role without hiding stage-level barriers

Trigger: A company hires one role family in eight countries through referrals, resume screening, a timed test, panel interviews, and a vendor ranking, but disabled candidates report barriers, one location applies an unwritten degree rule, overrides favor referrals, demographic data is missing unevenly, and leaders want one overall hiring-rate comparison to declare the process fair.

Overall hiring rates are similar. Can we close the review?
No. Map each decision and examine stage, role, location, accommodation, override, and subgroup outcomes.
Can the degree rule stay because one local manager prefers it?
Only a current job-related requirement with evidence belongs in selection.
Should we infer missing disability data?
No. Report missingness and use lawful aggregate plus qualitative evidence.
What happens to candidates already harmed?
Assign candidate remedy where possible, repair the barrier, and monitor whether the change works.

Final hires can hide early barriers.

Failure rehearsal

The timed test shows no overall difference, but accommodated candidates receive broken extra-time settings and abandon before scoring. The test pauses for affected candidates, an equivalent path is verified, prior cases are reviewed for remedy, and monitoring separates assignment from completion.

Questions people actually ask

Is equal final representation proof of fairness?

No. Unjustified barriers may occur at earlier decisions.

Can a manager add a preferred credential?

Only when current evidence shows it is necessary or valid for the job.

Should small groups be reported?

Use minimum thresholds and privacy controls to avoid exposing people.

Does correlation prove discrimination?

No. Investigate causes while treating observed harm seriously.

When is the audit complete?

After remediation, candidate follow-through, monitoring, and reopening controls are assigned and verified.

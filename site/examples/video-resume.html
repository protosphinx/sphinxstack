Won't this be cringe?

The cringe version is the "creative" one: jump cuts, background music, a bit. The skill bans all of it. You talk to a camera for a minute about true things, in sentences you actually said out loud during the interview step, and then you pick the take where you sound most like yourself instead of the take with zero fumbles. A small stumble with a recovery reads human. The finished video goes up unlisted, findable by link only, so nobody trips over it except the people you hand it to. The bar the skill holds you to: you would be comfortable if the exact person you want to hire you watched it, because that is who will.

  1. The proof beat carries the video. Specifics with links a screener can check do the work adjectives can't. Rehearsed from beats, since a memorized script reads as recited.
  2. The close is deliberately boring. Name, thanks, done. Every catchphrase that has ever been tried here made it worse.
  3. Unlisted is the right visibility. Findable by link only, off the channel page, and captioned because recruiters watch muted at desks.

Anatomy of the proof beat

The skill builds the middle of the script to this standard:

I built Splitwiser, a bill-splitting app my friend group uses · about thirty people, every weekend · the link is on my resume
  • A The thing, named plainly. No "platform", no "solution".
  • B What it does and who uses it, in one spoken clause.
  • C A real number, said only because it is true.
  • D Where a stranger can verify it. This mirrors the written resume line for line.

Questions people actually ask

Do I need to be good on camera?

No. You need to be yourself on camera, which is a different bar. Full run-throughs, pick the take that sounds most like you, and keep a small stumble if the recovery is natural. Lens at eye height and window light do more than screen presence.

What equipment does this take?

A phone, horizontal, at eye level on a stack of books, an arm and a half away. Face a window in daytime, quiet room, wired earbuds as the mic if the room is iffy. Nothing gets bought.

How many takes is normal?

Take one is a warm-up by definition. Most people land it between takes 3 and 6. Past 8, take a break, because tired takes get stiffer instead of better.

Where does the video live?

YouTube, unlisted: reachable by link, absent from search and from your channel page. The link goes on the resume near your contact line, and you test it from a logged-out browser window before sending anything.

What if my resume is too thin to talk about for a minute?

Then the resume is the first problem, and the build-resume skill handles it, including the honest option of shipping one real project first. Everything said on camera must be true and on the written page.

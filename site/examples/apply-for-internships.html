  1. Fine print read early. Age minimums, enrollment requirements, paid or unpaid, location. A perfect program you cannot attend is a wasted slot, so eligibility gets checked before any essay gets written.
  2. The essay names something specific. A team, a program detail, a product: proof you actually looked, honest about being early, concrete about what you want to learn. Workshopped hard, ghostwritten never.
  3. Recommenders get asked early, with the resume attached. Polite lead time and easy raw material make the letter easy to write.
  4. Missing requirements stay visible. Programs you cannot enter yet go on the list as blocked-until with a date, never fudged.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat.

Is it too late for this summer?

Depends on the month, and the session finds out fast because deadlines get recorded before anything else. If the good windows have closed, the honest plan is smaller local asks for this summer plus a packet ready for the fall openings.

Will it write my essay?

It drafts and workshops with you, and the final words have to be yours, because programs interview people about what they wrote. A ghostwritten essay sets you up to fail the easiest question in the room.

Do unpaid internships count for anything?

They can, and the fine-print check makes paid or unpaid explicit for every program so you decide with the facts. Whether the trade is worth it depends on your situation, and the skill lays that out flatly instead of deciding for you.

What if a program needs a recommendation letter?

The recommender gets asked now, with polite lead time, your resume attached, and the deadline stated. There is a whole skill for making that ask well.

I freeze the second the camera is on

The skill plans for that instead of pretending confidence will show up. The script is bullet beats in words you already said in chat, so there's nothing to memorize and forget. You rehearse the whole thing out loud exactly once, timed, before recording. And the first take is a throwaway by design, which removes most of its power to scare you. Two or three takes, keep the best, trimmed at both ends, done. Editing ambitions are for video two.

What you end up with

A recorded, uploaded video and the beat sheet that made it. The beat sheet looks like this.

Example
bus42 demo  ·  75 seconds
HOOK, WORD FOR WORD 1
"This is the 42 bus, live. The schedule at my stop says it comes at 8:10. It's 8:14 and it's still six blocks away."
Screen: the map, bus marker moving
BEATS 2
Why I built it: the schedule lies, I kept missing it
Screen: photo of the posted schedule
What you see: bus position, arrival guess for your stop
Screen: click a stop, guess appears
The honest part: rush hour guesses run late, working on it
Screen: stay on the map
CLOSE, WORD FOR WORD 3
"It's at busmap.example.dev. Try it with your own stop tomorrow morning."
Screen: the URL, big, then cut
  1. The hook is the thing itself. No greeting, no channel talk. A concrete moment a viewer recognizes, on screen within five seconds, scripted word for word because nerves eat first lines.
  2. Beats are prompts to talk over. One line of what gets said, in words from your own chat explanation, next to what's on screen while you say it. A demo where the screen lags the voice loses the viewer, so shots are planned with the lines.
  3. The close is one action, then stop. No fade-out thanking paragraph. Say where it is, say what to do, end the video.

Questions people actually ask

Is this free?

The skill is free, no account, no catch. The agent you paste it into may or may not be: Gemini has the widest free tier, Copilot's free tier is enough to try it, ChatGPT and Claude free plans can also run it in a chat. Recording uses the phone or screen recorder you already have.

Will the script sound like AI wrote it?

It can't, structurally: the script's raw material is you explaining the thing casually in chat, and the beats reuse your phrasing. The agent shapes and times; it doesn't write lines for you to recite. Any line you stumble on or would never say in conversation gets rewritten by you, out loud, on the spot.

Do I need a good camera or mic?

No. Phone camera or the screen recorder built into your computer. The skill's one production rule is that audio matters more than video: quiet room, mic close. Fancy gear is nowhere on the path for video one.

How long should my first video be?

60 to 90 seconds. At a natural speaking pace of roughly 140 words a minute, that's a shorter script than most people expect. If the rehearsal read runs over, you cut beats rather than talk faster.

Does the session really end with recording?

Yes, or at worst with you pressing record as it ends. A script that never meets a camera taught you nothing about making videos. First take is a throwaway by design, then two or three real takes, keep the best, upload it where it was needed.

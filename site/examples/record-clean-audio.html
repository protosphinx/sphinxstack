Don't I need to buy a microphone?

No, and this is the point of the skill. Most bad recordings are bad rooms: hard walls, running fans, a mic at laptop distance. Fix those three things, which cost nothing, and a phone's bottom mic at a hand-span produces audio most listeners will call professional. The skill runs the whole setup with you: the clap test to pick the room, a duvet over a chair if the room needs deadening, a real level check on your actual first lines, and a cleanup pass in Audacity, which is free on every platform. The session ends with a genuine take of something you need, exported, because a test recording you never use teaches you nothing.

What you end up with

A clean exported take plus the session sheet that made it, so you can repeat the setup alone next time.

SESSION SHEET — TUTORIAL VOICEOVER
Gear: phone, bottom mic  ·  Room: bedroom closet, door closed
SETUP 1
Clap test: hallway rang, closet passed
Noise: AC off, phone on do-not-disturb, window shut
Distance: a hand-span (15–20 cm), talking past the mic, slightly off-axis
Levels: real first lines at performance volume, peaks at −12 to −6 dB
Room tone: 10 seconds of silence recorded in position
CLEANUP IN AUDACITY, IN ORDER 2
1. Noise Reduction from the room-tone profile, about 12 dB, gentle
2. Cut false starts, long gaps, mouth noises; natural pauses stay
3. Loudness Normalization to −16 LUFS
FILES 3
voiceover-take3.wav (for the video edit) · voiceover-take3.mp3 (192 kbps) · Audacity project kept

  3. Two exports on purpose. WAV feeds further editing without quality loss; MP3 goes anywhere directly. The project file means the next fix takes minutes.

Questions people actually ask

Do I really not need any equipment?

Really. A phone or wired earbuds with an inline mic, plus a quiet soft room, produces a usable result. The skill is built around the gear you already own, and it will not suggest purchases.

Why do I hate the sound of my own voice?

You normally hear your voice partly through bone conduction in your own head, which adds depth a recording does not have. The recorded version is the one everyone else has always heard. It fades with exposure, and clean audio makes it fade faster.

What is room tone and why record it?

Ten seconds of "silence" recorded in position. Audacity's Noise Reduction learns the room's background hiss and hum from that clip, then subtracts it from the whole take. Without it, the cleanup pass has nothing to work from.

Can you fix a recording I already made?

Partly. If it contains a stretch of silence, noise reduction can pull out steady background hum. Echo from a hard room is baked in, though, and re-recording in a treated spot is usually faster than fighting it.

What settings do I export with?

WAV for anything you will edit further, MP3 at 192 kbps or higher for anything going straight out, and Loudness Normalization to about −16 LUFS first, a standard spoken-word level that sounds right on phones.

Worked case: select a chief executive under conflicted authority

Trigger: A listed company must replace its chief executive quickly while the board chair favors a longtime associate, one search partner has a referral-fee connection, the incumbent controls internal-candidate information, a pending acquisition makes names market-sensitive, and the board lacks an agreed recusal or interim-succession process.

Can the chair disclose the friendship and still lead every decision?
Not automatically. Classify the conflict and give an independent body power to recuse and control information.
Should we tailor the role criteria to the leading candidate?
No. Approve current role outcomes before substantive candidate review.
Can candidate names circulate to the full board?
Only where the approved process and need-to-know boundary require it.
What if the selected person fails a condition?
Use the rehearsed alternate or interim succession path and preserve the evidence.

Disclosure is not always mitigation.

Failure rehearsal

A preferred candidate's undisclosed related-party relationship emerges after compensation negotiation. Final approval pauses, independent advisers assess the conflict, the committee revisits comparable evidence and terms, and the interim-succession plan protects continuity without suppressing the finding.

Questions people actually ask

Is disclosure enough for every conflict?

No. Some conflicts require recusal, information restriction, independent review, or process redesign.

Who should approve role criteria?

The independently authorized decision body before substantive candidate assessment.

Can a search firm own candidate decisions?

No. It can advise and source, but governing authority remains accountable.

How is dissent recorded?

Preserve the evidence, concern, response, and decision without forcing false consensus.

What protects continuity if appointment fails?

A preapproved interim and alternate succession plan.

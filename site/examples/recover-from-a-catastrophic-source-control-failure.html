Worked case: reconstruct distributed source history

Trigger: An attacker compromises the central Git service, rewrites protected branches and tags, deletes repositories and backups, steals signing and deploy keys, while mirrors replicate damage and teams retain divergent clones plus unpushed work.

Which clone is the source of truth?
None automatically. Preserve all sources and reconcile objects, refs, signatures, artifacts, releases, and provenance.
Can we force-push the majority history?
Not before unique and disputed objects are quarantined and authority is established.
Do old signatures restore trust?
Only as bounded evidence; exposed signing keys require new trust.
What proves restoration?
Supported history, preserved disputes, rotated trust, reproducible critical builds, protected hosting, and independent acceptance.

Every clone is evidence, not automatic authority.

Failure rehearsal

A minority clone contains an unpushed security fix. Recovery preserves and reviews it rather than deleting it through a majority vote.

Questions people actually ask

Can mirrors be trusted?

Only after their replication point and integrity are reconciled.

Should garbage collection run?

No, not before all unique evidence is preserved.

Can releases validate commits?

They are corroborating evidence with their own provenance limits.

What if history stays disputed?

Publish bounded recovery and preserve disputed refs.

When can deploys resume?

After new trust and reproducible critical builds pass.

Where to go from here

Repair a single copy with Recover a corrupted Git repository, protect release paths with Audit a build pipeline, and verify recovery with Verify a backup restore.

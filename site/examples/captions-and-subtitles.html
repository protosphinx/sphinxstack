Can't I just leave the auto-captions on?

Auto-captions get you a draft in minutes, and that is their whole job. They break on the words that carry your video: names, project names, technical terms, anything a dictionary hasn't met. Viewers who know the topic notice instantly, and viewers who depend on captions get a garbled version of your actual point. The skill keeps the fast part, auto-generating with whichever tool sits closest to where your video already lives, and adds the part that matters: a human pass over every line, with the video playing, until the captions say what you said.

Questions people actually ask

What's the fastest way to get a draft?

Whichever tool sits closest to the video: CapCut and DaVinci Resolve auto-caption inside the editor, YouTube auto-captions after upload, and free Whisper-based tools transcribe a standalone file locally.

SRT file or burned-in captions?

Per destination. Sidecar SRT/VTT for YouTube and anywhere that accepts subtitle uploads, since viewers can toggle it and search can index it. Burn-in for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts, where styled captions are part of the video. Doing both costs little.

Can I clean up my grammar in the captions?

No. Captions transcribe what was said. An "um" can be dropped, but rephrasing yourself into someone more polished changes the record, and viewers lip-reading or half-listening notice the mismatch.

How big should captions be, and where on screen?

Two lines max, around 40 characters per line, big enough to read on a phone at arm's length. High contrast, white with a black outline or backing. Above the bottom quarter on vertical video, lower third on horizontal, never over faces or the thing being shown.

Are word-by-word pop animations okay?

Fine for shorts if your editor does them. Accuracy and legibility still rule: an animated caption that is wrong or unreadable is worse than a plain one that works.
